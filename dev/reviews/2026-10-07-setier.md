## Verdict: NOT MERGEABLE

Most of the lane holds up under attack. Tier 3 first fixes the
noise-row platform split, the boundary message has no false alarm in
800 fits, separation is named on every platform, and the nanse attack
set does not regress. Two findings block:

- B1: the curvature probe gives finite, plausible standard errors to
  parameters on a curved ridge that the data do not determine, where

- B2: `separation_check()` turns a sparse design dense on every

## Blocking

### B1. The probe "rescues" a straight line off a curved ridge

`se_curvature_real()` keeps a direction when both steps lose at least
`grad_tol`. It has no upper bound and no check that the loss is
quadratic. A straight step along the tangent of a curved ridge leaves
the ridge and loses likelihood as the FOURTH power of the step. At the
step the probe takes, that loss easily exceeds `grad_tol`.

Repro: `dev/setier-rev-c0k.R lane|base|merge` and
`dev/setier-rev-probefix2.R`. The model is lane nanse's
`c0 + exp(a)^k`, `a ~ 1 + (1 | g)`, seed 77. The Laplace likelihood is
invariant to `(a0, k, b) -> (s a0, k / s, s b)`. Moving along that
ridge with s from 0.5 to 2 changes the objective by at most 5.2e-11.
So `a_(Intercept)`, `k_(Intercept)` and `theta_1` are not identified.

    arm            SE a_(Intercept)  SE k   SE theta_1  lost
    base (r6)      NaN               NaN    NaN         c0, a, k, theta_1
    lane ref       9.655             NaN    0.3677      c0, k
    lane OpenBLAS  9.657             NaN    0.3664      c0, k
    trial merge    9.655             NaN    0.3677      c0, k

`theta_1` 0.37 at an estimate of -1.03 looks like an ordinary standard
error, and no warning names it. The probed direction has eigenvalue
1.29e-4, below its own noise. It has |cos| 0.972 with the exact ridge
tangent. Its loss against the step multiple shows the fourth power:

    step x 0.25: 2.4e-5   x 0.5: 3.6e-4   x 1: 5.6e-3   x 2: 8.7e-2
    (a quadratic would give a ratio of 4 per doubling; this gives 15.5)

The NEWS bullet says the check "keeps it when the likelihood falls as
its curvature says". The code tests only "falls by at least
grad_tol". A band on the loss (grad_tol to 8 * grad_tol) does not
catch this case, because the loss at step 1 is 5.6e-3.

Fix, demonstrated in `dev/setier-rev-probefix.R` (patched into the
namespace for that process only): also evaluate at half the step, and
keep the direction only when each side's loss ratio is in (3, 5.5)
and the full step loses at least grad_tol. Results:

- c0k seed 77 loses c0, a, k and theta_1, as base does;
- degree-5 and degree-6 raw polynomials, with and without `(1 | g)`,
  still keep every standard error: max |SE/ref - 1| is 5.7e-7, 9.2e-6,
  2.8e-4 and 4.1e-3 against lm() and lmer(), the same as the lane.

The cost is two more objective evaluations per probed direction. Add a
test with c0k seed 77 that asserts NaN for `a_(Intercept)` and
`theta_1`. It must be seen to fail on this build.

Incidence. Over c0k seeds 1 to 20 (`dev/setier-rev-c0k-sweep.R`) and
seeded versions of fixes' `a + exp(b)` and `a * b` rows
(`dev/setier-rev-aexpb.R`, 60 fits), lane and base agree fit by fit.
Base already gives silent finite standard errors on many of those
fits; see m8. The regression is the probe path: it occurs where base
reached tier 3, which seed 77 does.

### B2. separation_check() densifies a sparse design on every binomial fit

`sep_certificate()` calls `as.matrix(lp$X)` and then makes several
dense copies (`sweep`, the `use` and `hold` subsets) before an SVD
that also computes V. It runs at the end of every
bernoulli/binomial/beta_binomial fit, and in every refit that goes
through `fit_assembled()`. Measured with `dev/setier-rev-sepcost.R`
(the check alone, minimum of 3; gc max memory):

    design (n = 1e5)                  base fit         separation_check
    dense, 19 covariates              13.3 s, 287 MB   0.50 s, 336 MB
    sparse_x = TRUE, 500-level f + x  95.9 s, 297 MB   54.3 s, 2297 MB

The dense case is acceptable at 2 to 4 percent. The sparse case adds
about 2 GB, and the memory scales as n x p dense: for 1e6 rows and
2000 levels that is about 16 GB on a fit the user made sparse in order
to run at all. The lane measured only n = 50.

Fix: keep X sparse. The null space of the holding rows can come from
the p x p `crossprod()` of those rows (eigenvectors at about zero),
which a sparse X gives cheaply. The certificate is then checked
exactly with sparse `X %*% d`, so the squared conditioning does no
harm. Skipping the check above a size is an acceptable alternative.
Record the cost on a large dense and a large sparse design.

## Minor (m1 to m3 should go in the same pass; the rest may follow)

m1. **Boundary coverage depends on n.** The empty-row floor
(`se_empty_row = 1e-6`) is absolute. A log sd at its boundary has a
real row of order sd^2 times the score, and that row rises above the
floor as n grows. The lane's own data (`dev/setier-singular.R`,
reproduced fit for fit on the final build, identical on both BLAS
builds): of the 84 lme4-singular fits not flagged, 69 are rs20 code-7
fits (item 8). The other 15 keep theta standard errors from 3502 to
8173, with no message.

`dev/setier-rev-miss.R` looks at ri20 seed 30: theta_1 is -9.29, its
row maximum is 1.47e-6 (just above the floor), its noise is 1.4e-11,
and `se_at_edge()` returns TRUE. Moving theta_1 by -2 or -5 LOWERS the
nll by 1.1e-8, so the estimate is short of a boundary maximum, not an
interior point.

On larger designs (`dev/setier-rev-smallsd.R`, 40 seeds each,
`dev/setier-rev-smallsd2.R`) every lme4-singular single-sd fit is
missed:

- s1 (100 x 10): 0 of 8;
- s2 (300 x 20): 0 of 2;
- poisson p1: 0 of 7.

For example, s1 seed 10 has a row of 4.99e-6, theta SE 1515,
at_edge TRUE, and the same logLik as lme4.

False alarms in those 200 fits: 0. That includes identified sds down
to 0.019 and correlations of 0.95 and 0.99.

Fix: ask `se_at_edge()` of every log-sd theta (one objective
evaluation each) before tier 1 is accepted, and treat a TRUE as an
empty row. Failing that, the NEWS and the findings must state the
coverage. "Fires on 185 of 269" holds only for these small designs.

m2. **A flat smoothing sd is called a boundary without a probe.**
`se_boundary_names()` makes every flat hyperparameter of a smooth, gp()
or hsgp() block "boundary" when all of them are flat
(`length(cand) == length(ti)`). An s() block has one hyperparameter,
so this rule decides it without asking `se_at_edge()`.

`dev/setier-rev-smooth2.R`, `y ~ s(x) + s(x2)` with `x2 = x`, seed 1:
both sds are at log 0.42, and moving either by -2 costs 0.99 nll. Even
so, the lane labels both "boundary", drops them from the warning, and
summary() explains them as "a variance at zero" (base: "flat", in the
warning). Fix: probe single-hyperparameter blocks, and keep the
all-flat shortcut only for blocks with two or more hyperparameters,
with a joint move toward the limit.
