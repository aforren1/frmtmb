`dev/eamhierrev-sv.R`, seed 20260935, 12,000 trials, one subject.

Monotone to the floor. No grid point is above the free fit. Holding

**But it is not the filed seam's third instance.** The Core seams
entry (plan line 213) is about a BOUNDED link where "the derivative
vanishes at the edge, so the standard error COLLAPSES and the linear
predictor saturates below 10", measured at 11.20 with se 2.37 and at
7.17. Here the evidence pair fails the other way round: `abs(est)` is
7.7306 and fails the magnitude half, while se is 589.25 and PASSES the
se half. The remedy that entry proposes, "know whether a link is
bounded and, if it is, test proximity to the bound", would not catch a
log link running to zero: `sv`'s link has no bound to be near. Call it
a fourth failure mode of the same check, needing its own remedy, or
the filed fix will ship and this case will still pass silently.

**Would the existing threshold fire on a log link at all?** Yes. The
pair reads the coefficient on whatever scale the link puts it on and
knows nothing about the link's class, so an `sv` that had reached
exp(-15) rather than exp(-7.73) would have fired both halves. The
check's own docstring already carries a log-link case that it CATCHES,
`negbinomial()`'s shape near 18 with a non-finite standard error. So
what fails here is the CALIBRATION of the magnitude threshold against
a parameter whose useful range is narrow, not the link. On this design
the collapse is well inside 10: `sv` ran to 4.4e-04, and arm C's
sixty hierarchical fits stay between 0.152 and 0.574 with a largest
se(log `sv`) only 5.0 times the median, so nothing there is near the
edge, which is why the hierarchical arm shows 0 of 60.

Two smaller things:

- "the first on a log link" should read "the first MISS on a log
  link", for the reason above.
- The findings' remedy sentence, "the reported standard error was 2789
  times the median one over the same 60 replicates", is a
  cross-replicate statistic that a user with one fit cannot compute.
  The within-fit signature is available and much larger: in this one
  fit se(log `sv`) is 589.25 against 0.050094, the largest of the
  other four, a ratio of **11,763**. `man/wiener.Rd` says the right
  thing ("a standard error orders of magnitude larger than the
  others"); `dev/eamhier-findings.md` should say it too.
- "1 of 60 ... so a user meets it about twice in a hundred fits" rests
  on one event. The Wilson interval on 1/60 is 0.3 to 8.9 percent.
  This document puts a Wilson interval on every other rate.

## 2. The retraction: NOT FALSIFIED, and its stated reason is wrong

`dev/eamhierrev-records.R` re-reads all 240 replicate records with its
own parser, never the lane's summary scripts.

Arm C at 60, recomputed: `mu` condition 57/60, se/sd 0.984; `log sv`
57/60, se/sd 0.913. Both match. The first 14 seeds, 20260910 to
20260923, on the same statistic: `mu` condition se/sd **0.845**,
`log sv` se/sd **0.694**. Exactly the retracted numbers, to three
digits, on two statistics at once. Ordering the records by write time
gives the same 14. So the difference between the two reads is the
count and nothing else: same design, same estimator, same fits, a
contiguous prefix of the same records. **The retraction is what it
claims to be.** The prefix also reproduces across the two RUNS, since
the 0.694 was first computed on records that no longer exist.

The growing prefix, for the record:

```
  n=10 0.707   n=14 0.694   n=20 0.803   n=40 0.851   n=60 0.913
```

**What is wrong is the reason given.** The findings say "a ratio of two
spreads estimated from 14 replicates is itself noisy, and treating it
as usable sooner than a coverage count was a mistake". Neither half
holds:

- 0.694 is not ordinary noise. Over 20,000 random 14-subsets of the
  same 60 records the statistic has mean 0.966, sd 0.161 and a 2.5 to
  97.5 percent range of **0.742 to 1.368**, which does not contain
  0.694; P(a random 14-subset is this low) = **0.003**. The first 14
  seeds were an unlucky prefix, not a noisy statistic behaving
  normally. Say that instead.
- A coverage count would NOT have been the safer instrument. At those
  same 14, `log sv` covers 11 of 14 and its Wilson interval is
  (52.4, 92.4), which EXCLUDES 95: the lane's own decision rule fired
  at 14 and stopped firing at 60. The lesson available here is that 14
  replicates is too few for either statistic, not that one of them was
  the wrong choice.

## 3. The 1.0000 ratio: an identity, and a printed zero

**It is an identity, and it should stop being presented as a
measurement.** Under `ndt_group()`, `ndt_time()` returns
`plogis(b0 + u_i) * floor_i`, and `ndt_frac` is
`predict(re.form = NA)`, which is `plogis(b0)`. Arm B's truth is
`sd(ndt) = 0`, and the fitted variance component duly collapses (mean
3.801e-05, under 1e-4 on 27 of 30). With `u_i` at zero the numerator
`sd(hat)` IS `plogis(b0) * sd(floor)` and the denominator IS
`ndt_frac * sd(floor)`: the same expression on both sides of the
division. The two sides share the floors AND the fraction.

The printed zero is a rounding artifact, exactly the hazard the
standing rules name. Measured at full precision from the records:

```
  ratio  mean 0.9999999419   sd 1.355e-07   min 0.9999993784   max 1
  correlation of (ratio - 1) with the fitted ndt component: -0.9355
```

The residual is the collapse not being exactly zero, which the
correlation confirms. So the sentence to write is "the reported spread
is the floors multiplied by the fitted fraction BY CONSTRUCTION once
the variance component collapses, and the component does collapse:
mean 3.8e-05 against a truth of 0". That is one claim with one piece
of evidence, rather than a ratio of 1.0000 offered as a second
confirmation. The rest of arm B is unaffected: the paired differences
reproduce (population `ndt` bias +0.2376 +- 0.0663 ms, reported
`sd(ndt)` +6.6667 +- 0.2387 ms, logLik +0.4033 +- 0.4029, per-group
better on 17 of 30).

## 6. Power: FALSIFIED

`dev/eamhierrev-power.R`. Both numbers come from the TWO-SAMPLE
two-proportion formulas, reproduced to the digit:

| formula | power at n = 60 | n for 80 percent power |
|---|---|---|
| two independent proportions | **0.1800** | **431.7** |
| the lane's figures | 0.178 | 435 |
| one sample against a KNOWN 0.95 | 0.4471 | 184.8 |

A coverage count is compared with a nominal rate that is known
exactly, not estimated from a second sample of 60. Under the rule this
study actually applies, reject when the Wilson interval excludes 0.95,
the rejection region at n = 60 is X <= 53 and the exact binomial power
is:

```
  truth 0.93  0.1250      truth 0.90  0.3935      truth 0.85  0.8152
  truth 0.92  0.2020      truth 0.88  0.5908      truth 0.80  0.9692
```

with a size of 0.0297 at 0.95. Eighty percent power arrives at n = 205
(exact, same rule), not 435.

A second error rides on top. "The largest count whose Wilson interval
EXCLUDES 95 is 53 of 60, that is 88.3 percent. So this study can
detect a shortfall of about seven points" reads the BOUNDARY of the
rejection region as the detectable effect. The boundary is where power
is about one half. The sentence should read: this study can detect a
ten-point shortfall (power 0.82 at a true 0.85) and cannot detect
seven (0.59).

**Which way it cuts.** Both corrections leave the conclusions standing
and one of them strengthens them: the real power against a true 90
percent is 0.39, not 0.18, so "no Wilson interval excludes 95" is
somewhat better evidence for the null than the document claims. What
must not survive is the number 435: it would send the next round to
buy 2.1 times the replicates it needs, and this lane's own cost table
prices that at about five extra hours.

The claimed bias, on the same standard: **ordinary, and the lane's
reading holds.** The maximum-likelihood shrinkage of a variance
component at q = 30 groups, with the Jensen term for reporting a
standard deviation, is `sqrt(1 - 1/30) * (1 - 1/116) = 0.97472`, that
is 2.53 percent low. Against that expectation rather than against the
truth:

| component | mean | mcse | z vs truth | z vs ML expectation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `sd(mu \| s)` | 0.34743 | 0.00591 | -0.44 | +1.06 |
| `sd(log bs \| s)` | 0.18978 | 0.00371 | -2.75 | -1.39 |

One common shrinkage covers both components and nothing here separates
them. So "the one real bias is the boundary variance component" at
z = -2.7 is the wrong frame: it is the expected shrinkage, seen
clearly in the component whose Monte Carlo error is small. The lane's
sentence "that is the ordinary downward bias of a maximum-likelihood
variance component" is correct; it should carry this arithmetic rather
than assert it.

### The lane is right about n, and this review was wrong

**Power is not monotone in n, and 205 was the wrong number to state.**
The rejection region moves in WHOLE COUNTS while n grows, so each time
the largest rejecting count fails to advance, the size drops and power
drops with it. Scanned over n = 20 to 320 rather than sampled on a
grid:

```
  n=179  X<=164  power 0.7990  size 0.0359
  n=180  X<=165  power 0.8057  size 0.0374   first >= 0.80
  n=184  X<=169  power 0.8307  size 0.0439
  n=185  X<=169  power 0.7643  size 0.0240   region did not advance
  n=199  X<=183  power 0.8518  size 0.0427
  n=200  X<=183  power 0.7925  size 0.0238   again
  n=201  X<=184  power 0.7990  size 0.0248   last dip below 0.80
  n=202  X<=185  power 0.8053  size 0.0259   sustained from here
```

Both of the lane's numbers reproduce exactly: **180** where power
first reaches 0.80 and **202** from where it stays there. The only n
in 20 to 320 that reach 0.80 and are followed by an n that does not
are 184 and 199. This review's 205 is inside the sustained region
(power 0.8233) and so was correct as a sustained threshold and
incomplete as a statement, because it hid the sawtooth and was not the
smallest such n. The lane's decision to report both is the right one.

Worth one more sentence than the lane gives it, because the sawtooth
is paid for in SIZE: n = 180 reaches 0.80 at a size of 0.0374, while
