## 2. The integration-rule story: could not falsify

This is the lane's most consequential substantive claim and it survived
four separate attacks.

### 2a. Is `logLik()` the Laplace approximation of that same integral?

`frailtyrev-ll.R`, seed 20260910, 2000 rows, 40 centres, `df = 2`. The
per-cluster Laplace approximation was written out here in plain R,
`g(bhat) + 0.5 log(2 pi) - 0.5 log(-g''(bhat))` summed over clusters,
from the same hand-written basis the exact integral uses:

    hand-written per-cluster Laplace  = -2645.4095772355
    frmtmb logLik()                   = -2645.4095765303
    difference                        = -7.05e-07

and with the ANALYTIC curvature rather than a finite difference
(`frailtyrev-flip.R`) the residual falls to **1.38e-08** at 500
clusters and 2.43e-07 at 200. So `logLik()` is the Laplace
approximation of exactly the integral the reference integrates, on the
same scale, with no constant dropped on either side. A dropped constant
common to two of the three could not survive this, because the third
value is built from the first two components.

### 2b. Is the exact reference exact enough to resolve 4.9e-06?

Yes, by three orders of magnitude, and it was checked two ways.

- `integrate()`'s own `abs.error`: worst per-cluster
  `abs.error / value` **5.26e-13**, summed over the 40 clusters
  **1.43e-11**, which is what bounds the error of the log-scale sum.
- A structurally different rule with no window at all: 120-node
  adaptive Gauss-Hermite with nodes built here by Golub-Welsch. The two
  agree to **0.000e+00** at ten decimal places.

At 4 subjects per centre, where the integrand is least Gaussian, FOUR
rules were compared: the lane's window `bhat +/- 12 sdl`, a 4x wider
window, `integrate(-Inf, Inf)`, and 200-node adaptive Gauss-Hermite.
Widest disagreement **4.09e-12** (`frailtyrev-flip.R`, seed 20260910).

That last check was aimed at the one artifact that would manufacture
the sign flip. The lane sets its integration window from the LAPLACE
standard deviation, so if the true conditional had fatter tails than
the Laplace normal, the window would throw mass away, bias the "exact"
value DOWN and make the Laplace value look artificially HIGH. It does
not: `-Inf, Inf` and the windowed rules agree to 4e-12.

### 2c. The signs, and the sweep

`frailtyrev-sweep2.R`, seeds 20260910 to 20260914, five per cell, the
lane's own design (2000 rows, `df = 2`, `sd_b = 0.5`), frmtmb only. The
lane's cluster-size column reproduces exactly:

| centres | per centre | lane's `Laplace minus exact` | mine | mine, range |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | 200 | -0.0066 | **-0.006575** | -0.00685 to -0.00641 |
| 40 | 50 | -0.0666 | **-0.066583** | -0.07238 to -0.06127 |
| 200 | 10 | -0.0875 | **-0.087541** | -0.19229 to -0.01047 |
| 500 | 4 | +1.38 | **+1.384564** | +1.33745 to +1.41089 |

The sign flip is real, it is 5 of 5 positive at 500 centres, and it is
not a harness artifact. The mean is not a bound in either direction.

One thing the findings do not say and a reader should be told: **the
200-centre cell has a range of a factor of 18**, -0.010 to -0.192,
because that cell straddles the sign change and per-cluster errors of
both signs partly cancel. The findings quote its mean with no range
where the other tables carry one. NIT.

### 2d. "frmtmb's point is the worse one on 200 of 200"

`frailtyrev-optsign.R`, seeds 20260910 to 20260915, the recovery design
(`df = 3`, 40 centres of 50), 6 of 6 in the same direction, magnitude
4.13e-05 to 5.26e-05 against the lane's mean 5.53e-05 and worst
7.7e-05. The agreement columns reproduce on the same six: `beta` 1.6e-04
to 4.7e-04 of a standard error (lane: mean 3.2e-04, worst 8.8e-04),
`sd` 1.1e-03 to 1.4e-03 relative (lane: 1.4e-03 and 2.0e-03), rstpm2's
`sd` higher on 6 of 6, basis-map residual 1.4e-15 to 7.1e-15 (lane's
worst 2.96e-14).

I could not falsify it, but the 200 of 200 deserves a sentence it does
not have. rstpm2's rule is accurate to 4.9e-06, so rstpm2's estimate
sits essentially AT the exact maximizer; any displaced point must then
score lower under the exact criterion. The unanimity is structural, not
200 independent confirmations. The number that carries information is
the MAGNITUDE, 5.5e-05, and the lane does report it and does put it in
proportion. NIT, one sentence.

### 2e. The instrument the whole sweep rests on

`frailty-sweep.R` and `frailty-ident.R` evaluate the Laplace objective
at a named parameter point through
`setNames(c(gam[1], beta, gam[-1], log(sd)), names(dry$obj$par))`.
`setNames()` relabels and does not reorder, so that line asserts an
ordering of the objective's parameter vector which nothing in the lane's
scripts checks. If it were wrong, every offset in the findings would be
scored at the wrong point. It is right, and the check is one line:

    names(dry$obj$par) = beta, beta, betad, betad, theta
    logLik(ff)                        = -2645.4095765303
    -obj$fn(par_of(the estimates))    = -2645.4095765303   (diff 1.4e-11)

Verified at 40, 200 and 500 centres. NIT: the scripts should carry that
assertion rather than the assumption, since the parameter names are
duplicated (`beta`, `beta`, `betad`, `betad`) and give no protection.

## 3. The `t = 1` anchor: could not falsify

`frailtyrev-units.R`, seed 20260910, the lane's design (2000 rows, 40
centres), one dataset read at 1, 12 and 365.25.

A rescale comparison is only as good as its Jacobian, so the Jacobian
gets a control first: a model with NO random effect is the same model
in any time unit, so `logLik + n_event log c` must be flat for it.

| arm | spread over the three units | `sd(gamma1 \| centre)` |
|---|---|---|
| **CONTROL**, `~ trt`, no block | **2.18e-09** | none |
| **CONTROL**, `gamma1 ~ centre`, FIXED | **11.453** | none |
| `gamma1 ~ (1 \| centre)` | **8.1913** | 0.220079, 0.0755972, 0.0370124 |
| `(1 \| c \| centre)` on `mu` and `gamma1` | **4.2636e-08** | 0.225898, 0.225900, 0.225898 |

The control is flat to 2.2e-09, so the `+ n_event log c` correction is
right. The lane's two numbers reproduce to every digit it printed:
8.191 and 4.264e-08, and 0.2201 falling to 0.0370. The paired block's
intercept standard deviation moves 0.150898 to 1.380624 (lane: 0.151 to
1.381), which is the `-u log c` the algebra predicts, and the implied
correlation between the two blocks, -0.264, closes the variance
identity.

`VarCorr()`'s row names are `time.mu:(Intercept)` then
`time.gamma1:(Intercept)`, so `frailty-gamma1-units2.R`'s
`s[[length(s)]]` does read the slope and not the intercept. That was
worth checking, since the whole table would invert if it did not.

### 3a. The anchor is not a random-effect property

`gamma1 ~ centre` with FIXED per-centre slopes and a common intercept
moves **11.453** log likelihood units over the same rescale. The
anchoring has nothing to do with the block being random; it is a
consequence of letting the slope vary while holding the intercept
common, and a user who writes the fixed-effect spelling gets the same
trap with no help page pointing at it.

This CONFIRMS the lane's decision not to add a refusal (a check would
belong in core, where a random slope without a random intercept lives),
and it argues for one clause in the help page: the anchor follows from
a varying slope with a shared intercept, however that is spelled. NIT.

### 3b. Does the documentation say it where a user would look?

Yes. `?royston_parmar` gains "A frailty, and a random effect on gamma1"
with "Pair the block unless you mean the anchor" as its last sentence,
and `vignettes/royston-parmar.Rmd` cross-references that section from
the paragraph where a reader first meets `gamma1`. The `|ID|` compat
row carries the same statement for a reader who arrives through
`frm_compat()`. That is three of the three places a user would look.

### 4a. Does `test-frailty.R` assert a coverage the count cannot resolve?

No. The only coverage-shaped assertion is

### 5b. The barrier arithmetic checks out

`sp_floor_pos(u, eps2 = 1e-14)` returns `sqrt(eps2) / 2 = 5e-08` at
`u = 0`, `0.5 eps2 / (|u| + sqrt(u^2 + eps2))` for negative `u`. Hand
arithmetic: `log(5e-08) = -16.81`, `log(2.5e-13) = -29.02` at
`u = -0.01`, `log(1.25e-14) = -32.01` at `u = -0.2`. All three of the
lane's barrier figures are exact.

### 5c. BLOCKER: the floor is reachable, and `rp_floored()` reports 0

The lane says the floor is hard to reach because at `df = 1` the log
density carries `log(gamma1 + u)` and a centre with any event pays a
barrier that grows without bound. That part is right. It then writes:

> the residual risk is a centre with NO events, whose deviation is
> unidentified and shrinks toward zero anyway

That is false, and it fails in the direction that matters. For a centre
with no events and all rows censored at times above 1, the score in `u`
is `-sum(x_i H_i) < 0`, so the fit PUSHES that centre's slope negative
and only the normal prior stops it. There is no `log(gamma1 + u)` term
for that centre, because it has no event rows, so there is no barrier.

**The construction** (`frailtyrev-guards.R` and `frailtyrev-silent.R`,
seeds 20260910 to 20260915). The lane's own floor design (400 subjects,
40 centres of 10, population shape 0.6, centre `sd` 0.35) with five
centres followed to the same administrative time and no deaths in any
of them, which is an ordinary thing for a small centre in a
multi-centre trial:

| 20260911 | 0.6848 | **-0.2583** | 1,2,3,4,5 | **0** | **FALSE** |

**The mechanism** is one line, `R/rp-check.R`:

    mono_rows <- which(f$cens == 0 & f$detadx <= 0)

Only UNCENSORED rows are tested for monotonicity, and these centres
have none. That line is PRE-EXISTING; the lane did not write it and did
not change `R/rp-check.R`. Judged against `rp_floored()`'s own charter,
"this fit's reported likelihood is not the model's", the line is even
defensible: `sp_floor_pos()` sits in the density and never touches a
censored row, so nothing was floored and the reported likelihood IS the

**What the lane owns is the sentence it added to `?royston_parmar`:**

> First, `gamma_1 + u` has to stay positive, or that centre's spline
> turns over and there is no hazard there. The link is the identity and
> does not hold it, but the LIKELIHOOD does [...] `rp_floored()` is

"The LIKELIHOOD does" is false for a centre with no events, and
"`rp_floored()` is still what reports it" is false for the same centre.
The lane is shipping `gamma1 ~ (1 | centre)` as a supported, documented

**The fix, and it is small.** Either

- say in `?royston_parmar` and in the findings that `rp_floored()`
  tests the OBSERVED EVENT rows, so a centre with no events can carry a
  negative slope unreported, and say what to look at instead
  (`ranef()` plus the population `gamma1`, which is two lines); or
- widen the check. `mono_rows <- which(f$detadx <= 0)` would report
  every observed row whose fitted spline is decreasing whatever its
  censoring status. That is a behaviour change to a refusal and belongs
  to whoever owns `rp_floored()`, not to a documentation row; the

The FIRST is the blocker and it is a paragraph. The second is a filed
defect for a later round.

`dev/frailty-findings.md` also needs the "shrinks toward zero anyway"
clause struck, since it is the claim that was falsified, and

NOT evidence that code is unreachable. Prove reachability or

### NIT (new): the remedy is a `df = 1` check and does not say so

At `df = 1`, `d(eta)/d(log t)` IS `gamma1 + u`, so the two lines test
the right quantity exactly. Above `df = 1` they do not:
`d(eta)/dx = gamma1 + u + sum_j gamma_{j+1} v'_j(x)`, and the remedy
drops the interior terms. Measured on a real `df = 3` fit with
`gamma1 ~ (1 | centre)` (`frailtyrev2-remedydf.R`, seed 20260910, 800
rows, 20 centres):

| quantity | min | max |
|---|---|---|
| the remedy's `gamma1 + u` | 1.207 | 2.1618 |
| what `rp_floored()` tests, `d(eta)/d(log t)` at the rows | 0.76782 | 2.0174 |

Per centre, `min(detadx) / (gamma1 + u)` runs **0.636 to 0.796**, so the
remedy overstates the margin by 20 to 36 percent, and it errs
OPTIMISTIC, which is the dangerous direction. Signs agree on this fit,
but a centre at `gamma1 + u = 0.1` could carry `min detadx` below zero
and the two lines would report it clean.

The surrounding paragraph is explicitly about `df = 1` ("At `df = 1` it
is a per-centre Weibull shape"), so a careful reader has the context,
and for a group WITH events at any `df` the shipped `rp_floored()`
already answers it. The exposed set is a group with no events at
`df >= 2`. The fix is one clause: say the two lines are the `df = 1`
form, and that above it the derivative carries the interior
coefficients too. NIT, not a blocker, and not worth a round on its own.

### NIT (new): the paired spelling makes "that column" ambiguous

The same help page says "Pair the block unless you mean the anchor".
Under the paired spelling `ranef(fit)[["centre"]]` has TWO columns,
`time.mu:(Intercept)` and `time.gamma1:(Intercept)`, and the remedy

**That case cannot occur under the default knot rule, and the reason

the last event, and the narrow check already covers that region.
