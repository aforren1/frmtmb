## Findings, by severity

### F1. HIGH. `imp_stall_tol`'s recorded margin is 25 times too big

`R/importance.R:1082-1110`, `dev/debts-findings.md:98-126`, `NEWS.md`.

The roxygen says the two regimes are "four orders of magnitude apart"
and that `1e-2` "sits with a factor of 39 over the widest stalled
spread and a factor of 115 under the narrowest converging one". The
findings file says "more than three orders of magnitude", 39 and 32.
The calibration behind those numbers is 23 fits, all Bernoulli or
gaussian, at two group counts.

The two documents also disagree with each other. The roxygen's 115 is
`1.15 / 1e-2`, the narrowest CONVERGING fit; the findings file's 32 is
`0.322 / 1e-2`, the narrowest still-moving one, which is the wandering
20-group fit the findings table lists and the roxygen leaves out. And
2.6e-04 against 1.15 is 3.6 orders of magnitude, not four. Neither
number survives the wider measurement below, but they should at least
have been the same number.

I widened it: three families (bernoulli, poisson, **gaussian**, and
poisson is one the lane did not use), four data seeds, group counts 8,
12 and 20, draw counts 24, 50 and 100, importance seeds 1 and 7. 216
cells, of which **184 are capped**. Statistic as shipped,
`(max - min) / mean` of `fit$importance$moves`:

| quantity | lane's claim | measured on 184 fits |
|---|---|---|
| widest stalled spread | 2.6e-04 | **6.51e-03** |
| narrowest still-moving spread | 0.322 | **2.10e-02** |
| tol / widest stalled | 39 | **1.54** |
| narrowest moving / tol | 32 | **2.10** |
| separation of the regimes | ">3 orders" / "4 orders" | **a factor of 3.2** |

The gap the threshold sits in is a factor of 3.2 wide, not a thousand.
It is still a gap, and `1e-2` is still inside it, but the margin is one
poisson dataset from closing, not 39 times anything.

**And the classifier misses the fits that matter most.** Six of the
184 have a Laplace `sd(alpha)` below 1e-3, which is the lane's own
stated cause, and are classified NOT stalled, so they get the old
"Raise `frmtmb_control(importance_rounds =)`" sentence. Their move
vectors say plainly what they are:

```
bernoulli 12x3 ds4  24 draws  3.3700605 0.9453923 0.9447889 0.9446959 0.9446821
poisson   12x3 ds1  50 draws  0.8900404 0.9059145 0.9085583 0.9089865 0.9090590
gaussian  20x3 ds3  24 draws  0.9416831 0.9082802 0.9010720 0.8998345 0.8996303
gaussian   8x3 ds2  50 draws  2.2825748 0.9661097 0.9519076 0.9499561 0.9496666
```

Every one of them settles onto the same draws-determined constant the
stalled fits of its own cell report (0.94468, 0.90907, 0.89959,
0.94961). The fixed-point map takes one or two rounds to reach its
translation constant, and `(max - min) / mean` over ALL rounds is
dominated by that transient.

The advice they get is wrong, and I measured how wrong. Same fit,
`importance_rounds` 5 then 10:

| fit | shift at 5 rounds | at 10 rounds | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| poisson 12x3 ds1, 50 draws | 4.5226 | 9.0679 | **2.005** |
| gaussian 20x3 ds3, 24 draws | 4.5505 | 9.0485 | **1.988** |

That is exactly the failure item 2 was raised to remove, still present
on a fit the new gate declines to catch.

**The rule itself is good.** Cross-tabulating the 184 capped fits by
`imp_stalled()` against the collapsed component the message blames:

| Laplace `sd(alpha)` | not flagged | flagged stalled |
|---|---|---|
| below 1e-3 (collapsed) | **6** | 139 |
| 1e-3 to 1e-2 | 6 | 0 |
| above 1e-2 (survives) | 31 | **2** |

139 of the 141 it flags really have a collapsed component, and it finds
139 of the 145 that do. That is a 98.6 percent precise, 95.9 percent
complete classifier, and nothing regresses: the six it misses get in
0.55 exactly what they got in 0.54.0.

This is HIGH not because the behaviour is wrong but because the
recorded margins are wrong by three orders of magnitude, and
consolidation will quote them into a release. A threshold whose
documented safety factor is 39 and whose measured safety factor is 1.5
is a threshold nobody will re-measure before moving something near it.

**What I do NOT recommend.** The obvious repair is to judge the rounds
after the first. I measured it on the same 184 fits and it is not
better: false negatives fall from 6 to 3, but fits whose component
survives and are wrongly blamed on `VarCorr()` rise from 2 to 4, and
the margins collapse to 1.04 and 1.09. Both statistics are fragile;
the shipped one is the safer of the two. The fix is to the recorded
numbers, not to the code.

**Asked for:** correct the roxygen at `R/importance.R:1082-1110`, the
findings table, and the NEWS wording to the measured 1.54 and 2.10 on a
184-fit set, and record the transient-first-round miss as a known
limitation beside `imp_stalled()`.

### F2. MEDIUM. The backlog's "step cap" is wrong; the lane is right

The lane says there is no step cap and the constant step is a property
of the draws. **The lane is right and the backlog is wrong.**

`dev/feature-gaps.md:1438-1448` says "the correction is walking at its
step limit" and "the reported shift is exactly rounds times the cap".
It took that from `dev/learn2-findings.md`, which says the same at
lines 16, 257-265, 294 and 1090-1093 ("it walks at its step limit until
the rounds run out", "the shift is the step cap times the round count").

There is no such limit. `importance_fit()` computes
`moved <- max(abs((opt$par - par) * (par_units %||% 1)))`, which bounds
nothing, and `optimize_obj()` passes `optCtrl` straight to the
optimizer. Measured on the 12x3 sd=0 fixture, five rounds each:

| optimizer setting | mean step |
|---|---|
| nlminb, defaults | 0.90907614 |
| nlminb, `optCtrl$step.max = 0.05` | 0.90907045 |
| nlminb, `optCtrl$step.max = 100` | 0.90907614 |
| `optimizer = "optim"` (L-BFGS-B) | 0.90906956 |

A 2000-fold change in the optimizer's own step bound, and a different
optimizer, move the number in the sixth digit. It is not a cap.

The lane's positive claim survives a much harder test than it set
itself. Grouping the 141 stalled fits by `(groups, draws,
importance_seed)` alone gives 18 cells, **every one of which spans all
three families and seven to nine distinct datasets**:

| cell (groups/draws/seed) | fits | families | step | within-cell relative spread |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8/24/1 | 8 | 3 | 0.76047777 | 7.2e-06 |
| 8/50/1 | 8 | 3 | 0.94961283 | 1.1e-05 |
| 8/100/1 | 8 | 3 | 1.04481990 | 8.8e-06 |
| 12/50/1 | 8 | 3 | 0.90907055 | 1.2e-05 |
| 12/100/1 | 8 | 3 | 0.40242440 | 1.6e-05 |
| 20/50/1 | 7 | 3 | 0.34337717 | 8.4e-06 |
| 8/50/7 | 8 | 3 | 0.31792229 | 3.4e-05 |

Fifteen of the eighteen cells are below 2e-04; the three loosest
(4.9e-02, 7.7e-03, 1.5e-03) are the cells whose step is smallest,
0.013 to 0.028, where the anchor is nearly at the fixed point. A
bernoulli, a poisson and a gaussian fit of unrelated data agree on the
step to five or six digits whenever they share the group count, the
draw count and the seed. The step is a property of the draws.

**Asked for:** correct `dev/feature-gaps.md` item 2 and the four places
in `dev/learn2-findings.md` that call the constant step a cap. The
arithmetic in both is right; only the word "cap" is wrong.

### F5. MEDIUM, FIXED HERE. A blind spot in `frm_hazard_reads()`

`R/hazard-containers.R`, `walk()`/`part()`.

The two scanners were claimed to agree. They do on the whole monorepo
and on the pre-sweep coupling tree (34 = 34, same pairs, reproduced),
but they did not on an adversarial control. Running the OLD scanner
from `a2deb65`'s test file and the NEW `frm_hazard_reads()` over the
same 20-line file:

```
only in source   : ctx$mix
```

`ctx$mix` was written as `function(ctx, j = ctx$mix) j` INSIDE another
function's body. Cause, pinned directly:

```
class(quote(function(ctx, j = ctx$mix) j)[[2L]])  ->  "pairlist"
is.call(same)                                     ->  FALSE
```

`walk()` recursed only into parts that `is.call()`, and a `function`
expression carries its formals as a pairlist, so the recursion stepped
over every inner function's defaults. The top-level formals were
handled by a separate explicit loop, which is why `f(pars, k =
pars$beta)` was caught and the inner one was not. Four shapes, before
the fix: a top-level default argument and an inner function's BODY were
both reported; a named local closure's default and an anonymous
`vapply()` callback's default both reported NOTHING FOUND.

This is the same class of hole the lane says it already closed once
(`fit$frame$data`), and it matters more than a normal scanner bug
because the export exists so that extensions can rely on it: an
extension outside this monorepo would pass its own guard on a read the
project's own rule calls a hit.

**Fixed here**, minimally: `part()` now converts a pairlist to a call
before descending, guarded for the empty formal the same way the empty
argument was already guarded. After the fix all four shapes are found,
the whole monorepo is still 0 hits in all eight packages by both
scanners, and pre-sweep coupling is still 34 = 34. The case is pinned
twice, in the environment control and in the two-scanner control, so
the file now compares three reads rather than two.

### F9. LOW. Item 3's "found, not fixed" is correctly scoped

The lane records that for a stationary point the `+/-e2` stencil enters
`.se` through `f2`, and for a crossing it enters nothing reported. Both
halves are true of the code. I tried to make a maximum land within `e2`
of a knot on a single-`ps()` fit by walking the grid end from 1e-2 to
5e-6 of the span inside the knot; the located peaks stayed at 0.2582 and
0.7500 with curvature -32.0054 and 34.8756 and standard errors 0.011138
and 0.010981, stable to six digits across all three grids, because a
peak of a fitted sine is nowhere near the span edge. Constructing it
needs a curve whose stationary point sits at the knot, which is a
separate exercise. I would file it as a recorded debt in
`dev/feature-gaps.md` rather than leave it only in a lane findings file,
since that file will be superseded.

One thing in that section is loose: the lane offers "a crossing 3e-05
inside the span reports se 0.14249 where the same curve reports 0.0131
in the middle" as showing "what that looks like from outside". For a
crossing the standard error is `qf(C0)/|f1|` and `e2` enters nothing, so
that contrast is the delta method dividing by a small slope near the end
of the range, not the stencil cliff. The lane says as much two sentences
earlier; the illustration contradicts it.

## Item 9, the questions asked directly

**Is the public export justified?** R does not require it. `:::` from
a test suite works and `R CMD check` does not flag it: 42 of core's
test files already use `frmtmb:::`, `test-importance.R` alone had 77
such calls at `a2deb65`, and core checks with one environmental NOTE.
So the alternative is real, it is already the house idiom, and it costs
one character per call site.

I would keep the export anyway, for a reason the lane does not state:
the guard is meant to reach packages that are not in this monorepo, and
a `:::` contract is one CRAN policy reading away from being unusable
there, whereas an exported testing aid is not. The documentation carries
its own weight here, and it does: the Rd's first sentence is "A testing
aid for frmtmb and its extensions, not part of a fitting workflow", and
`@details` says what it cannot see. That is the plain statement the
brief asks for. It sits under `_pkgdown.yml`'s "Package" section beside
`frmtmb-package` and `habit_prep`, which is the right shelf.

**Scanner equivalence over every R directory in the monorepo.** Both
scanners, all eight packages, source scanner taken verbatim from
`a2deb65`:

```
frmtmb           source 0  namespace 0  same pairs TRUE
frmtmb.coupling  source 0  namespace 0  same pairs TRUE
frmtmb.eam       source 0  namespace 0  same pairs TRUE
frmtmb.latent    source 0  namespace 0  same pairs TRUE
```

An empty tree proves nothing, so the pre-sweep `frmtmb.coupling` at
`7a01ed2` was extracted independently and scanned by both: 34 and 34,
same pairs, `setdiff` empty both ways. Adversarial control, both
scanners on one file: a `$` inside a string, inside a comment, on the
result of a call, inside `quote()`, inside a formula, a backtick name,
a string slot, `a$b$c` where only the middle link is a hazard name,
`fit[["frame"]]$data`, `av[, 1]$cens` with an empty argument, a
top-level binding that is not a function, an inner function's body and
an inner function's default argument. Nine hits, agreeing after F5's
fix except for the backtick spelling of F6. Top-level code outside any
function is seen by the source scanner and not by the namespace one,
exactly as the help says.

**The three layouts.** The property the old header protected is kept:

| layout | core's own source scan | extension source scan | every-extension-has-the-file |
|---|---|---|---|
| full monorepo checkout | runs, fails closed | runs | runs |
| built tarball, `extensions/` stripped | skips (no `../../R`) | skips | skips |
| sparse or partial checkout | runs if `R/` present | skips (no `extensions/`) | skips |

All three assertions share one `bracket_source_dir()` gate, and the new
one adds `skip_if_not(length(dirs) > 0L)` on top, so the tarball and
partial-checkout cases cannot fail open OR fail spuriously. `.Rbuildignore`
carries `^extensions$`, so the tarball case is the one that actually
ships. The one new coupling is that any directory under `extensions/`
with an `R/` subdirectory must carry the file, which is the intended
fail-closed behaviour; note it also means a scratch package parked there
breaks core's suite.

Measured rather than reasoned, in both directions:

* The extension half RUNS inside a tarball check. `frmtmb.spline`'s
  own `R CMD check` reports `SKIP 1`, and the one skip is
  `test-message-uniqueness.R:32:3`, not the bracket test. Same for

* Core's suite DOES fail when an extension in the tree lacks the file.
  I moved `extensions/frmtmb.ode/tests/testthat/test-bracket-access.R`
  aside, ran core's `test-bracket-access.R`, and got

## Suspicions chased and disproved

Recorded because a reviewer's negative results are worth as much as the
positive ones, and because each of these cost a measurement.

1. **"The constant step is an nlminb PORT step bound."** `nlminb` does
   have a `step.max` control and `optCtrl` is passed to it untouched,
   so this looked likely. Disproved: `step.max` at 0.05 and at 100 give
   the same step to six digits, and L-BFGS-B gives it too. See F2.
2. **"The relative spread is not scale free, because `moved` is a max
   over parameters and `par_units` can change which parameter wins."**
   The mechanism is real, the effect is not reachable where it matters.
   `autoscale = TRUE` against `FALSE` moved the spread from 6.03e-06 to
   2.81e-05 on a deeply stalled fit, four orders below the threshold
   either way, and by less than 0.1 percent (2.1026e-02 to 2.1034e-02,
   and 4.6207e-02 to 4.6209e-02) on the two fits nearest the threshold.
   I could not construct a rescaling that flipped a classification.
3. **"The new spline gate will fire on a legitimate model."** Twelve
   constructions, listed in F4, and only the intended one refuses.
4. **"The extra `predict()` on an unpinned grid will error where the
   search did not."** A grid carrying a character column, an integer id
   column, the response, and a column containing `NA` all predict
   cleanly. Disproved.
5. **"The `frm_curve_feature()` estimate changes when the second check
   moves above the stencil build."** It does not: 0.840355589493 and
   0.14249/0.0131381 are identical across both builds on every case
   that still returns.
6. **"`imp_stalled(NULL)` or a one-round fit will error."**
   `length(moves) < 2L` catches both; `moves` is always present when
   `capped` is TRUE.
7. **"Core's testthat WARN is new."** Same warning, same line, same
   text against the unmodified 0.54.0.
8. **"The container list drifted when it moved into `R/`."** `diff` of
   the two extracted blocks is empty, and the installed
   `frmtmb:::hazard_containers` is `identical()` to `a2deb65`'s literal.
9. **"The 0.9496142 in the findings table does not reproduce on core."**
   It did not, on my construction, and I nearly reported it. The lane's
   construction is in `debts-item2-seed.R` and `debts-item2-core-stall.R`
   in the shared scratchpad: the "8 subjects" rows are `frmtmb.learn`
   `bandit2arm_delta()` fits, and the core row is data seed 5, not data
   seed 1 (the "seed 1" in that table is the IMPORTANCE seed). Under the
   lane's construction the numbers reproduce, and my wider sweep confirms
   the claim far more strongly than the lane's five rows did. The
   standing lesson held.
10. **"The extension guard will skip under a tarball check, the way
    core's source scan does, so it buys nothing."** It runs.
    `frmtmb.spline`'s tarball check skips exactly one test and it is
    `test-message-uniqueness.R:32:3`; `frmtmb.learn` skips ten and none
    of them is the bracket test; `frmtmb.ode` skips none and passes 212.
11. **"Moving the second span check above the stencil build changes
    which roots are found."** The roots and their standard errors are
    identical on every returning case in twelve constructions.
