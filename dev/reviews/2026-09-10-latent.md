### R3-BLOCKER. The remedy `?lca` points at does not recover coverage

`dev/rev-latent-profile.R`, and `dev/rev-latent-ztail.R` for the
mechanism.

**The subset, and why it is the right one.** The recovery table scores
a CONTRAST, `theta_{perm[a]} - theta_{perm[1]}`, because frmtmb
references the last class and the truth references class 1.
`confint(method = "profile")` profiles a named parameter, not a
contrast, so it applies to the scored quantity only where
`perm[1] == K`. On the fresh block that is 59 of the 200 replicates,
and Wald is recomputed on the SAME 59 so the comparison is like for
like. All 118 profile intervals computed, none failed.

| | c3_3 (`theta2_x21`) | c4_3 (`theta3_x21`) | pooled |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wald coverage | 51 / 59 = 86.4% | 52 / 59 = 88.1% | **103 / 118 = 87.3%** |
| profile coverage | 51 / 59 = 86.4% | 52 / 59 = 88.1% | **103 / 118 = 87.3%** |
| median width, Wald | 0.5592 | 0.6182 | |
| median width, profile | 0.5597 | 0.6188 | |
| median ratio | 1.001 | 1.001 | **1.001** |

Profile is wider on 118 of 118 intervals and **not one of them changed
a coverage verdict.** 4.03 s each, 9.6 minutes for the subset.

**Why it cannot work here.** A profile interval widens a Wald interval
when the profile log-likelihood is not quadratic in the parameter. That
is a curvature problem. What is happening instead is that the reported
standard error is below the estimator's sampling spread, uniformly:

| block | coef | sd(z) | IQR(z)/1.349 | ratio | kurtosis |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| fresh | c3_3 | 1.0847 | 1.1204 | 0.968 | 2.600 |
| fresh | c4_3 | 1.1770 | 1.1599 | 1.015 | 2.950 |
| tuning | c3_3 | 1.0343 | 0.9524 | 1.086 | 3.086 |
| tuning | c4_3 | 0.9761 | 0.8855 | 1.102 | 3.063 |

`sd(z)` is 1 when the reported standard error matches the spread. On
the fresh block it is 1.08 and 1.18, and the robust spread agrees with
it to within 3 percent with kurtosis BELOW 3. There is no heavy tail
and no curvature: the whole distribution is inflated. Profiling
addresses the shape of the likelihood and the shortfall is in the
magnitude of the standard error, so the page's diagnosis and its remedy
are mismatched.

**What would clear it.** Drop the pointer, or replace it with something
measured. Two candidates the lane could price cheaply, neither of which
this review has measured and neither of which should go on the page
unmeasured: `confint(method = "boot")`, which estimates the spread
rather than reading it off the Hessian, and `hypothesis(method =
"profile")` with a `lincomb`, which is the only profile route that
reaches the CONTRAST at all and therefore the only one applicable to
141 of the 200 replicates. Saying "the interval is 4 to 6 points narrow
on these two coefficients and we do not yet have a remedy" is also
fine, and is better than a pointer that does not work.

quadratically.** `grad_tol` is documented as a convergence test, and a

### Verified: the tolerance's precondition holds structurally

The `grad_tol^2` argument needs a smooth interior optimum. In `hmm()`
every constrained quantity is on a link (a state sd through log, a
transition through a reference-cell logit, a random-effect sd through
log), so the outer coordinates are unbounded and there is no boundary
to sit on. The nearest thing is a variance running to zero, and that is
an asymptote rather than a boundary: constructed on 12 sequences of 40
with no group variation (`dev/rev-latent-tol.R`, seed 606), the fit
lands at `sd(re) = 0.0329` with `max|grad| / |logLik| = 2.10e-07`, so
the gradient does vanish, and `hmm_starts()` handles it (4 converged, 0
not, 0 errored, `scale_how = "se"`).

The tolerance is also generous: the observed spread over 8 refits that
all reached ONE optimum is 3.30e-10 absolute, 1.35e-12 relative, and
the tolerance is 1e-6 relative, which is **743 657x** the noise it
exists to absorb. It could be far tighter and still kill the false
alarm. That is a comment on headroom, not a defect.

## 1. `hmm_starts()` as a guard

### 1.1 BLOCKER: the remedy fires on a correct model, 6 of 6

`dev/rev-latent-guard3.R`, case F. Six independent well-behaved
two-state chains (`small_data(seed = 4501..4506)`, 10 sequences of 20),
one `hmm_starts(n = 8, jitter = 2)` each, `hmm_starts` seeds 8801 to
8806. On every one of the six, all 8 refits converged to the same
optimum and `nrow($modes)` was 1. What the summary then said:

| data seed | modes | spread | best minus original | printed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4501 | 1 | 1.35e-09 | 3.33e-10 | "found a local optimum" |
| 4502 | 1 | 1.32e-09 | 9.40e-10 | "found a local optimum" |
| 4503 | 1 | 4.64e-10 | 4.64e-10 | "found a local optimum" |
| 4504 | 1 | 2.03e-09 | 6.04e-11 | "found a local optimum" |
| 4505 | 1 | 1.46e-09 | 1.46e-09 | "found a local optimum" |
| 4506 | 1 | 9.45e-09 | 5.72e-11 | "found a local optimum" |

The verbatim line, from seed 4501:

```
  best logLik    -245.6213689
  original       -245.6213689   (3.334e-10 BELOW the best: the
                                 original fit found a local optimum)

  logLik spread, the original and the 8 converged refits: 1.346819545e-09

  distinct optima among the converged: 1
```

The function contradicts itself in one printout. `hmm_starts_modes()`
merges two log-likelihoods into one optimum when they agree to
`grad_tol^2` relative, which on that fit is **2.46e-04 log-likelihood
units** (`dev/rev-latent-guard4.R`, section III). It correctly reports
one optimum. The sentence three lines above it fires on a difference as
small as **5.7e-11**, because `gap > 0` in
`print.frmtmb_hmm_starts()` and `lli > as.numeric(stats::logLik(best))`
in `hmm_starts()` carry no tolerance at all. The ratio between the merge
threshold and the largest observed false trigger is **1.7e05**.

This is not a rare corner. If the `n + 1` values are exchangeable, the
incumbent loses to at least one refit with probability `n / (n + 1)`,
which is 8 of 9 at the default `n = 8`, and 6 of 6 has probability 0.49
under that model, so the measurement is consistent with it. The refit
gradients on those runs (3.07e-08 to 2.13e-06 against the incumbent's
1.08e-06) give no reason to think the refits are systematically tighter,
so exchangeability is the right way to read the rate: about nine runs in
ten, at the shipped default.

The consequence is not only cosmetic. `$best` is then a REFIT rather
than the fit the user handed in, so `hmm_starts()` silently substitutes
a different fitted object for a gain of 3e-10.

The lane's own rule is the one being broken: "a check that fires on a
correct model is worse than no check", and the brief's "a remedy that
fires on a correct model is a real cost". The suite does not cover this
branch: no test in `test-hmm-starts.R` constructs a unimodal fit and
asserts what the summary says about it.

**The fix is measured, not argued.** `dev/rev-latent-fixcheck.ps1` and
`dev/rev-latent-fixcheck.R` build mutant F1, which gives both
comparisons the relative tolerance `hmm_starts_modes()` already uses,
install it, and run both ends against it:

| | shipped | with F1 |
|---|---|---|
| false alarms on the six unimodal fits | 6 of 6 | **0 of 6** |
| `$best` left as the incumbent there | 0 of 6 | **6 of 6** |
| the d4 probe still reports the local optimum | yes | **yes**, margin 7389x |
| `test-hmm-starts.R` | 57 pass | **57 pass, 0 fail** |

Two lines. The suite should also gain a test that asserts the print says
"the original fit was the best found" on `small_data()`, since nothing
covers that branch today.

### 1.6 NIT: the alignment check in `hmm_starts_scale()` is a tautology

`hmm_starts_scale()` documents itself as CHECKING an assumption:

> `confint()` reports one row per outer parameter, in the order
> `fit[["opt"]][["par"]]` holds them, but that alignment is an
> assumption rather than a contract, so it is CHECKED against the `est`
> column and abandoned when it does not hold: a misaligned scale vector
> would jitter the wrong parameters and the failure would be invisible.

`confint.frmtmb_fit()` sets `est <- object$opt$par` (`R/confint.R:383`)
and builds the returned matrix as
`cbind(lwr = est + q * se, upr = est - q * se, est = est)`. The check
therefore compares a vector with itself. Measured
(`dev/rev-latent-guard1.R`): `identical(ci[, "est"], opt$par)` is TRUE
and `max |est - opt$par|` is exactly 0.

The failure the comment describes is a permutation of the STANDARD
ERRORS relative to `opt$par`, and those come from `sdr$cov.fixed`. The
check never looks at them, so it cannot see that failure. It is not
harmful, and the row-count check beside it is a genuine (if also
near-tautological) guard, but the comment claims protection the code
does not give. Either say what it actually rules out, or check
something that can differ.

## 5. `hmm_starts_scale()`'s `"unit"` fallback: reachable, and how

`dev/rev-latent-guard3.R` section H. The lane says it could not build a
fit that reaches the branch without breaking the thing under test, and
records that as an evidence gap. Confirmed, and here is the reason,
which is more useful than the gap.

The branch has four entrances. Two of them are closed:

- **`nrow(ci) != length(par)`** and **the `est` alignment test** both
  read quantities that `confint.frmtmb_fit()` derives from
  `object$opt$par` itself. Measured: `identical(ci[, "est"], opt$par)`
  is TRUE, `nrow(ci) == length(opt$par)` is TRUE. The only way either
  can differ is `confint()`'s own `length(est) != length(se)` fallback,
  which the core's comment at `R/confint.R:398` calls defensive only
  ("profiled betas are absent from BOTH `opt$par` and `par.fixed` in
  current RTMB").

- **No usable standard error at all** requires EVERY entry of
  `(upr - lwr) / (2 * qnorm(0.975))` to be non-finite or non-positive.
  Five degenerate constructions were tried and none got there:

  | construction | outer par | se finite and positive | `scale_how` |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | K = 2 on one-state data | 6 | 6 of 6 | se |
  | K = 3 on one-state data | 12 | 12 of 12 | se |
  | K = 3 on 8 rows | 12 | 11 of 12 | se-partial |
  | perfectly collinear covariate | 8 | 8 of 8 | se |
  | two-valued response | 6 | 5 of 6 | se-partial |

  Degeneracy costs individual standard errors, and losing one lands in
  `"se-partial"`, which is the branch beside it. Losing all of them at
  once needs the whole inverse Hessian to come back unusable, and a fit
  that far gone does not reach `hmm_starts()` intact.

The fourth entrance is open: **`confint()` erroring**. The branch runs
and returns `list(s = rep(1, npar), how = "unit")` when handed an object
whose `confint()` throws, demonstrated on a minimal stub.

So the honest statement, which the lane can put in place of the gap, is
this: the `"unit"` fallback is reachable only through a `confint()` that
errors, and no `hmm()` fit constructed here errors there. It is not
dead code and it is not exercised by any fit, and the two other
entrances the comment names cannot fire for structural reasons. Worth
noting that if it ever did fire, `jitter = 2` on the internal scale
would move a log standard deviation by two units, so the practical
consequence is refits that error rather than refits that mislead. That
is "fail closed", as the lane says.

## 9. The design question: is a documented recipe defensible for `lca()`?

**No, not as the row currently ships, and the fix is not
`lca_starts()`.**

What the measurement says, all of it re-derived above:

| | `hmm()` | `lca()` |
|---|---|---|
| the failure | 8.099 units, one hand-built probe | 243 to 284 units |
| how often, at the plan's realistic design | not measured | **8 of 200, 4.0 percent** |
| passes pdHess and relative gradient under 1e-3 | yes | **7 of 8** |
| `diagnose()` silent | yes | on 1 of 8 |
| what shipped | `hmm_starts()`, a new export | a help-page section |
| what the remedy costs | 24 minutes at the realistic scale | **6 to 17 seconds** |
| does the shipped remedy's recipe work on this family | yes, 5 of 5 seeds | **no, 0 of 8** |

The asymmetry runs the wrong way on every row. The family with the
larger error, the measured population rate, and the cheaper remedy got
the smaller response.

Three things make this more than a scope quibble.

**One.** The plan's Rule 3 ranks a silent wrong answer above a refusal
above a missing feature. Item 2.4's row said "none expected" and the
lane found a 4 percent silent-wrong-answer rate on a shipped exported
function. That finding changes the row's own classification: it is no
longer a measurement row with a null result, it is a measurement row
that found a defect, and Rule 3 says the defect outranks the feature
that was not built.

**Two.** Deferring to `frm_multistart()` defers to something that, as
filed, does not fix this. The core seam is specified as
`frm_multistart(fit, n, jitter)`, jitter around the incumbent. The lane
measured that exact recipe recovering **0 of 8** and leaving 72 to 273
units on the table, and on one data set (20261013) it did not improve on
the cold start in ten refits. The lane records this and recommends the
seam row gain a note about it, which is the right call and is the single
most valuable thing this lane learned. But until that note is acted on,
"deferred to the seam" means deferred to a design that would not have
helped.

**Three.** The lane was RIGHT not to build `lca_starts()`. Two copies of
one core seam is worse than one seam, and the `?lca` recipe is the
second family of starts that the seam needs, written out by hand. That
part of the reasoning is sound and should stand.

So the objection is narrow and the fix is cheap. Before this row is
marked done, one of these two:

- `lca()` emits a message on fitting, naming the measured rate and the
  recipe. It fires on every fit, so it has no false-alarm cost in the
  sense the standards mean, and it says something true about every fit.
  This is what the `?lca` prose cannot do, because a user who does not
  open the help page never sees it, and a user who is 280 units wrong is
  by construction a user with no reason to open it.
- Or the plan row is explicitly reclassified, in
  `dev/extension-gaps-plan.md`, from a measurement row to a KNOWN SILENT
  DEFECT with the remedy deferred and a note that the seam as filed does
  not supply it. The lane already drafted the text; it just has to be
  labelled as a defect rather than as a measurement.

The second is cheaper and this reviewer would take it. What is not
defensible is shipping `hmm_starts()` and the `?lca` paragraph in the
same release with the row marked "measured, DONE" and no record that
`lca()` carries the worse of the two defects.

`conv_ll` is built from `status %in% c("original", "converged")`
regardless of `original_converged`, so the modes table can carry a row
for a fit that failed the test. Deliberate and explained in the code
comment, but the label is not accurate. Seen in
`dev/rev-latent-guard2.R` case C.

### NIT 6. `$spread` reads 0 when nothing was measured

Every refit erroring gives `spread = 0` and `spread_all = 0`. `print()`
says "errored 4, reaching no optimum at all", so a reader of the print
is safe; a caller reading `ms$spread` is not. `NA` would be truthful.
`dev/rev-latent-guard2.R` case D.

### NIT 7. The arm tables mislead a later reader in two ways

`identity_ok` is the LABEL match against the truth, not the third-party
match, and it reads 1 on all 15 arm B rows including the 9 with no
hmmTMB fit. Separately, numeric NA is written padded with spaces, so
`read.delim()` returns a character column and a denominator taken
straight off the file counts 15 reference fits where 6 ran. This
reviewer hit both.

### NIT 8. "299 assertions" counts 2 skip records as assertions

`test-scale.R` contributes 2 in this counting and 0 passing assertions
in `dev/suite-baseline.tsv`'s definition. Nothing dropped; the join is
exact either way. Section 6.2.

### NIT 9. The cost control covers one of the three measured terms

183.5 s and 37.2 s are single uncontrolled readings and both feed the
`n = 4 / 8 / 16` projections. Section 8.

### NIT 10. Two `skip_if()` can drop 3 to 4 assertions silently

`skip_if(!is.finite(best))` and `skip_if(anyDuplicated(p) > 0L)` in
"a three-state gaussian HMM agrees with depmixS4" abandon the block
after 1 and after 2 assertions, so they drop 4 and 3 of the block's 5.
On this run neither fired (0 skips in `test-hmm.R`), and the baseline
join would catch a drop, but a label permutation on another platform
turns an identity test into a green line.
