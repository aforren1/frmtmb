combination. `g2` then drops the WHOLE gradient of those coefficients, not

code-1 warning is dropped too (`!bstop`).

Reproducer `selimit2-review-grad2.R` (logs `grad2-lane.txt`,

Group b's mean is 85 percent off, the gradient is 94.2 on each coefficient

## F2 (MEDIUM-HIGH). The joint verdict fires on a finite MLE

Rare-event poisson, `y ~ x`: x = 30 points on [0, 10] with y = 0, then y = 1
at x = 15 and at x = 15 - delta. Two counts a distance delta apart cannot
both be matched as the slope grows, so the MLE is finite.
`selimit2-review-fp.R lane pois` (logs `fp-lane-c.txt`, `fp-base-c.txt`).
Profile along the stored joint direction (natural units, rest held; the
re-optimized profile is the same):

    delta 1e-4 lane: "(Intercept) (at -58.88, toward -Inf), x (at 3.925, toward +Inf): ... the maximum likelihood estimate does not exist", SEs NaN NaN
       held t: -10:9.51e-08 -2:1.29e-09 2:6.2e-10 12:1.27e-08 100:2.26e-07 1000:1.16e-05 10000:0.00105 100000:0.105 1e+06:23.1
    delta 1e-5 lane: same verdict; held t: 1000:1.18e-07 10000:1.05e-05 100000:0.00104 1e+06:0.105
    delta 1e-3 lane: same verdict (borderline: 12 units 1.05e-6)
    base: delta 1e-3 SEs 5131.9 342.1 (no word); 1e-4 and 1e-5 "flat" (a true description)
    true limit (one count at the top): held t up to 1e5 within 1.2e-10 (correct)

The stop is a local minimum of the objective along the direction at
delta 1e-4 (both sides positive at 2 units) and the objective rises without
bound further out, so "the maximum likelihood estimate does not exist" is
false, and the brief's acceptance ("no false positive on finite MLEs")
fails. The rule's thresholds (level to 1e-6 at 2 and 14 units) accept any
interior maximum with curvature below about 5e-7 natural units; the joint
search makes that region reachable in every coding. A longer probe is not
a proof either: the true hurdle limit reads +2.89e-6 at 1e6 units from
rounding (`fp-lane-c.txt`). Expected: no limit verdict here. Fix: certify
a joint end exactly where the family allows it (the recession-direction
test of detectseparation: for a count or bernoulli mean, every row with
`X w` above a relative 1e-8 of the largest move must have the outcome the
move sends it to; for zi/hu/zoi, every row moved up is the inflated value),
else leave the coefficients "flat". Add this case as the absent case of
the 4800 test (its present absent case, one count in group a, has a
curvature of about 1 and never reaches the joint search).

## F3 (MEDIUM, silent wrong value). "Inside the fitted span" is not a proof

`R/se-check.R:2764` (se_limit_span()) and `:4200` (jc_limit_extrap()): a row
whose move `a` along a direction lies within the fitted rows' range keeps
the test at the estimate. That premise holds for the fitted rows' own
outcome probabilities, not for a new row whose other columns differ.
`selimit2-review-span.R` (logs `span-lane.txt`, `span-base.txt`), ZIP,
`zi ~ x + z`: rows A (x = 0, z in [0, 1], zi = plogis(-9 + 18 z)) fix
zi's intercept and z slope; rows B (x in [1, 2], z = 0) have no zeros, so
zi_x runs off (quiet boundary message, zi_x "limit" at -8.309). New rows
inside every column's observed range:

    row (x, z)   Estimate  Est.Error   mean over zi_x in [-30, -6] (objective within 5.4e-7)
    (1, 1)       0.2165    0.4857      4.0639 ... 0.0226   (8.3 SE)
    (1, 0.8)     3.3697    0.8433      4.0639 ... 1.3226   (3.3 SE)
    (1.5, 0.9)   3.9454    0.2625      4.0639 ... 2.0751   (7.6 SE)

Base and lane print the same numbers: finite SEs, no warning, while a
zi_x that fits as well moves the mean by 3 to 8 SEs; the point estimate is
one arbitrary point of that set (the limit model's value is 4.0639). The
lane's own principle (NEWS: a row "that a limit direction moves away from
the data" gets NaN) applies to these rows, and its span rule exempts them.
The eam premise the span rule rests on is a heuristic: it hides real moves
of the mean along the level set. Expected: NaN and the warning for rows 1
to 3. Fix: measure the move with the end coordinates taken to their limit
(move the end along its own direction to the far end of its level range
before moving the undetermined parameters, and compare against the value
at the estimate), test every row, and drop the span exemption; that keeps
the eam fitted rows finite (at weight 0 the wiener parameters do not
enter) and makes these three rows NaN. If not fixed in this lane, file it
HIGH (silent wrong) and change the NEWS bullet, which now says such rows
keep a valid test.

## The user's questions

(a) Rows inside the span: neither "NaN for every fitted row" nor the span
rule. Measure each row's move over the level set with the end taken to its
limit first (F3's fix). The eam mixture's fitted rows then keep their
SEs for a stated reason (at weight 0 the undetermined parameters do not
enter the mean), and a row whose mean the level set really moves gets NaN
whether or not its `a` lies in the span (F3's three rows). Today's span
rule is neither the limit model's answer (it reports the stop's value, 0.22
in F3, not the limit's 4.06) nor the level set's.

(b) asym_laplace() without random effects: refuse the Hessian SE by name;
do not add a sandwich now. `selimit2-review-asym.R` (`asym.txt`, 4 seeds,
quantiles 0.3 and 0.5, n = 200): frmtmb's estimates equal quantreg::rq's
to 2e-5 in 7 of 8 fits (seed 1, quantile 0.3: 2e-3 off, check loss 3.1e-5
worse, code 1), and every fit warns twice. rq's own SEs differ by method by
up to 2.0 times on one fit (seed 1, quantile 0.3, intercept: nid 0.050,
ker 0.094, iid 0.046), so a sandwich is a bandwidth choice that brms has no
counterpart for. A named refusal (the log density is piecewise linear in
the location, so the Hessian gives no variance; frmtmb.sample for
posterior SDs, or quantreg::rq() for a sandwich) replaces today's two
optimizer warnings with one true message. A sandwich can be a separate,
filed feature with rq parity as its test.
