## Findings, ranked

### F1 (must fix, blocks the claim not the code). Malingering_2's residual is misattributed, and the model is a fourth unbounded-under-ML fit the lane did not identify

`dev/bcm-findings.md:201-206` says the -2.18e-05 residual "is the
difference between `beta_binomial_lpmf`'s normalizing constant and
`RTMBdist`'s, accumulated over 22 rows". That is not what it is. A
normalizing-constant convention would appear in BOTH components and be
independent of the shapes. Measured, decomposing the same fit against a
longhand `lchoose(N,k) + lbeta(k+a, N-k+b) - lbeta(a,b)`:

    component 1 (phi1 = 7.52)      RTMBdist - exact, summed:  -7.1e-14
    component 2 (phi2 = 7.19e+08)  RTMBdist - exact, summed:  +2.73e-05
    stan - longhand   7.9e-08      longhand - frmtmb  -2.19e-05
    RTMBdist total mass over 0..45, component 2:  1.000002167954
    exact  total mass over 0..45, component 2:    0.999999998102

So component 1, at ordinary shapes, agrees to 1e-14. The whole residual
comes from component 2, where `alpha2 = 7.13e8` and `beta2 = 5.3e6` and
both implementations lose precision in the lgamma differences, RTMBdist
by 2.2e-6 of total mass. It is a precision loss at DIVERGENT shapes,
not a convention difference.

The reason the shapes are divergent is the real finding. Profiling the
exact mixture log-likelihood in phi2 with everything else held at the
estimate:

    phi2=1e+04   -58.7234875260
    phi2=1e+06   -58.7147459055
    phi2=1e+08   -58.7146583610
    phi2=7.19e+08 -58.7146575484   <- where the optimizer stopped
    phi2=1e+10   -58.7146572816
    phi2=Inf (binomial limit) -58.7146574774

Monotone up to the binomial limit. **The MLE of phi2 is infinity**: the
second component has no person-level overdispersion at all, and the
optimizer stops at 7.19e8 only because the surface is flat to machine
precision. (`phi2=1e+12` returns -58.7146022595, WORSE than 1e+10,
which is floating-point noise and confirms the flatness.) The reported
`Std. Error 6.2689` on `log(phi2)` in `summary()` is a Hessian on a flat
direction and is meaningless.

`tests/testthat/test-bcm-latent-mixtures.R:289-292` asserts

    # both precisions are finite, which is what says the integral was
    # done rather than the per-person rates estimated
    expect_true(all(is.finite(c(unname(fixef(fit)$phi1),
                                unname(fixef(fit)$phi2)))))

The inference is unsound. `fixef()` returns `log(phi)`, so the assertion
passes at `log(phi2) = 20.39` and would pass at any numerically finite
value however divergent. What phi2 = 7.2e8 actually says is the
opposite of what the comment claims: the second group is a plain
binomial, not a dispersed one.

Reproduction: `scratchpad/rvbc-malingering.R`, `scratchpad/rvbc-phi2.R`.

Fix: restate the attribution in `dev/bcm-findings.md` and the vignette
as a precision loss at a divergent precision parameter; add
Malingering_2/Cheating to the unbounded-under-ML list beside
SevenScientists, the free contaminant and uncapped Retention; and
replace the `is.finite` assertion with one that says what is true (for
example that phi1 is finite and moderate while phi2 diverges, or bound
phi2 and assert the likelihood is within 1e-6 of the binomial limit).

### F2 (must fix). The proposed core fix for seam 4 is itself broken

`dev/bcm-findings.md` proposes that `R/families.R:2917` take
"two quantiles of `y / trials` rather than of `y`". Measured on the very
model that motivates it:

    current   quantile(y, 1/3)   = 30        qlogis -> NaN
    current   quantile(y, 2/3)   = 45        qlogis -> NaN
    proposed  quantile(y/n, 1/3) = 0.6666667 qlogis -> 0.6931472
    proposed  quantile(y/n, 2/3) = 1.0       qlogis -> Inf

Ten of the 22 respondents scored 45 of 45, so the upper quantile of
`y/trials` is exactly one and the logit of it is infinite. Starting
there does not degrade quietly, it kills the fit:

    Error: The optimizer failed on this model
    (mixture(beta_binomial, beta_binomial), ML, nlminb):
    NA/NaN gradient evaluation.

The fix has to CLAMP as well, exactly the way each component family's
own `init_dpars$mu` already does (`min(max(p, 0.02), 0.98)` at
`R/families.R:2150-2155`). With the clamp it lands correctly:

    clamped start (0.6666667, 0.98) -> mu = (0.509133, 0.992604),
    logLik -58.71466, the separated-start optimum to 5 dp.

Reproduction: `scratchpad/rvbc-seam4.R`, `scratchpad/rvbc-seam4b.R`.

### F3 (should fix). Seam 4 is stated too narrowly; the trigger is not boundedness

The findings frame seam 4 as a bounded-response problem. It is not. The
degeneracy needs EVERY component's `mu` init to fall outside the link's
range, so that all of them fall back to the same origin and the mixture
starts exactly at its exchangeable point, where the gradient in the
separating direction is zero. A zero-heavy `mixture(poisson, poisson)`
hits the same `init` bug, warns identically:

    Starting value 0 for mu1 is -Inf through its log link, so it was
    ignored and that intercept starts from zero on the link scale.

but ESCAPES, because only mu1 is spoiled and mu2's quantile is still
usable, so the components remain separated and the fit reaches
mu = (0.1894, 9.2269). That is the discriminating experiment and it
sharpens the core issue: the defect is "all components collapse to one
start", not "the response is bounded".

Reproduction: `scratchpad/rvbc-seam4b.R`.

`dev/bcm-findings.md:236` and

The number the passage needs is RTMBdist's `1.0000022`. As written the
claim does not support itself: an error of 1.9e-09 cannot accumulate

conclusion (precision loss at divergent shapes, in the family's
implementation, not a convention) is right; only the quoted figure is
from the wrong column.

Fix: in both places, quote `1.0000022` as the family's sum and keep
`0.999999998` only if the longhand is named alongside it as the
