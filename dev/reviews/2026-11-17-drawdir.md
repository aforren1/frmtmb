## Verdict: FIX

The detector does what the brief asked: it finds one weak direction
among many well-identified parameters (18 of 18 at every K, by the
block-diagonal identity, which I checked in the code), and it is silent
on 155 of 155 fits of new designs without a random slope. But:

1. A direction whose end point the objective cannot evaluate is read
   as an infinite "joint wall" and fires. On the one such fit I found,
   the direction is flat, not steep: the objective fails numerically
   76 log units out (section 4).
2. On random-slope GLMMs (8 of 126 fits of new designs fire), the
   reweighting made the in-sample predictive means worse against the
   truth on 6 of 8 fires, and every one warns k > 0.7 at 20000 draws.
   Against a sampled posterior (brms default priors) the normal law's
   90% new-level interval on the lane's own two worst fits was right;
   the reweighted one is 10% to 11% too narrow (section 2, 6). This
   needs a user decision.
3. The statistic does not measure what its docs, message and NEWS say
   ("beyond what that parameter alone shows"); the verdict on a pure
   one-parameter skew depends on the correlation, not on the size of
   the error (section 1).

The numbered FIX items are at the end.

## 1. The statistic

Notation: f the objective, theta-hat its minimum, V the law's
covariance, L its upper Cholesky factor (`crossprod(L) = V`), k =
`draw_edge_k` = 2.

Direction j: `Z = k L[, j] / ||L[, j]||`, and `||L[, j]||^2 = V[j, j]`,
so the move is `t(L) %*% Z = k V[, j] / sd_j`. That is theta_j moved k
marginal sds and every other parameter moved to its conditional mean
given theta_j under N(theta-hat, V) (`V[i, j] / V[j, j]` per unit of
theta_j). The law's drop there is `|Z|^2 / 2 = k^2 / 2 = 2` whatever the
other parameters do. Check 4's "follow by regression" is exactly the
conditional mean move, before the side law maps each coordinate.

Write f(theta-hat + u) = u'Hu / 2 + r(u). eA = r(direction), eB =
r(k csd_j e_j), stat = eA - max(eB, 0) (eB read only where eA > 6).

- Correct Gaussian law (r = 0): eA = eB = stat = 0 exactly. The lane's
  gaussian GLMs read S2 at most 4e-12. Holds.
- Non-Gaussian, r depending on theta_j alone (a one-parameter skew,
  the side law's own case): integrating the others out of the Gaussian
  conditional gives the true marginal of theta_j proportional to
  exp(-u_j^2 / (2 V_jj) - r(u_j)). So eA = r(k sd_j) is exactly the log
  error of the normal law's marginal at k marginal sds, and eB =
  r(k csd_j) is the same function nearer in (sd_j / csd_j = sratio >=
  1). For a cubic r, stat = eB (sratio^3 - 1). The subtraction does
  NOT remove a one-parameter skew; it removes it only where the
  parameter is uncorrelated.

Measured (`drawdir-review-skew.R`, log `drawdir-review-skew.txt`): a
2-D quadratic with unit marginal sds and correlation rho, plus s
max(u_a, 0)^3 / 6 in a alone. The marginal error eA = 8 s / 6 is the
same at every rho:

    rho 0.0 sratio 1.00 s 12.0 | slice excess eB 16.000 | stat  0.000 fire FALSE
    rho 0.0 sratio 1.00 s  8.0 | slice excess eB 10.667 | stat  0.000 fire FALSE
    rho 0.9 sratio 2.29 s  5.0 | slice excess eB  0.552 | stat  6.115 fire TRUE
    rho 0.9 sratio 2.29 s  8.0 | slice excess eB  0.883 | stat  9.783 fire TRUE

and stat equals eB (sratio^3 - 1) to the printed digits on every line.
At rho 0 an error of 10.7 log units at 2 sds (s = 8) passes both checks:
the side law is not steep there either (drop 1.83 at 1 csd, under 2).
At rho 0.9 an error of 6.7 fires and the message says the fall is
"beyond what that parameter alone shows", which is false: all of it is
that parameter's.

The threshold 6 = k^2/2 (1/c^2 - 1): for a quadratic misfit it means
the law's sd along the direction is at least twice the likelihood's
(drop 8 where the law says 2); for any other shape it is a density
error of e^6 = 403 at 2 sds. It is a heuristic that reuses the side
law's `draw_edge_c`, not a calibrated false-alarm rate: the statistic
is deterministic, and its "null" is not zero on GLMMs, whose variance
components are not Gaussian in log sd. The equivalence with the side
law is loose: the side law asks at which distance the drop reaches 2,
this asks the drop at a fixed distance; they agree only for a
quadratic.

The lane's "4 of 472 gaussian GLMs/GLMMs" is a correct count of its
own logs (awk over `dev/drawdir-v-{gglm,gglmm,wb}.tsv`: 128 + 144 +
200 fits, 4 fired, all S0.1/S0.2, every `lib:` line the lane library),
but it is in-sample: the threshold was chosen between those fits'
maximum (5.61) and the mixtures' minimum (13.3), with the four fires
argued away. It is not a false-alarm rate.

## 2. The 4 random-slope fires of the lane: is the normal law wrong?

`drawdir-review-mcmc.R S0.1:14 S0.1:16` (log `drawdir-review-mcmc.txt`),
new level at x = 2, 4000 draws, seed 1. Oracle (known parameters) 90%
width 4.26, 98% width 6.03. "brms" is `frm_sample()` with brms's
default priors, 4 chains of 1000 after 2000 warmup, the new level's
predictive drawn by hand from each draw (posterior_predict() on draws
refuses a new level). "mlik" is tmbstan (laplace = TRUE) on the fit's
own objective, the density the reweighting targets:

    S0.1:14  norm  cov90 0.953 w90  5.238 cov98 1.000 w98 10.724
             lane  cov90 0.916 w90  4.670 cov98 0.990 w98  6.897
             mlik  cov90 0.916 w90  4.589 cov98 0.987 w98  6.641
             brms  cov90 0.954 w90  5.274 cov98 0.996 w98  7.516
    S0.1:16  norm  cov90 0.967 w90  5.548 cov98 1.000 w98 12.276
             lane  cov90 0.927 w90  4.653 cov98 0.989 w98  6.653
             mlik  cov90 0.935 w90  4.777 cov98 0.992 w98  6.843
             brms  cov90 0.955 w90  5.201 cov98 0.995 w98  7.372

- Against a posterior, the normal law is wrong in the far tail only:
  its 98% width is 1.43 and 1.67 times brms's, while its 90% width is
  0.99 and 1.07 times brms's. The lane's "1.36 to 2.04 times the true
  width" is against the oracle law, which a posterior predictive
  should over-cover; the NEWS says "the true one".
- The reweighted law is narrower than the posterior at both levels
  (90%: 0.89 of brms's on both; 98%: 0.92 and 0.90).
- Its target is improper on these fits. tmbstan on the objective
  drifts: the log slope sd's 0.5% to 99.5% range is -112.9 to -74.1
  (S0.1:14) and the correlation parameter's -214 to 237 (-1400 to 374
  on S0.1:16), max R-hat 6.1e14 and 8.6e14. The flat-in-the-outer-
  parameters likelihood has infinite mass toward a slope sd of 0; PSIS
  sees only the part of it the normal proposal reaches. The reweighted
  interval agrees with that drifting chain (mlik), not with a proper
  posterior.

So the check fires on a real defect (the 98% tail), and the remedy it
triggers moves toward the wrong target. The fire on these fits is not a
false alarm; the reweighting is not a fix.

## 4. The regression move and the support

The outer parameters of sds and correlations are unconstrained (log
sd; the correlation parameter reads -223 at its limit), so no move can
put a correlation past +-1 or an sd below 0 on the natural scale. A
dpar with an identity link can leave its support, but on the identity-
link `sigma ~ x` fits the directions stayed inside it (sigid:4, the
"-" direction of sigma_(Intercept): sigma(x = 0) 0.125 at the end
point; sigid:5 0.056), while 31 and 49 of 4000 normal-law draws did
not.

The defect is elsewhere: the move can carry an unconstrained parameter
so far that the objective fails numerically, and a non-finite drop is
read as a "joint wall". `drawdir-review-wall.R slpw:12` (log
`drawdir-review-wall.txt`): direction theta_3 + moves the log slope sd
from -1.68 to -78.0 and the correlation parameter from 0.41 to 47.1
(sratio 6.1). Along it:

    t 0.25 drop 0.07626 law 0.1250
    t 0.50 drop 0.2345  law 0.5000
    t 0.75 drop 0.4846  law 1.1250
    t 0.90 drop NaN     law 1.6200
    theta_2 alone at -78.02: drop NaN
    theta_3 alone at 47.11: drop 0.01955

The direction is FLATTER than the law (excess -0.64 at t = 0.75); the
objective returns NaN once the log sd is below about -65 (the inner
solve; the likelihood itself tends to the zero-slope-variance value
there). The slice of theta_3 is finite (eB -1.98), so stat = Inf, and
the cached `dir` is Inf; the message would print "falls Inf further
than that law says". The theta_2 - direction is the same. slpw:12
still fires on finite directions (theta_2 + S2 26.0, sigma_(Intercept)
- S2 48.0, `drawdir-review-probe.txt`), so a fix loses nothing there.

## FIX items

1. `draw_check_dirs()`: a non-finite drop at a direction's end point
   is not evidence of a wall. Step back along the direction (for
   example t = 0.75, 0.5) and read the finite excess there against the
   law's `t^2 k^2 / 2`, or leave the direction unread; never cache or
   print `Inf` as the excess. Add a test with an objective that is NaN
   past a point along a direction that is flat before it (the
   behavioral failure is a fire on the current build). slpw:12's
   finite directions (S2 26.0 and 48.0) keep its fire.
2. USER DECISION, the reweighting on random-slope fits. The reading
   finds a real 98% tail defect there (section 2), but the reweighting's
   target is improper on those fits and its draws made in-sample
   predictions worse on 6 of 8 new fires (section 6) and the new-level
   interval 10% too narrow against a posterior on the lane's two worst
   fits. Options: (a) ship as is (warning, reweighted draws); (b) where
   the firing direction belongs to a variance-component parameter,
   warn without reweighting (the normal law's draws, which matched the
   posterior's 90% interval); (c) narrow the variance component's side
   by the side law instead. I recommend (b) for this round and filing
   (c). Whatever is chosen, the warning must not send a new-level
   prediction to `frm_sample()` without saying that it cannot predict
   a new level yet.
3. Correct the statistic's description in `?predict.frmtmb_fit`, the
   roxygen of `draw_check_dirs()` and `draw_dir_tol`, the message
   (`draw_check_said_what()`) and NEWS: it is the direction's excess
   less the slice's at a shorter distance, so a one-parameter skew of a
   correlated parameter counts in full times (sratio^3 - 1); the
   threshold is a heuristic (a sd twice too wide for a quadratic
   misfit), not a calibrated false-alarm rate; "the true one" in NEWS is
   the known-parameter law, not a posterior. Drop "beyond what that
   parameter alone shows" from the message or make it true.
4. Findings: add this review's survey (0 of 155 non-random-slope fits,
   8 of 126 random-slope fits, ordrs:9 at 6.17) beside the 472, and say
   the 472 are the fits the threshold was set on.
5. Backlog: the MEDIUM on the 3 silent fits should give the lane's
   forced-arm errors (worse on 2 of 3) and drop to LOW. File LOW: the
   Pareto k verdict, and so the warning, depends on `ndraws` (slpw:12
   k 0.56 at 200 draws, 0.80 at 20000). File LOW: the deviates'
   product is formed twice at large p (section 5); correct the
