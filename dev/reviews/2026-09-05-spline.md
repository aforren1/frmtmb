## Item 1. Cost of the covariance rebuild

### Reproduced

`y ~ s(x, k = 10)`, n = 250, 20-point grid, the package's own test model:

| quantity | measured |
|---|---|
| `n_predict` | 11 (claim: 11) |
| covariance check `rel` | 7.66e-15 (claim: 3e-15 to 9e-15) |
| `frm_curve(simultaneous = FALSE)` wall | 0.28 s |

The claimed call count and the claimed agreement both reproduce exactly.

### The cost claim measures the wrong term

The breakdown on that same k = 10 fit:

| stage | time | share |
|---|---|---|
| `sp_curve_design` (all 11 `predict()` calls) | 0.030 s | 11 % |
| `RTMB::sdreport(getJointPrecision = TRUE)` | 0.210 s | 75 % |
| `predict(se.fit = TRUE)` check call | 0.19 s (in the total) | |
| **total** | **0.28 s** | |

The design rebuild is already the *smallest* term at the size the
documentation advertises it at. `R/curve.R:43-50` ("Cost") and the
proposal's "11 `predict()` calls in about 0.04 s plus `sdreport` in a
further 0.04 s" both frame the cost as a `predict()` call count. The
call count is not what governs the cost, and it is not what governs the
scaling either (see below): `sp_joint_cov()` is.

### Scaling, measured

`s(t, k = 8) + (1 + t | subject)`, 8 rows per subject, 20-point grid,
`re.form = NA` (the default), one process each:

| random coefs | joint precision | design (all predict calls) | `sp_joint_cov` | `frm_curve` total | peak RSS |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 8 (`s(x, k=10)`) | 12 x 12 | 0.03 s | 0.26 s | 0.28 s | - |
| 500 | 513 x 513 | 0.22 s | 0.18 s | 0.33 s | - |
| 1 000 | 1013 x 1013 | 0.04 s | 0.41 s | 0.69 s | - |
| 2 000 | 2013 x 2013 | 0.45 s | 2.72 s | 3.18 s | 215 MB |
| 4 000 | 4013 x 4013 | 0.13 s | 10.78 s | 14.14 s | 610 MB |
| 8 000 | 8013 x 8013 | 0.28 s | 114.41 s | 118.47 s | 2 108 MB |

Also measured: `s(t, subject, bs = "fs", k = 10)`, 100 subjects, 1 000
random coefficients: 0.37 s at `re.form = NA` (44 calls), 0.59 s at
`re.form = NULL` (116 calls), `rel` 1.0e-14 and 1.1e-15.

The design rebuild is **flat at 0.13-0.45 s across the whole range**. The
cost the documentation quantifies is the only term that does not grow.
What grows is `sp_joint_cov()` (`R/curve-cov.R:163-202`), and it grows
faster than quadratically: 2 000 to 4 000 is 3.9x, 4 000 to 8 000 is
10.6x, because `Q <- as.matrix(Q)` (`R/curve-cov.R:192`) densifies the
whole joint precision before the Schur complement subsets it, and the
`solve(Q[rest, rest], ...)` that follows is a dense solve over every
coefficient the curve does NOT touch. At 8 000 coefficients that is a
64-million-entry dense matrix and a two-minute call.

`sdreport(getJointPrecision = TRUE)` returns `Q` as a sparse
`dgCMatrix`. Nothing in the Schur complement needs it dense: a sparse
`solve()` on `Q[rest, rest]` would keep the sparsity that TMB went to
the trouble of producing.

### Answer to item 1

**Usable at movement and growth sizes, and the shipped cost documentation
describes the wrong term.** 100 subjects x 10 coefficients, or 500
subjects with a random intercept and slope, is 1 000 coefficients and
sub-second. 2 000 subjects with intercept and slope is 4 000
coefficients and 14 s, which is tolerable interactively and painful in a
bootstrap. 8 000 coefficients is 2 minutes and 2.1 GB, and the growth is
in a dense matrix, so the next doubling is a memory failure rather than
a slow call. A 500-subject factor-smooth at k = 10 (5 000 coefficients)
is already in that band.

**Shipping without the `frm_lp_basis()` seam is acceptable.** The seam
would remove the design rebuild, which is the term that is already
negligible; it would not touch `sp_joint_cov()`, which is the term that
scales. The seam is worth doing for correctness and reach (it is the
only way `nl` and `rr` at `re.form = NULL` are ever served), not for
cost. What must come first is not the seam but the two lines below.

**Punch list, item 1.**

* `R/curve-cov.R:192` - `Q <- as.matrix(Q)` densifies an 8013 x 8013
  sparse matrix to take a 20 x 20 block out of it. Keep `Q` sparse and
  use `Matrix::solve()` for the `rest` block. This is the whole scaling
  problem and it is one line.
* `R/curve.R:43-50` (`@section Cost:`) and `man/frm_curve.Rd:60-70` -
  the section quantifies `predict()` calls and says nothing about
  `sdreport` or the dense solve, which together are 75 % of the cost at
  the size the section uses as its example and 99 % of it at 8 000
  coefficients. A reader sizing a job from this section will be wrong by
  two orders of magnitude. State the joint-precision cost and its
  growth.
* `dev/spline-seam-proposal.md:88-94` - same misattribution ("11
  `predict()` calls in about 0.04 s plus `sdreport` in a further
  0.04 s"): measured here, `sdreport` is 0.21 s against the design's
  0.03 s on that model, a 7x ratio in the opposite direction to the one
  the sentence implies.
* `R/curve.R:49` - "a 20-subject factor-smooth model with 110 random
  coefficients costs 32 calls" is not exercised by any test, and no
  `bs = "fs"` model appears anywhere in `tests/`. The nearest test
  (`tests/testthat/test-curve.R:44-62`) is `s(x, k = 8) + (1 | g)`. My
  own 20-subject `fs` fit gives 120 random coefficients and 7 calls,

## Item 2. Right censoring

### How the censored term is computed today

Core, `R/objective.R:100`:

```r
if (length(i_r)) ll[i_r] <- log(bound_rows(Fub, i_r) - Fv[i_r])
```

Without `trunc()`, `Fub = 1`, so the right-censored contribution is
`log(1 - F(y))` formed on the **probability** scale. `Fv` is whatever
the family's `lcdf` returns; `royston_parmar()`'s returns
`sp_squeeze(-expm1(-exp(eta)))` (`R/royston-parmar.R:190-198`). There is
no `expm1` form and no log-scale route: `log S` is never computed as
`-H`, which is what it is in closed form on the hazard scale.

`sp_squeeze()` (`R/rp-basis.R:110-113`) holds `F` at most
`1 - 5.5511e-16`, so **`log S` is floored at -35.127363** whatever the
model says.

### Where accuracy is lost (measured)

Hazard scale, true `log S = -H`:

| H | true `log S` | computed | abs error | `F == 1` exactly |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|:--|
| 10 | -10 | -10.000000 | 1.3e-13 | no |
| 19.2 | -19.2 | | **1e-8** | no |
| 25 | -25 | -25.000004 | 3.8e-06 | no |
| 28.5 | -28.5 | | **1e-4** | no |
| 30 | -30 | -29.999834 | 1.7e-04 | no |
| 36.0 | -36 | -34.945041 | **1.06** | no |
| 40 | -40 | -35.127363 | 4.87 | **yes** |
| 100 | -100 | -35.127363 | 64.87 | **yes** |

The **gradient** fails before the value does. `d log(1-F)/d eta` against
the truth `-H`: exact at H = 10; -20.01 vs -20 at H = 20; and **exactly
0** at H = 30 and above. On flexsurv's `bc` at an inflated slope, the
objective's own gradient in `gamma1` is 10.8 % of the true gradient at
`gamma1 = 10`, 0.95 % at 20, and 0.00 % at 30 and 40. The objective goes
flat exactly where it should be pushing back hardest.

The **normal scale is far worse**, because its tail decays like
`exp(-eta^2/2)` rather than `exp(-e^eta)`: at `eta = 10` the true
`log S` is -53.23 and the computed value is -35.13, an error of 18.1.
The floor is reached at `eta ~ 6` rather than `H ~ 36`.

The lane's own count reproduces: on `bc`, `gamma1 = 30` puts **573 of
686** rows at `F == 1` exactly (355 of 387 censored rows). The lane says
572; the difference is the covariate model.

### The flexsurv identity test does not cover the region

Measured on `bc` at flexsurv's own fitted coefficients (k = 1, hazard):
the largest cumulative hazard on any of the 387 censored rows is
**H = 2.0123**. The first H at which the error in `log S` reaches 1e-8
is 19.2. **Every censored row in the identity test sits an order of
magnitude below the first inaccurate H**, which is why the test returns
1e-16 and why it cannot see this defect. Reproduced independently: at
flexsurv's optimum my exact reference and frmtmb's objective agree to
0.0e+00; at `gamma1 = 5`, with only 55 of 387 censored rows floored,
they already differ by **973 log-likelihood units (11.4 % relative)**.

### Whether a fitted optimum lands there

Mostly no, and once, decisively, yes.

Four constructed datasets, 50-76 % right censoring, censoring times up
to 185x the largest event time, `df = 3` hazard scale. In every one the
largest H on a censored row **at the optimum** was 0.60-0.72, and:

* where flexsurv could fit, frmtmb matched it to `+0.000000` in log
  likelihood and 1.4e-05 in coefficients;
* where flexsurv could **not** fit (`initial value in 'vmmin' is not
  finite`), frmtmb converged and its optimum agreed with the exact
  likelihood to 1e-10 and 4e-12 in coefficients.

The reason is structural and is a point in the family's favour: a
right-censored row contributes `-H`, so the MLE is strongly repelled
from large H, and an RP spline flattens its tail to accommodate a long
survivor. The bad region is transient, on the optimizer's path, not a
place optima live.

**Except when other rows can outvote the penalty.** 600 subjects, a
strong group effect (group A scale 0.30, group B scale 3.0), every
subject observed except **one group-A subject censored at t = 50**:

```
frmtmb converged.
  mu.(Intercept)      2.00219      gamma1.(Intercept)   1.83633
  mu.grpB            -4.68372      gamma2.(Intercept)  -0.04117
  reported logLik  -365.5803       gamma3.(Intercept)   0.05275
  reported AIC      741.1607

  H(t = 50) on the censored row: 21 690
  true log S there: -21 690        scored as: -35.1274
  EXACT log likelihood at those coefficients: -22023.06
```

The reported log likelihood is wrong by **2.166e+04 units**, and the AIC
of 741.16 looks entirely ordinary. Maximizing the exact likelihood from
the same start:

| coefficient | frmtmb | exact MLE | difference |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `mu.(Intercept)` | 2.00219 | 2.13003 | -0.128 |
| **`mu.grpB`** | **-4.68372** | **-2.72074** | **-1.963** |
| `gamma1` | 1.83633 | 1.85757 | -0.021 |
| `gamma2` | -0.04117 | -0.05740 | +0.016 |
| `gamma3` | 0.05275 | 0.14506 | -0.092 |

The exact likelihood is **2.148e+04 higher** at its own optimum. The
headline treatment coefficient is out by 72 %. `S(50)` in group A is
reported as 0 where the exact MLE gives 8.3e-30. Nothing warns.

This is the floor's zero gradient doing exactly what the gradient table
predicts: past H = 30 the row's contribution is a constant, so the
optimizer prices that subject at -35.13 no matter what it does, and
fits the other 599 rows as if the survivor were free.

flexsurv on the same data **refuses** (`initial value in 'vmmin' is not
finite`), at its own inits and at the exact MLE's. That is the contrast
that matters: the reference implementation declines to answer, and this
family answers wrongly and silently.

### A second floor, found on the way: monotonicity

The same probe found a separate silent failure with the same shape. On
a 60 % cure-fraction dataset the fit converges with **6 event rows whose
fitted `d(eta)/d(log t)` is negative**, so no hazard exists there and a
true log density is `-Inf`. `sp_floor_pos()` replaces each with about
-32, and:

```
  logLik(fit) reported: -665.1365      AIC reported: 1338.2731
  exact log likelihood at those coefficients: -4617.4882
  rows whose density is a floor artifact: 6 of 600
```

`R/royston-parmar.R:58-74` documents that monotonicity is not enforced
and says the floor "is inert wherever the model is sane", which is true
and is not the case here: where the model is *not* sane the floor is
load-bearing, and the reported `logLik` and `AIC` are of a floored
pseudo-likelihood rather than of any density. The docs tell the user to
check with `frm_curve_deriv()`; nothing makes them.

### Classification: BLOCKS

Not because the arithmetic is wrong in the ordinary case - it is right,
and I could not break it with heavy censoring alone - but because there
exists a converged, warning-free fit whose reported `logLik` is wrong by
2.2e+04 and whose headline coefficient is wrong by 72 %, and the package
ships a compat row (`royston_parmar x cens()` = `works`,
`R/zzz.R:47-48`) whose evidence is a test that cannot reach the failing
region.

**Punch list, item 2.**

* `R/rp-basis.R:92-113` / `R/royston-parmar.R:190-198` - the squeeze is
  the right workaround for `-Inf` and the wrong one for accuracy. The
  proposal's `lccdf` slot is the correct fix and should land in core
  before this family claims `cens() = works`.
* Until it does, the family must **refuse or warn** when any censored
  row's fitted `eta` implies `H` past the accurate region. The threshold
  is measured and specific: `H > 19.2` on the hazard and odds scales,
  `eta > 6` on the normal scale. This is parameter dependent so it
  cannot go in `family_finalize()`, but it is a natural post-fit check
  and `royston_parmar()` already owns a `post` slot.
* The same post-fit check should report any row with non-positive
  `d(eta)/d(log t)`, for the monotonicity floor above.
* `R/zzz.R:47-48` - the `cens()` row should be `conditional`, not
  `works`, with the region named, until `lccdf` exists.
* `tests/testthat/test-royston-parmar.R:28-52` - the identity test
  should assert what it covers: add the measured `max H = 2.01` on
  `bc`'s censored rows, so the next reader knows the test's reach.
* `R/royston-parmar.R:76-82` (`@section Censoring and truncation:`) and
  `man/royston_parmar.Rd` - document the accuracy limit on `log S`.

## Item 3. The `sign()`-selected positive part

`sign()` **is** tape-safe in RTMB. A tape over `sum(sign(u) * u)` at
`u = (-2, 0.5)` builds and returns 2.5 exactly, so `sign()` on an
advector is neither refused nor silently demoted (unlike `pmax()`,
which RTMB refuses outright).

`sp_floor_pos()` on the tape, values and derivatives:

| u | value | taped d/du | central-difference d/du |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| -35.75 | 6.993006993007e-17 | 1.9560858722e-18 | 1.956079222e-18 |
| -1 | 2.5e-15 | 2.5e-15 | 2.5e-15 |
| **0** | **5e-08** | **0.5** | **0.5** |
| 1 | 1 | 1 | 1.0000000 |
| 3 | 3 | 1 | 1.0000001 |

* **At `u = 0`** the value is `5e-08`. The taped derivative is `0.5`,
  matching the numeric one, so `sign()`'s zero derivative at the origin
  does not leak into the result and the function is differentiable
  through it. Values either side (`+-1e-12`, `+-1e-9`, `+-1e-6`) walk
  through `5e-08` smoothly, so it is continuous as claimed.
* **At `u = -35.75`** the value is `6.993e-17`, matching the documented
  `7e-17`, and `log()` of it is `-37.199`, finite. The naive
  `0.5 * (u + sqrt(u^2 + eps2))` at the same point is **exactly 0** and
  its log is `-Inf`. Both the defect and its fix reproduce precisely.

**One doc correction, and I made it.** `R/rp-basis.R:49` said the two
branches "agree at `u = 0`, where both are `sqrt(eps2)`". They agree,
but at `sqrt(eps2) / 2`: `sqrt(1e-14) = 1e-07` and the function returns
`5e-08`. Both branches evaluate to `5e-08` there (positive branch
`0.5 * w`, negative branch `0.5 * eps2 / w`, `w = 1e-07`), so the
continuity argument stands and only the constant was wrong. Edited to
`sqrt(eps2) / 2`.

**"Bit for bit" is too strong.** The per-element function *is*
bit-identical between the taped and numeric paths: over a 7-point probe,
`identical(taped, numeric)` is `TRUE`. Over the 686 rows of the vignette
data the two sums differ in the last 1-2 ulp (1590.315511268185 vs
1590.3155112681861, 7e-16 relative), at all three parameter points
tried, including the one where all 686 rows have a negative derivative.
The difference is the tape's accumulation order against R's `sum()`, not
`sp_floor_pos()`. Substance holds; the phrase should say "to the last
ulp" rather than "bit for bit".

### The workflow claim is loose

"differs from the ddm one in its header comment only" is not what the
diff says. `diff check-frmtmb-ddm.yaml check-frmtmb-spline.yaml` returns
eight hunks: the header comment, and then `frmtmb.ddm` -> `frmtmb.spline`
in the two path filters, the workflow `name:`, the job id, the
`read.dcf()` path and `working-directory`. Every one of those is a
mechanical substitution that *must* differ, so the claim is right in

## Item 9. Reading the seam proposal

### Is `frm_lp_basis()` the right shape?

**Yes, and the proposal's own test for it is the right test.** The
returned list is `list(eta, A, coef_pos, V, coef_names, extra_var)`, and
every element already exists inside `predict(se.fit = TRUE)`:
`lp_delta_A()` produces `A` and `coef_pos`, `get_joint_cov()` produces
`V`, `lp_extra_var()` produces `extra_var`. I confirmed the shape by
reading the consumer: `R/predict.R:1235` is

```r
var_eta <- pmax(rowSums((A %*% V) * A), 0)
```

which is exactly `diag(A V A')`, so `predict(se.fit = TRUE)` really does
become a two-line consumer of the proposed return value. Separating
`extra_var` rather than folding it into `A V A'` is right for the stated
reason: an exact `gp()` kriging variance is not coefficient uncertainty.

Two corrections to the proposal's account of core.

* The `rowSums()` line is at **`R/predict.R:1235`**, not 1019. Line 1019
  is the head of `predict.frmtmb_fit`. The other four citations I
  checked are accurate (`methods-fit.R:377` is `vcov.frmtmb_fit`,
  `predict.R:12` is `get_joint_cov`, `autoscale.R:137` is
  `autoscale_sdreport`, `predict.R:1558` is the nl `se.fit` refusal).
* The proposal quotes only the **response-scale** nl refusal
  (`predict.R:1558`). There is a second, link-scale one at
  `R/predict.R:1179` ("se.fit is not supported for the nonlinear
  predictor yet"). That is the one a nonlinear `frm_lp_basis()` would
  have to lift, and it is the one worth naming.

**What the proposal misses entirely, and it matters for item 1.** Core
already caches both halves:

```r
sdr_of(fit)        # R/fit.R:1209-1217 - caches fit$cache$sdr
get_joint_cov(fit) # R/predict.R:12    - caches fit$cache$Vjoint
```

`sp_joint_cov()` calls `RTMB::sdreport(fit$obj, getJointPrecision =
TRUE)` **fresh on every call**, duplicating an object the same fit has
already cached (the `predict(se.fit = TRUE)` check inside
`sp_curve_parts()` populates it). So the extension pays full price twice
per call for something core computes once. That is a large part of the
scaling wall in item 1, and it is invisible in the proposal's cost
paragraph.

It is also a correctness hazard: `sdr_of()` goes through
`autoscale_sdreport()`, which reparameterizes when `fit$par_units` is
set; the extension's raw `RTMB::sdreport()` does not. `R/curve-cov.R:153`
acknowledges this ("what frmtmb's own `autoscale_sdreport()` calls
**when the fit carries no autoscaling**") and relies on the covariance
check to refuse otherwise. That is a defensible design, but it means
`frm_curve()` is expected to refuse on any `control = frm_control(
autoscale = TRUE)` fit, and **no test and no compat row says so**.
`autoscale` should be a compat row with status `refused` or
`conditional`, or the function should read the cached sdreport and
simply work.

### Is `lccdf` the right fix for item 2?

**Yes, and it is necessary rather than merely nice.** Item 2's evidence
is that the workaround has a failure mode the workaround cannot reach:
`sp_squeeze()` keeps the fit alive, but it floors `log S` at -35.13 and
zeroes the gradient past H = 30, and I found a converged optimum whose
headline coefficient is wrong by 72 % because of it. A log-scale slot is
the only fix that removes the class, because the quantity core needs is
`log S` and the family currently has to round-trip it through a
probability whose complement is not representable.

Two things the proposal gets slightly wrong about it.

* "This is a few lines in one function." The dispatch is, but
  `fam_lcdf()` (`R/families.R:3898-3903`) already carries an arity
  shim for categorical's four-argument form, so `lccdf` needs the same
  treatment, plus a `custom_family()` argument, its validation, its
  `frmtmb_ad_overload()` wrapping at `R/families.R:259`, and the
  `R/frame.R:1249` guard that currently refuses `cens()` when
  `lcdf` is absent must learn that `lccdf` alone is also sufficient.
  Still small, but it is five sites, not one.
* "Left censoring keeps `log(Fv - Flb)`; ... no family here needs it."
  True for censoring. **Not true for truncation**: `R/objective.R:110`
  applies `ll - log(Fub - Flb)`, and for a left-truncated survival model
  (delayed entry, which is routine) `Flb = F(entry time)` runs into the
  identical representability problem from the other side. The proposal
  should say that `lccdf` fixes right censoring and leaves left
  truncation on the old footing, rather than implying the class is
  closed.

### Is `ps()` implementable inside the existing nl-body evaluation?

**Yes for the evaluation, no for the parse.** The body is evaluated at
`R/objective.R:292-294`:

```r
ev <- c(dparv[[lp$resp]][c(lp$nl_pars, lp$nl_dpar_refs)], lp$data_list)
eta <- tryCatch(eval(lp$nl_body, ev, ad_overload_env(lp$nl_env, lp$nl_body)),
                error = function(e) nl_body_error(e, lp))
```

That is a plain `eval()` with advectors in `ev`. A `ps` closure injected
into `ev` per objective call would be found by ordinary R lookup, would
receive its argument already evaluated as an advector, and could return
an advector. **No second evaluation path is needed**, and the
proposal's measurements (branch-free divided differences, 4.64e-14 with
`x` as an AD parameter) say the basis itself tapes.

But the proposal's own table puts the closure in `nl_env`, and that is
wrong for core as it stands. `nl_env` is fixed at parse time
(`R/parse.R:1357`, `environment(nf) %||% env`), before any coefficient
exists, so a closure living there cannot see the current draw of the
block. It has to go in `ev`, which is rebuilt per call. One-word change
to the table, but it is the difference between a design that works and
one that captures a stale environment.

The harder gap is upstream. `nl_dpar()` (`R/parse.R:1246-1252`) collects
everything it knows about a body from one line:

```r
vars <- all.vars(body)
```

and derives `nl_pars` and `datavars` from it. It never inspects call
structure. So core has **no place today that would notice a `ps()` call
at all**, let alone allocate its knot vector, its null-space columns and
its `theta`. That is a new parse pass over the body, and the proposal's
cost paragraph ("a new term in the parser, a frozen basis on the frame,
a coefficient map, a tape closure, and a nonlinear branch in the delta
method") does list it - but the frame table above presents `ps()` as
slotting into existing machinery, which understates the parser work.

### The parser defect: reproduced exactly

```
all.vars(quote(f(x)(y)))            -> y            (x gone)
all.vars(quote(a * curry(tv)(zv)))  -> a zv         (tv gone)
all.names(quote(f(x)(y)))           -> f x y

frm(bf(y ~ a * curry2(tv, zv), a ~ 1, nl = TRUE))     -> fitted, a = 2.0060
frm(bf(y ~ a * curry(tv)(zv),  a ~ 1, nl = TRUE))     -> Error: object 'tv' not found
frm(bf(y ~ a * curry(tv)(zv) + 0 * tv, a ~ 1, nl=TRUE)) -> fitted, a = 2.0060
```

Identical model, identical coefficient to four decimals, and the only
difference is whether `all.vars()` can see `tv`. The proposed
`body_vars()` walker returns `c("a", "tv", "zv")` and agrees with
`all.vars()` on every ordinary body I tried (`a * x + b`,
`a * exp(-b * t)`, `ult * (1 - exp(-exp(lrc) * x))`). The defect site is
**`R/parse.R:1247`**, one line, and the proposal's diagnosis and fix are
both correct. This is a core bug independent of anything in this
package and is worth filing on its own.

## (c) Reading `fit$cache$Vjoint`

### Is it a sanctioned seam?

**No. It is the same kind of internal reach the brief forbade with
`:::`, and it passes a `grep ":::"` only because `$` on an environment
is not `:::`.** `get_joint_cov()` is `@noRd` (`R/predict.R:12`); the
name `Vjoint`, its `list(V =, names =)` shape, and the meaning of
`names` are all private to that one function's memo. Nothing in
`frmtmb-extension-api` documents `fit$cache`, and unlike `fit$obj` and
`fit$estimates` - which the proposal defends by pointing at
`frmtmb.sample` and the extension-api example - there is no precedent
for an extension reading the cache.

**It is nonetheless the right trade, and strictly better than what it
replaced.** The old code called `RTMB::sdreport()` directly: a public
RTMB API, but it bypassed `autoscale_sdreport()` and was therefore
*wrong* on an autoscaled fit. The package swapped a public-but-wrong
route for a private-but-correct one, on a call that verifies itself
against `predict(se.fit = TRUE)` every time. Given that check, the
failure modes of the reach are bounded: if core renames `Vjoint` or
changes its shape, `is.null(jc)` is false-y and the code silently takes
the sparse fallback - correct, 16x slower. If core changed the meaning
of `V` without renaming, the covariance check refuses. **Neither failure
mode is "wrong answer".** That is a well-chosen dependency, and it
should still be legitimized rather than left as a reach.

### Does it exist on every fit?

**No - it is ABSENT on every fresh fit.** It is a memo, written the
first time anything calls `get_joint_cov()`. Measured, `Vjoint` before
any `predict()` and after `frm_curve()`:

| fit | before | after | `cov_rel_error` | n_predict |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|
| ML + random effects | ABSENT | present | 1.22e-15 | 31 |
| **REML** | ABSENT | present | **1.55e-15** | 31 |
| **autoscale** | ABSENT | present | **1.22e-15** | 31 |
| **profile = TRUE** | ABSENT | present | **1.11e-15** | 31 |
| **no random-effect block** (`y ~ x`) | ABSENT | present | **2.22e-16** | 3 |

The extension does not depend on the cache being warm: it warms it
itself, by ordering the `predict(se.fit = TRUE)` check *before*
`sp_joint_cov()` (`R/curve-cov.R:255`, with a comment saying exactly
that). That ordering is load-bearing and is now the only thing keeping
the fallback off the hot path.

Three of those rows are new capability, not just new speed. **REML,
`profile` and autoscale were `untested` or refused before; all three now
work at machine precision.** A fit with no random-effect block
previously *errored* ("this fit reports no joint precision matrix") and
now works at 2.22e-16 - `get_joint_cov()` falls back to
`sdr_of(fit)$cov.fixed`, whose `names` are `beta, betad` rather than
joint-precision row names, and `sp_coef_pos()` handles both shapes.

Mapped `b` I could not reach: `frmtmb_control()` takes no `map`
argument, so there is no supported route to a mapped random-effect
block. The nearest analogue, a `betad` entry held fixed
(`bf(y ~ s(x, k = 8), sigma = 0.5)`), works at 3.22e-15.

### What happens when it is absent

The sparse fallback: a fresh `RTMB::sdreport(getJointPrecision = TRUE)`,
Schur complement kept sparse, `Matrix::solve()`. Correct, and about 16x
slower at 8000 coefficients.

**One latent trap.** That fallback is the *old* route, so it still
bypasses `autoscale_sdreport()` and would give an autoscaled fit the
unscaled covariance. It is unreachable today because the `predict()`
check always fills the cache first, and the covariance check would
refuse the result anyway. But it is a correctness bug sitting behind an
ordering constraint, and the ordering is enforced only by a comment.

### Should the proposal ask for an accessor instead of `frm_lp_basis()`?

**It should ask for both, and it currently asks for neither of the
first.** The updated proposal documents the cache read and defends it
(lines 58-88) but its formal ask is still only `frm_lp_basis()`.

They are different asks and the accessor is now the better-value one:

* An exported joint-covariance accessor (export `get_joint_cov()`, or
  `frm_joint_cov(fit)` returning `list(V, names)`) legitimizes the reach
  the extension already makes, is a five-line change, and removes the
  only unsanctioned internal dependency in the package.
* `frm_lp_basis()` additionally supplies `A` and `coef_pos`, which the
  extension still rebuilds by 351 `predict()` calls at 8006
  coefficients - **0.28 s of a 5.91 s call**. It remains worth exporting
  for REACH (`nl`, `rr` at `re.form = NULL`), which the proposal now
  says correctly, but its cost argument is gone.

So: add the accessor as Part 1a, keep `frm_lp_basis()` as Part 1b.

## Residual items

None of these blocks a 0.1.0 merge. In rough order of importance.

* **R1. `logLik()` and `AIC()` still report the floored value silently.**
  `royston-parmar.R` cannot gate them: `logLik()` reads
  `object$opt$objective` (`R/methods-fit.R:233-240`) and the family
  protocol has no hook that runs when a fit finishes. My 600-subject fit
  still reports `logLik = -365.5803` against a true `-22023`. A user who
  fits and reads `AIC()` without ever calling `rp_floored()` or
  `frm_curve()` still gets a wrong number with no signal. The package
  documents this precisely in three places
  (`R/rp-check.R:132-140`, the `cens()` compat row, the vignette) and
  refuses everywhere it can, which is the whole of what an extension can
  do. **The fix is core's**: either the `lccdf` slot, or a post-fit
  family hook. This is the one thing that keeps the package at
  "documented limit" rather than "correct".
* **R2. `R/curve-cov.R:186-202`** - the sparse fallback still bypasses
  `autoscale_sdreport()`, so it would hand an autoscaled fit an unscaled
  covariance. Unreachable today only because the `predict()` check at
  `R/curve-cov.R:255` runs first. Either route the fallback through
  `autoscale_sdreport(fit, jp = TRUE)`, or make the ordering an
  assertion rather than a comment.
* **R3. `R/curve-cov.R:180`** - `fit$cache$Vjoint` is an undocumented
  internal read (see (c)). Ask core to export an accessor; until then
  the reach is defensible but should be named as a reach in the
  package's own docs, not only in `dev/`.
* **R4. `dev/spline-seam-proposal.md`** - add the joint-covariance
  accessor as an explicit Part 1a ask. Right now the document explains
  why the extension reaches into the cache and then does not ask for the
  thing that would stop it.
* **R5. `R/zzz.R:49` and `R/royston-parmar.R:114-117`** - both quote "a
  reported log likelihood wrong by 2.4e+03". That is the worker's seed;
  mine gives 2.166e+04 on the same design. Both are true and neither is
  wrong, but a single seed-specific figure reads as a property of the
  construction. Say "by thousands to tens of thousands, depending on how
