# Review: the 0.68.0 release tree's consolidation edits (2026-10-06)

Scope: what the integration did on top of lanes fixes, gpby, ordmix and
vigport in `../frmtmb-wt-release` (base `9e902909`, uncommitted),
installed in `rellib-r6`. The lanes' own content was not re-reviewed.

To isolate the consolidation, `dev/relrev-recon.sh` rebuilds the
textual merge of the four lanes onto `9e902909` in the order the
integration used (vigport, fixes, gpby, ordmix; `git merge-file` file by
file, generated files left out) and diffs the release tree against it
(`dev/relrev-log/recon-diff.txt`). 38 files differ. In `R/`, the
difference is the 3 resolved `R/priors.R` hunks, the compat cells,
`ord_internal_labels()`, `ord_thres_linpred()`, the `cs()` lift
(`R/frame.R`, `R/families.R`, `R/compat.R`, `R/fit.R`), the
`conditional_effects()` median, `gp_krig_factor_dense()` and the
`?refit` text. Nothing else differs. Every script and log named below
is `dev/relrev-*` and `dev/relrev-log/`.

## Verdict: MERGEABLE

There are no blocking findings. Fix m1 and m2 before the commit: each
is a one-line text correction in shipped code or comments, and neither
changes behavior. m3 to m12 can go to the backlog or a later round.

## What holds, with numbers

### 1. Merge resolutions (`R/priors.R`, compat, labels)

- **Per-threshold rows equal brms 2.23.0's `default_prior()`.** I ran
  brms myself (`relrev-mix.R`, `relrev-mix2.R`). The Intercept and
  delta rows are the same on 12 mixture shapes: order `"none"` with
  (cumulative, sratio), (cumulative, cumulative), (sratio, acat), three
  components, an equidistant component and hurdle x hurdle; order
  `"mu"` with (cumulative, sratio), (sratio, acat) and a sum-to-zero
  component; `thres(gr = g)` under both orders (18 and 9 rows); and an
  `mvbf()` of two cumulative responses (7 rows). There is one
  difference, a `sum_to_zero` component under order `"none"`, where
  brms lists 4 `mu1` rows and frmtmb lists none. This is frmtmb's
  existing rule for a plain `sum_to_zero` family: class `"Intercept"`
  is refused there, as the compat row says. It is not a merge defect.
- **Early return and refusals.** A coef prior is refused, with a
  message, where brms has no parameter for it: on an equidistant
  component, on a sum-to-zero component, coef 4 of 3, coef with no
  dpar on an order-`"none"` mixture, `dpar = "mu1"` under order
  `"mu"`, and `dpar = "mu3"`. brms refuses the same three rows I
  checked it on.
- **The log prior density against hand computations on shapes the
  tests do not use.** On `mixture(sratio(), cumulative())`, order
  `"none"`, I set coef 1 on mu2, coef 3 on mu1 and a class
  `student_t` on mu1. brms's centering applies to each component, and
  the ordered map's Jacobian applies to the cumulative component only.
  The result is frmtmb -9.47211734828091 against the hand computation
  -9.47211734828091 (relative 1.9e-16). The uncentered hand value
  misses by 0.164, so the check can fail. On order `"mu"` (sratio,
  acat), relative error is 0. On an `mvbf()` coef 2 with `resp = "y2"`,
  relative error is 0. On a mixture with `thres(gr = g)`, coef 3 of
  group b on mu1, uncentered, relative error is 2.6e-16.
- **`confint()` and `vcov(full = TRUE)` names.** I checked 14 fitted
  mixture shapes (`relrev-mix.R` (d), `relrev-mix3.R`): order
  `"none"`, `"mu"`, equidistant (cumulative and acat), sum-to-zero
  under both orders, modeled `disc1`, hurdle x hurdle, and a `cs()`
  component. On every shape the rows are unique and equal to
  `vcov(full = TRUE)`'s rows. Every row resolves through
  `confint(parm = )` to itself (all rows ok on all 14 shapes). The
  shared vector is labeled once, as `Intercept[k]`.
- **Compat cells.** `disc` and `sum_to_zero` x `mixture` are "works",
  and `equidistant` is "conditional". Each "works" case fits in
  `test-compat.R`, and brms's refusal text for equidistant under order
  `"mu"` is asserted.
- **The mutant evidence is the claimed resolutions undone.** I diffed
  `dev/rel068-mut/naive` against `h3fix`, and each arm against the
  release `R/`:

    return after the block lookup, rows without `dpar`, a count read

  - `h3mix` differs only in the missing `coef_k`.

### 2. `cs()` on `cumulative()`

- **Not a silent wrong answer on 0.67.0.** "Reads the offsets" in the
  record means the `cs()` offsets (`dpars$.cs`), not `offset()`. On
  rellib-r5, 13 spellings of `cs()` on `cumulative()` are refused
  before any density is built (`relrev-cs.R r5`): bare, `bf()`, the
  family as a string, `+ cumulative()`, probit with a predictor,
  equidistant, sum_to_zero, a random intercept, `thres(gr = )`,
  `mvbf()`, hurdle, mixture, and gaussian. The `(cs(1) | g)` spelling
  errors on both builds (m10). 0.67.0 had no ordinal mixture. So
  `fam_cumulative()`'s ignored `.cs` was unreachable, and no bug-fix
  bullet or failing-on-r5 test is needed.
- **Plain ordinal fits are bitwise unchanged.** `relrev-bitwise.R` is
  the ordmix reviewer's 44-model driver plus 6 models with `offset()`
  (cumulative logit, probit with `disc`, equidistant, with a random
  effect, sratio and hurdle) and one-step residuals on 9 models. It
  ran on rellib-r5 and rellib-r6: **529 of 559 outputs are identical
  over 50 models.** All 30 differences are `default_prior()`, and each
  is exactly the added per-threshold class `"Intercept"` rows (3, 4
  or 6 added, 0 lost; `relrev-bitwise-prior.R`), which is lane fixes'
  intended change. `opt$par`, `logLik`, `obj$fn` and `obj$gr` at a
  perturbed point, `fitted()` in sample and on newdata, `simulate()`,
  `fixef()`, `variables()` and `print()` are identical. The one-step
  residuals are identical on all 8 models that have them, including
  the 2 with offsets. hurdle_cumulative refuses OSA by design. The
  one-step branch is `fam_cumulative()`'s `osa_unwrap(y)` re-tape for
  `residuals(type = "osa")`, and its `tau[k] - 0 - eta` is bitwise
  `tau[k] - eta` there.
- **brms's warning.** Counts from `relrev-cs.R`, against brms's
  `stancode()`:

  | shape | brms | frmtmb |
  |---|---|---|
  | `cs(x)` | 1 | 1 |
  | `cs(x) + cs(z)` | 1 | 1 |
  | `cs(x + z)` | 1 | 1 |
  | three `cs()` terms | 1 | 1 |
  | `mvbf()` of two cumulative responses | 2 | 2 |
  | `mixture(cum, cum)` | 2 | 2 |
  | sratio and plain cumulative (controls) | 0 | 0 |

  brms's `check_cs()` warns once per linear predictor, not once per
  `cs()` term, and frmtmb matches. The refusal of a non-ordinal family
  is in brms's words (`brmsterms.R:1105`), for population-level `cs()`
  and for `(cs(x) | g)` on `poisson()`.
- **Log density.** `relrev-lpcheck.R` is `rel068-cs-lpcheck.R` with a
  scratch copy of the Stan cache, and it compiled nothing. I reran all
  six cases. At the optimum the result is 0.0 ulp on all six. At the
  perturbed points it is at most 0.9 ulp. brms's gradient at frmtmb's
  optimum is 1.26e-4 to 3.75e-4, and 1.15 for the falsely converged
  mixture. The counts are **18 finite and 6 NaN** of 24 points, not 20
  and 4 (m1).
- **Crossing rows** (`relrev-cs.R` (d), `relrev-cs2.R`). On a newdata
  grid of 7 rows, 2 cross at the estimate:
  - `fitted()` is NaN on exactly those 2 rows, for all four columns.
    The non-crossing rows are finite and sum to 1.
  - `simulate(nsim = 200)` returns a `data.frame` of doubles, as for a
    plain cumulative fit, with NA on exactly the 2 rows (200 of 200)
    and 0 elsewhere. In sample there are 0 crossing rows and 0 NA.
  - In frmtmb.sample, `posterior_epred()` is NaN and
    `posterior_predict()` is NA (type double) on the same draws: 196
    and 139 of 200 on the two crossing rows, 4 on a near row, 0
    elsewhere. brms draws from another distribution there (brms-22).
  - `predict()` is the exception, m3.

### 3. `conditional_effects(method = "predict")` median

`relrev-ce.R` ran on r5 and r6 with `ndraws = 1001`. The independent
check is the median of `simulate(newdata = grid, nsim = 20001)` at the
point estimate. The display runs, and `lower__ <= estimate__ <=
upper__`, on gaussian, lognormal, weibull, poisson, negbinomial,
binomial with `trials()`, bernoulli, `trunc()`, `cens()`,
hurdle_poisson, zero_inflated_poisson, a gaussian mixture, `mvbf()`
(`resp = "yp"`), `mi()` and a random-effect Poisson.

- **Discrete families** give whole counts. Poisson, binomial,
  bernoulli, hurdle, zero-inflated and RE Poisson are identical to the
  independent median. negbinomial is within 1 count.
- **Hurdle and zero-inflated** give 0 at every grid point, a point
  mass of 0.7 and 0.6.
- **Continuous families** are within Monte Carlo error: gaussian 0.037
  (sd 1.05), trunc 0.039, cens 0.035, weibull 0.035, lognormal 0.083,
  and the bimodal mixture 0.23 (sd 2.24).
- **The median uses the band's sims.** It is from the same `sims`
  matrix and the same `stats::quantile()` type 7 as the band
  (`R/conditional-effects.R:2530`). `band != "wald"` with `"predict"`
  is refused before the grid is built, so the band cannot come from
  other draws.
- **Ordinal and categorical** are refused, as on r5.
- **frmtmb.sample** changed only in a roxygen paragraph
  (recon-diff), so its draws path is unchanged.
- **NEWS.** The BREAKING bullet is accurate, and its numbers cite
  `test-ce-predict-median.R`.

### 4. `gp_krig_factor()` dense fallback

`relrev-krig.R` uses a 400-position grid, with the lengthscale bisected
so that the lane's pivoted-only factor has rank 99, 100, 101 and 102
(n/4 = 100):

| rank | route | same as lane | law error, release / lane |
|---|---|---|---|
| 99 | pivoted | bitwise | 8.1e-13 / 8.1e-13 |
| 100 | pivoted | bitwise | 8.1e-13 / 8.1e-13 |
| 101 | dense | | 9.1e-13 / 9.1e-13 |
| 102 | dense | | 9.8e-13 / 9.8e-13 |

The law error is a fraction of sd^2. So rank exactly n/4 stays on the
lane's route, and the law does not move across the switch. Two calls
give `identical()` factors, and two seeded `gp_krig_draw()` calls give
identical draws. The route depends only on the inputs. Grids of 1 to
5 positions keep the law to 1.9e-16.

### 5. The runner's global `f`: a test defect, not a package defect

`relrev-item5.R` shows that frmtmb's newdata path reads a missing
column from the formula environment and its parents for every
predictor-side variable:

- a linear predictor (`z`: 92.1 with a global `z = 100`, refused
  without one);
- an `offset()`;
- a random-effect grouping factor `g`;
- `gp(by = f)`. A global `f` equal to level `b` gives exactly the
  linear predictor of `newdata$f = "b"`.

At fit time, a global `f`, `g` or `w` is also used. brms 2.23.0
refuses every one of these, at fit time and on newdata ("can neither
be found in 'data' nor in 'data2'").

The 2026-09-30 user decision (`dev/round-handoff.md`, NEWS 0.67.0)
restricts addition terms to data columns and explicitly keeps R's rule
for predictor formulas and offsets. `gp(by = )` is predictor-side, so
the package behaves as decided and consistently with every other
predictor. The defect is the test: `test-gp-by.R` asserts a refusal
whose outcome depends on the global environment. I confirmed it with
`relrev-run1.R`, `relrev-tests.sh`. From a clean global environment
the file passes 82 of 82. With a global `f` set to the file's path, it
reads pass 81, err 1 ("New factor levels ... Levels found:
'C:/.../test-gp-by.R'"). `dev/release/run-tests.R` and
`dev/warnleak-scan.R` in `local()` are the right fix for the runner.
For the test, see m6.

### 6. frmtmb.ode 0.7.2 and its floor

The floor bump is not strictly required for users: the package code
works on 0.61.0 and later. It is required for the package's own check.
`test-ode-nlf.R` requires 0.68.0's warning, so `R CMD check` of 0.7.2
against any core the old floor admits fails. A DESCRIPTION floor
should admit only cores the shipped package passes its checks on.

Recommendation: keep the bump. It matches the 0.66.0 precedent
(frmtmb.eam 0.11.2), the ecosystem releases in lockstep, and there are
no existing users to strand. A patch bump is correct for a test-only
change.

The alternative is to keep `frmtmb (>= 0.61.0)` and gate the
requirement: `require = if (packageVersion("frmtmb") >= "0.68.0") flat`.
Use it only if the extensions ever need to install against an older
core.

## Minors

- **m1 (fix before commit): the `cs_pred()` x `cumulative` compat row
  has the wrong count.** The row says "at the 4 of 24 points ... both
  are NaN", and the record says "20 finite points; the 4 NaN points".
  The record's own table and my rerun have 6 NaN points
  (`cum_cs_probit_disc` 1, `cum_cs_equi` 2, `cum_cs_probit2` 3) and
  18 finite points. The text is in `R/compat.R` and in
  `dev/round-20261005.md`, section a. `cum_cs_probit2`'s three
  perturbed points are all NaN, so that case compares only its
  optimum.
- **m2 (fix before commit): stale timing in two comments.**
  `R/predict.R`, in `gp_krig_factor()`, and the comment of the new
  test in `test-gp-multidim.R` both say "0.117 s against 0.017 s at
  rank 400 of 400". The log `dev/rel068-log/krig-rank.txt` and the
  NEWS say 0.0709 s against 0.0195 s.
- **m3: `predict()` on a crossing row silently conditions on the draws
  where the row does not cross.** `predict_category_props()` drops the
  NA replicates and divides by the rest
  (`R/predict-brms.R:470`). On `relrev-cs2.R`'s grid, row 4 crosses
  at the estimate: 977 of 1000 replicates are NA, and `predict()`
  reports P(Y = 4) = 1 from the other 23. Row 5 has 698 NA. Row 3
  does not cross at the estimate, and 34 NA are dropped there. There
  is no NA and no message. `fitted()` is NaN on the same rows, and
  the documentation lists `fitted()` and `simulate()` only. The same
  holds for `hurdle_cumulative()` from lane ordmix: 987 of 1000 NA,
  proportions over 13. The lift makes this reachable on the most used
  ordinal family. Suggest NA proportions for a row when any replicate
  (or more than a stated share) is NA, or a message with the count.
  Document it in `?frm`'s `cs()` section.
- **m4: the mutant arms' provenance is imprecise in the record.**
  `dev/rel068-mut/*` were copied at 04:41 from the tree before small
  changes a, b and e, and run at 04:43 against a rellib-r6 that held
  that tree (logs read "frmtmb 0.67.0"). They are not the final
  release tree. Each arm differs from the final `R/` by the
  resolution it undoes plus the later `cs()` lift, median, kriging
  and `?refit` edits, which none of the tested hunks touch. So the
  evidence holds, but the record should say what the arms were built
  from. Also reword "reads the offsets" as "reads the `cs()` offsets",
  which is what caused the question in this review.
- **m5: `cs()` on a cumulative component of a mixture often fails in
  the optimizer.** `relrev-csfail.R` used 20 seeds, n = 300, and data
  that are a two-class mixture with no `cs()` effect:

  | model | NaN-gradient error | non-converged | ok | other |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | `mixture(cumulative, sratio)`, `cs(z)` | 7 | 11 | 1 | 1 warned |
  | `mixture(cumulative, cumulative)`, `cs(z)` | 4 | 16 | 0 | |
  | control: `mixture(sratio, sratio)`, `cs(z)` | 0 | 6 | 3 | 11 warned |
  | control: `mixture(cumulative, sratio)`, no `cs()` | 0 | 5 | 14 | 1 warned |

  Plain cumulative and probit with `cs()` fit 20 of 20. The NaN
  gradient comes from crossing during the search. brms's warning
  ("may have convergence issues") covers this. File it in
  `dev/test-backlog.md` with these counts.
- **m6: make `test-gp-by.R`'s refusal independent of the search
  path.** The package's behavior is the decided one (item 5). The
  assertion `frm_linpred(fit, newdata = data.frame(x = 1))` errors
  with "by variable 'f'" only when no `f` is visible from the formula
  environment. `devtools::test()` in a session that has an `f` fails
  it. Two options:
  - build the formula in an environment whose parent is
    `baseenv()`;
  - name the by variable something no runner or user would define,
    and say why in a comment.

  Separately, the "snapshot outside constants" idea filed on
  2026-09-30 is where a brms-style refusal for predictors would land.
  This review adds one data point: a grouping factor of a random
  effect is also read from outside the data on newdata (a global `g`
  answered).
- **m7: a `cumulative("probit")` row without `cs()` can now draw NA.**
  `pnorm()` is not monotone in floating point near its branch point
  ±0.67449: 7,601 and 3,897 negative steps of 1 to 2 ulp in 2e5
  adjacent doubles (`relrev-mono.R`). plogis, pcauchy and the cloglog
  CDF have none. With two thresholds 1 ulp apart there, P = -1.1e-16.
  rellib-r5 drew a category on 200 of 200 draws, and rellib-r6 draws
  NA on 200 of 200 (`relrev-mono2.R`). The density there was already
  NaN on both builds. The case needs a degenerate threshold gap and a
  latent value within about 1e-11 of the branch point, so it is
  negligible in practice. Applying the NA only where `dpars$.cs` is
  present, or allowing a few ulp of negative, would remove it.

    eight do not. frmtmb.spline uses the prefix, and frmtmb.sample's
    first breaking bullet is not bold.
  - "The kriging draw on a high-rank grid is faster" is a performance
