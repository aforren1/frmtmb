# Review: lane `remlopt`, round 0.55

Reviewer's tree: `frmtmb-wt-remlopt` (branch `wt-remlopt`, base `a2deb65`),
uncommitted. Reference: `frmtmb` at `a2deb65` (0.54.0), read only.
Private libraries `rvrm-lib` (lane) and `rvrm-main-lib` (0.54.0).

## Verdict

**Mergeable after named fixes.** The density is correct. I could not
break it anywhere I looked, and it is better than the code it replaces
at every `nu` I measured, not only in the tail that motivated it. The
derivatives are better by the same margin and keep their sign where the
old ones flipped. Every gated count matches the 0.54.0 release figure.
Both fuzz runs reproduce the lane's table to the digit, and the deleted
entry is genuinely unnecessary.

Three things must be settled before this merges.

1. **F1 (HIGH, fixed here).** The new `diagnose()` message gives the
   opposite of the right advice when `nu` runs to the LOWER end of its
   link. On Cauchy data it prints "no heavy tails in the data, and the
   fit is the gaussian one". Fixed in this worktree, with a regression
   test seen failing first.
2. **F2 (HIGH, now resolved: see the appendix).** The performance claim
   did not hold. The new density is 1.06 to 1.17 times the old cost
   with a scalar `nu`, not 0.75 to 0.84, and it was 2.6 to 5.4 times
   the old cost when `nu` carries a linear predictor. NEWS said "It
   costs nothing". The lane corrected NEWS and found a shorter
   recurrence; I verified it and applied it, which cut the vector path
   to 0.61 to 0.70 of what this review measured. Appendix Z4.
3. **F3 (MEDIUM, not fixed).** The reason given for the branchless
   construction is false on the facts. RTMB 1.9 does offer a taped
   comparison that does not freeze. The construction is still right;
   the comment is not.

## What I measured and how

References are `Rmpfr` at 300 bits, and at `200 + 4 * ceiling(log2(a))`
bits above `a = 1e50` because a fixed-precision reference cancels there
itself (see "Suspicions chased and disproved"). Timings are the minimum
over 9 or more rounds, both tapes built in ONE process and measured
interleaved, with each call at a DISTINCT parameter vector.

## F1. HIGH. The new message is backwards at the lower end of `logm1`

`diagnose_unbounded_dpar()` fires on `abs(est) > 10`, so it catches
`nu` running DOWN to one as well as up. The printed message describes
only the upward case. Verbatim, on Cauchy errors, seed 77101, `n` 200:

```
Distributional parameter at the end of its link: nu: (Intercept) =
-19.1 (se 4261, natural scale 1)
  The likelihood was still rising when the optimizer stopped, ...
  For student()'s nu that reads as no heavy tails in the data, and the
  fit is the gaussian one: refit with gaussian() to say so, ...
```

That is Cauchy data. The advice is the opposite of the truth, and a
user who follows it fits a gaussian to the heaviest tails there are.

How often: 120 student fits over 10 error laws the lane did not use,
`n` of 60 and 200, 6 replicates. 8 of them landed at the lower bound
(`log(nu - 1)` from -20.5 to -17.7, standard error 4003 to 8533,
`nu` = 1.0000000x) and the check fired on all 8. The fuzz tier reaches
it too: seed 20470956 is a student fit at `nu` = 1.00733.

This also corrects item 10 of `dev/remlopt-findings.md`. The lane wrote
that `unbounded_dpar` "does not see" the lower end because the estimate
is small. That is true of the cells it happened to measure, which
stopped at `nu` = 1.31 and 1.48. When `nu` actually reaches the
boundary the estimate is large and negative and the check does see it.

**What I did.** Rewrote the printed message in `R/confint.R` to name
both directions, extended the `diagnose()` roxygen and the
`@noRd` block on `diagnose_unbounded_dpar()`, regenerated
`man/diagnose.Rd`, and added a test in `test-diagnostics-ux.R`. Run
against the lane's unfixed build the new test gives 126 pass / 1 fail,
failing only on the message assertion, which proves that the trigger
already fired at the lower end and only the words were wrong. After the
fix: 127 pass, 0 fail.

**Still open for the lane owner.** `?frmtmb-families`
("Degrees of freedom that run off"), `vignette("diagnostics")`
("A distributional parameter with no maximum") and the NEWS entry all
describe only the upward case. They are not wrong, but they are half
the story and they are where the message now sends people.

## F2. HIGH. The performance claim is a clock artifact, and a
## distributional `nu` is a real slowdown

`obj$fn` caches its last evaluation point, so a benchmark that calls it
with the same parameter vector every time measures the cache. And
`proc.time()` on this machine ticks at 10.0 ms, which I measured; the
lane's "min of 9 rounds of 200 calls" at a claimed 200.0 us is 0.04 s,
four ticks. Its 200.0 / 150.0 us pair is 4 ticks against 3, and 3/4 is
exactly the 0.75 reported.

My measurement, distinct parameter vector on every call, both tapes in
one process, interleaved with the order alternating between rounds,
minimum of 7 rounds each targeting 1.2 s:

| case | n | op | new | old | ratio |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| scalar `nu` | 5000 | `fn` | 344.2 us | 325.9 us | **1.056** |
| scalar `nu` | 5000 | `gr` | 664.1 us | 610.4 us | **1.088** |
| scalar `nu` | 50000 | `fn` | 4238.3 us | 3789.1 us | **1.119** |
| scalar `nu` | 50000 | `gr` | 7539.1 us | 6464.8 us | **1.166** |
| `nu ~ x` | 5000 | `fn` | 7109.4 us | 1489.3 us | **4.774** |
| `nu ~ x` | 5000 | `gr` | 10000.0 us | 2910.2 us | **3.436** |
| `nu ~ x` | 50000 | `fn` | 85937.5 us | 15781.2 us | **5.446** |
| `nu ~ x` | 50000 | `gr` | 110625.0 us | 42578.1 us | **2.598** |

The scalar case is a small regression, not a speedup. The
distributional case is the one that matters: with `nu ~ x` the shift
argument `a` is a VECTOR of length `n`, so the fixed 25-step recurrence
becomes 25 vector `log1p` nodes over `n` elements where the old code
had two scalar-per-element `lgamma` nodes. `bf(y ~ x, nu ~ z) +
student()` fits today, so this is a supported model, not a hypothetical.

**What to do.** At minimum, correct NEWS: "It costs nothing" and the
"0.75 to 0.84" table are not reproducible. Beyond that, `m = 25` with
four Binet terms is one point on a curve. Binet terms are two flops
each and shared; recurrence steps are `log1p` nodes on the tape. A
smaller `m` with a longer series would buy most of the vector cost
back. That is a design change to a shipped density and it needs its own
accuracy sweep, so I did not make it. The measurement is here so the
decision can be made on numbers.

**RESOLVED.** The lane corrected NEWS and located the shorter
recurrence; the appendix to this review verifies it against the same
scaled-precision reference and applies it. `m = 12` with six Binet
terms replaces `m = 25` with four. The vector path is now 0.61 to 0.70
of the numbers in the table above, against a control that resolves to
a few percent, and the scalar path does not move. Accuracy is a tie,
not the improvement the lane expected. Appendix Z1 to Z4.

## F3. MEDIUM. "RTMB has no `CondExp`" is true; the consequence drawn
## from it is not

`R/families.R` says a comparison "would freeze at whatever value the
tape was built with". Measured on RTMB 1.9:

* `getNamespaceExports("RTMB")` contains no `CondExp*`. True.
* `TapeConfig()[["compare"]]` is `"forbid"` by default, and a
  comparison on an advector then ERRORS
  (`Comparison is generally unsafe for AD types`). It does not freeze.
* Under `TapeConfig(comparison = "tape")` a comparison TAPES.
  `b <- x > 25; b * (x * 2) + (1 - b) * (x + 100)` re-evaluated at
  1, 10, 24, 25, 26, 100 gives the indicator 0 0 0 0 1 1, and the
  jacobian is 1 on the low branch and 2 on the high one. Nothing froze.
* `RTMB::ifelse` is exported but errors on an advector test under both
  settings (`Error in Ops.advector(test) : argument "e2" is missing`),
  so the only usable form is the multiplicative blend.

Branchless is still the right call, for reasons the comment does not
give: `TapeConfig` is a process-global setting whose default forbids
comparisons, and a package must not flip it under the user; and the
only reachable conditional is a blend, which evaluates both arms and so
does reintroduce the `0 * Inf` the lane cites. Rewrite the reason.

## F4. MEDIUM. The sigmoid blend was rejected on the wrong number

The lane says the blend returns `NaN` above `a = 1.8e308`. `1.8e308` is
not a representable double: `is.finite(1.8e308)` is `FALSE`.

Bisecting the real threshold, `lgamma(a)` is finite up to
`a = 2.533e+305` and `Inf` from there. That is `nu = 5.07e+305`,
reached at `log(nu - 1) = 703.9`, inside the double range, which allows
`log(nu - 1)` up to 709.78. `dt_stable()` is still right there: at
`nu = 5.07e305` it returns -0.918938533204653 against
`dnorm(0, log = TRUE)` of -0.918938533204673.

So the rejection stands and is STRONGER than argued: the blend fails at
a reachable `nu`, not beyond the double maximum. Only the threshold is
wrong, by three orders of magnitude. I did not try to reproduce the
blend's claimed 3.6e-13 accuracy, because the blend is not in the tree
and my own reconstruction would not be evidence about theirs.

## F5. MEDIUM. The check is general; its message is not

`unbounded_dpar` is not a student-only diagnosis and does not behave
like one.

* `negbinomial()` on Poisson-generated data (`n` 150, 4 seeds): log
  shape 16.0 to 19.0, standard error `NaN` on three and 29.9 on the
  fourth. The check fired on all four. `shape -> Inf` IS the Poisson
  limit, so the diagnosis is right and the message is about
  `student()`'s `nu` and `gaussian()`.
* `Beta()` at `phi` = 5000 (`n` 150, 4 seeds): log phi 8.54 to 8.62,
  standard error 0.116. Identified, not flagged. Correct.
* `skew_normal()` on symmetric data (`n` 150, 4 seeds): `alpha` stayed
  within +-1.4; one seed gave `alpha` = 0.002 with a standard error of
  14.6. Unidentified, and NOT flagged, because the estimate is small.
  That is the gap the lane recorded as open, with an example attached.
  Note also that `skew_normal()`'s `alpha` takes an IDENTITY link, so
  the fixed `abs(est) > 10` is not scale free there.
* `student()` on genuine `t(3)` data (4 seeds): not flagged. Correct.

My F1 fix widens the message but keeps `student()`'s `nu` as the named
example. If the check is to name `negbinomial()` too, that is the lane
owner's call.

## What I verified and could not break

### The density, across the whole range

There is no data-dependent shift threshold to test either side of:
`m = 25` is unconditional, so the smallest `b = a + m` any positive `a`
can produce is 25, and the boundary the task asks about is `a -> 0+`.
I tested `a` at 1e-8, 1e-4, 0.01, 0.05, 0.1, 0.25, 0.5, 0.65, 0.75, 1,
1.25, 1.5, 2.5, 5, 15, 24, 24.5, 24.9, 25, 25.1, 26, 50, 100, 1e3, 1e5,
1e6, 1e8, 1e10, 1e12, 1e14, 1e16, 1e20, 1e30, 1e50, 1e100, 1e200,
1e300, for `s` in 0.5, 1, 1.5, 3.

Worst absolute error of `lgamma_shift_diff()` over those 148 points is
5.68e-14, at `a` = 1e50 and `s` = 3 where the answer is 345. Worst
relative error is 2.9e-14, at `a` = 0.75 and `s` = 1.5 where the answer
is -0.078 and the absolute error is 2.3e-15. The naive difference is
wrong by 5e-3 at `a` = 1e12 and by 4.6e4 at `a` = 1e200.

At the LARGE `s` the covariance blocks use (`s = k/2`, so `s` = 50 for
an `ar(cov = TRUE)` group of 100 rows), `s` in 5, 10, 15, 25, 50, 100
over `a` from 1 to 1e200: worst relative error 5.5e-16. No regression
in the regime `covstruct.R` and `autocor.R` exercise.

`lgamma_binet()` at its worst reachable argument, `x = 25`, is 2.2e-16
below the reference, which is 6.6e-14 relative to `mu(25) = 3.33e-3`
but under one ulp of the order-one answer it feeds, and the two calls
partially cancel (2.2e-16 and 1.84e-16, difference 3.6e-17). The
roxygen's "exact in double precision from about `x = 25` up" is true in
the absolute sense that matters and loose in the relative one.

`dt_stable(z, nu)` against the reference on 21 values of `nu` from 0.1
to 1e300 crossed with `z` in 0, 1e-8, 0.25, 1, 2, 5, 10, 100, -1, -3.7:

* Worst error is 6.0 ulp of the answer for every `nu` up to 1e50.
  CORRECTED in the appendix: at `nu` = 1e100, 1e200 and 1e300 it is
  28.8, 40.8 and 98.5 ulp, which is 2.0e-14 absolute. The first
  version of this line said 6.0 ulp over all 210 points, reading rows
  whose reference had itself cancelled. The absolute figure was right
  in the gaussian-limit table below all along, and the behavior is the
  same for every `(m, k)` pair tried, so it is the outer subtraction
  and not the series. See appendix section Z1.
* **At the moderate `nu` where the old code was fine (5, 10, 30, 100,
  1000, 1e5, 1e6) the new code is within 3.3 ulp** and `stats::dt` is
  comparable. No digit was lost to fix the tail.
* At `nu` = 1 it matches the Cauchy closed form `-log(pi) -
  log1p(z^2)` to 1.8e-15 or better at `z` = 0, 0.5, 1, 3, 50, 1e6.
* `nu` at 0 or below gives `NaN`, as `stats::dt` does. `z` non-finite
  with `nu` finite behaves as `stats::dt` does.

The gaussian limit, `dt_stable(z, nu) - dnorm(z, log = TRUE)` at

## Suspicions chased and disproved

* **"`dt_stable` is wrong by 115 log units at `nu` = 1e100."** It is
  not. A 300-bit `Rmpfr` reference forms the same
  `lgamma(a + s) - lgamma(a)` and cancels in exactly the same way once
  `a` exceeds about 1e80: at `a` = 1e100 each `lgamma` is 2.3e102 and
  300 bits resolves it only to 1e12. Re-measured with the precision
  scaled to the argument (`200 + 4 * ceiling(log2(a))` bits, so 1532
  bits at `a` = 1e100 and 4188 at 1e300), `lgamma_shift_diff()` is
  EXACT to the last printed digit at `a` = 1e50, 1e80, 1e100, 1e200 and
  1e300, and `dt_stable()` is within 2.0e-14 of the reference at
  `nu` = 1e300. The same artifact made a 400-bit reference claim the
  taped NLL was 1.5e4 too small at `log(nu - 1)` = 500; it is not.
* **"The 25-step recurrence must cost more than two `lgamma` nodes."**
  With a SCALAR `nu` it barely does, because the recurrence is 25
  scalar nodes against `n` vector elements: 1.06 to 1.17 times. The
  cost only becomes real with a vector `nu`. That is why F2 separates
  the two cases.
* **"`unbounded_dpar` fires on ordinary fits."** It does not. 0 of 99
  identified fits over 10 unseen error laws.
* **"The `row_permutation` residues are assembly identities the
  convergence gate should not excuse."** The shared-parameter check
  evaluates `obj$fn` at the MIDPOINT of the two optima, and with random
  effects present that involves an inner Laplace solve, so it is not a
  pure function of the outer parameters. The gate is defensible. I
  still flag seed 20309751 above, where the fit is only mildly off.
* **"The gated brms tier will move because `student()` is in it."** It
  is not in it at all. See F7.

# Appendix, 2026-09-08: the (m, k) variant, verified and applied

The lane found that the Binet series length depends on no parameter,
that a longer series lets the recurrence shorten, and that
`m = 12, k = 6` looked both cheaper and more accurate than the
`m = 25, k = 4` it shipped. It did not apply the change, on the
grounds that its own re-run of the sweeps would not be the independent
check the shipped numbers rest on. That was right, so the check is
here.

## Verdict on the variant

**Applied. The merged code should carry `m = 12` with a six-term Binet
series.** It verifies at least as well as the shipped constants and it
cuts the vector-path cost raised as F2 by about 37 percent.

One correction to the lane's case, which does not change the decision
but does change the reason. **It is not more accurate. Accuracy is a
tie.** The two figures the lane compared (2.938e-14 for the shipped
constants against 1.276e-14 for the variant) are both single-point
rounding accidents at `(a, s)` points where the answer nearly
vanishes: -0.0784 and 0.0139 respectively. Ordering variants by that
statistic is ordering them by noise, and `m = 17, k = 5` scores the
same 1.276e-14 while `m = 12, k = 8` scores 4.602e-15. Over 800 points
in six bands neither ordering dominates. The case for the change is
cost, plus a genuine but small edge at the series floor.

## Z0. What I built, and that it is what now ships

I wrote the `(m, k)` family myself from the Bernoulli numbers rather
than auditing the lane's scratch file, so that agreement between the
two is evidence rather than a shared typo. The coefficients are
`B_{2n} / (2n(2n - 1))`, and I checked each one against the truncation
it predicts: for this series the remainder after `k` terms has the
sign of, and is bounded by, the first omitted term, so measured error
over that term must be about -1. Measured -0.96 at every `k` from 4 to
7 crossed with `x` in 8, 12, 17, 25, 28. A wrong coefficient breaks
that ratio; none did.

At `m = 25, k = 4` my implementation is bitwise identical to what the
package shipped: `lgamma_binet()`, `lgamma_shift_diff()` and
`dt_stable()` all `identical()` at every probe, and so is the taped
objective. After applying the change, the installed package is bitwise
identical to my `m = 12, k = 6`. So the sweep below is a statement
about the code that now ships, not about a look-alike.
