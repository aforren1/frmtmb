## Seam 1: what I verified, and what it cost

### Verified sound

**The factorization identities hold on the tape, not just numerically.**
On my own data (7 subjects, ragged trial counts 14-25, `id` levels whose
alphabetical order is neither appearance nor length order):

- `rw_delta`: `sum(loglik_row)` and `sum(loglik_group)` against `loglik`,
  through `RTMB::MakeTape` at four parameter vectors: worst residual
  **5.68e-14** on values of magnitude 90-128 (relative 5e-16). Taped
  *gradients* agree to **0**. Stacking x2 reproduces `c(g, g)` and
  `c(r, r)` exactly.
- `rw_delta` against an independent plain-R recursion written from the
  model equations: `max|loglik_row - reference| = 4.4e-16`,
  `max|loglik_group - reference| = 1.8e-15`.
- `hmm`: `loglik_group` against an independent plain-R forward algorithm
  (stationary initial distribution solved separately): **2.1e-14** over
  5 sequences, and `sum(reference) - logLik(fit) = -2.8e-14`.

**The group ORDER is right and a permutation is caught.** The hmm layout
sorts sequences by decreasing length; on my data that permutation was
`5,2,3,4,1`, so `[order(hg[["order"]])]` (hmm.R:936) is load-bearing, not
decoration. The pieces came back in `levels()` order; had they come back
in layout order the error would have been **48.15**, not 2e-14. And a
family that deliberately reverses its units is refused at the first
freeze by `imp_verify()` (importance.R:835):

    `importance` could not reproduce this model's joint log-density from
    its per-group pieces (group '1': -0.4558654683 against -0.1287963821)

**The group branch of the importance correction is arithmetically the
rowwise one.** A toy structured family whose likelihood is exactly
gaussian, fitted three ways (built-in rowwise, `loglik_group`,
`loglik_row`), `importance = 16`:

- `max|amat(group) - amat(row)| = 0` (bit-identical)
- against weights hand-built from public accessors as
  `loglik_g(theta, u_j) + logprior_g(u_j)`:
  **max|hand - (amat - zsq)| = 3.55e-15**, correlation 1, and the
  objective the hand weights imply differs from the core's by **0**.
- against the built-in rowwise gaussian, objective 135.937798993 vs
  135.937800536 (relative 1e-8; each fit freezes its proposal at its own
  estimate, and the estimates differ at 4e-7).

**Deviance residuals from `loglik_row` are the rowwise formula.** A toy
family declaring `lpdf` and `loglik_row` consistently reproduces the
built-in gaussian's deviance residuals exactly up to the toy's own choice
of scaled unit deviance: the ratio is a constant `1/sigma_hat = 0.97060`
on every row (built-in glm deviance is `(y-mu)^2`, the toy's is
`(y-mu)^2/sigma^2`). `sum(d^2) = 60.0001` on 60 rows, which is what a
gaussian at its ML sigma must give.

**`frmtmb.sample::log_lik()` returns the coarsest declared slot.** On a
sampled `rw_delta` fit (8 subjects, 320 rows, 300 draws) the matrix is
`300 x 8`, one column per subject, varying across draws, with
`attr(, "unit") = "one subject's trial sequence"`.

## Findings, by severity

### 1. MEDIUM. Deviance residuals double-apply `weights()`

`structure_unit_deviance()` passes the row weights INTO the family's slot
(`R/structure.R:827`):

    ll <- lr(fit$frame[["y"]][[rn]], eval_dpars(fit)[[rn]], av,
             av[["weights"]] %||% 1, blk, fit_extras(fit))

and the caller then multiplies the returned unit deviance by the same
weights again (`R/predict.R:2418`):

    r <- sign(r) * sqrt((av[["weights"]] %||% 1) *
                          structure_unit_deviance(object, rspec, st, blk))

A family cannot satisfy both. Worse, `frmtmb_structure()` steers families
into the failing branch: `R/structure.R:338-349` WARNS any slot lacking a
`weights` argument that the weights "are dropped", and says "Take
`weights` and use it".

Measured with a toy family that IS gaussian, declaring `lpdf` and
`loglik_row` consistently, 60 rows, integer weights 1-4:

| slot behavior | ratio to the built-in weighted gaussian |
|---|---|
| slot IGNORES the weights | 0.97060 on every row (a constant: the toy's scaled-deviance convention) |
| slot USES them, as documented | 0.97060 times `sqrt(w)` |

With w = 1,2,4,3 the observed ratios were 0.97260, 1.37546, 1.94520,
1.68459, i.e. exactly `sqrt(w)` times the correct value. Reproduction:
`scratchpad/rvps-b9-dev.R`.

The two consumers of the same slot disagree about the convention: the
importance correction (`R/importance.R:756`) passes `wts` in and does NOT
reapply, so there the FAMILY is the weight applier; the deviance path
treats the CORE as the weight applier. Only one can be the contract.

Latent today: the only shipped family with `loglik_row` (`rw_delta`)
refuses `weights()` in `check_spec`. It is a trap set for the next
family, not a live wrong number.

FIX: drop the outer multiply at `R/predict.R:2418-2419`, making the
family the weight applier and matching importance, and say so in the
`loglik_row` docs beside the saturated-value paragraph.

### 2. MEDIUM. `loo()` on a group-unit matrix never says it is per group

`log_lik()` does its half correctly: on a sampled `rw_delta` fit the
matrix is 300 x 8, one column per subject, and carries
`attr(, "unit") = "one subject's trial sequence"`. Then
`loo.frmtmb_draws()` (`extensions/frmtmb.sample/R/loo.R:361-367`) hands
the bare matrix to `loo::loo.matrix()`, which knows nothing about the
attribute:

    Computed from 300 by 8 log-likelihood matrix.
             Estimate   SE
    elpd_loo   -160.2 14.0

Nothing there distinguishes 8 subjects from 8 observations, and
`attr(lo, "unit")` is NULL. The documentation at `loo.R:216-220` claims
the opposite: "The matrix then carries `attr(x, "unit")` naming what a
column is, so `loo()` on it is leave-one-UNIT-out and reads that way." It
does not read that way. Reproduction: `scratchpad/rvps-d3-sample.R`.

This is the one place a user can silently mis-read a number:
`elpd_loo = -160.2` is leave-one-SUBJECT-out and looks exactly like
leave-one-observation-out.

FIX: surface the unit (wrap the loo object with a print method that adds
one line), or at minimum correct the sentence at `loo.R:220` to say the
attribute is carried for the caller and is not surfaced by `loo()`.

### 4. LOW. The allow-list does not fix the `wiener()` case that motivated it

`dev/structured-family-protocol.md:522` gives the motivating defect as
"`wiener()` took a `vint()` it cannot use the same way". That is wrong
twice.

`wiener()` CAN use `vint()`: its lpdf reads it as the boundary indicator
when `dec` is absent (`wiener-family.R:438-439`), and measured,
`rt | vint(upper)` and `rt | dec(upper)` give a bit-identical logLik of
-130.566406836. `vint()` is a working alternative spelling, not a term
the family cannot use.

The real defect is the two TOGETHER, and it survives this lane:

    frm(bf(rt | dec(upper) + vint(upper) ~ 1), family = wiener(), ...)
    #> fits. logLik -113.080912231, bit-identical to dec() alone.
    #> aterm values on the fit: dec, vint1

`dec` wins at `wiener-family.R:438` and the `vint()` column travels into
the fit and changes nothing, with no warning. The allow-list cannot catch
it, because both terms are legitimately on the list. The lane discloses
this as a "left out" item (exclusivity among `required_aterms`
alternatives); the protocol doc does not, and states the opposite.
Reproduction: `scratchpad/rvps-f1-refuse.R`, `rvps-m1-wiener.R`.

FIX (doc, this lane): correct `:522` to name the real case, `dec()` and
`vint()` supplied together. FIX (code, a follow-up): exclusivity among
alternatives.

### The regression I went looking for and did not find

A family carrying an `lcdf`/`lccdf` can support `cens()` and `trunc()`
through the core, not through its own lpdf, so an allow-list that omits
them would silently remove working functionality. Checked every family:

- families with a CDF whose declaration drops `trunc()`: **none**
- families with a CDF whose declaration drops `cens()`: `poisson` only,
  which is correct, because `cens()` is refused for a discrete response
  one guard earlier.

### Refusal precedence

- `poisson` + `cens()` -> "cens() is not supported for discrete families
  yet (truncation is)". The specific guard fires first. Correct.
- `binomial` + `se()` -> "se() is supported for gaussian and student
  families only". Correct.
- `gaussian` + `trials()` -> the allow-list message. There was no
  pre-existing family-specific refusal for this pair (the base tree's
  `R/frame.R` refuses only non-integer counts), so this is a NEW refusal
  of a previously silent acceptance, not a preempted message.
- `gaussian` + `vint()` -> the allow-list message. Nothing else refused
  it before.

`wiener()`, `lba(3)`, `rdm(3)` all refuse `dec()`/`vreal()` where the
declaration says they should; see findings 4 and 5 for the two costs.
