## Verdict: FIX

The route is right. TMB's `"cdf"` method gets the lower and upper tails in
the form it reads (`Fx = F/(F+S)`, `px = p/(F+S)`, residual
`qnorm(Fx - U px)`), and my own Laplace approximation of the three
integrals reproduces the lane's residuals to 1.4e-5. Every count family
now sits in its own step with no group-level term, the bernoulli defect is
gone, the four mutants fail the new tests, and the extension suites that
touch OSA pass. What is wrong is around the route: case weights under a
group-level term give residuals of a model that was not fitted (B1), the
compat row for von_mises was never flipped (M1), the Laplace error and the
cost are stated as smaller than they are (M2, M3), and a large positive
outlier comes back as `Inf` with a warning that blames the CDF (M4).

## Findings

### BLOCKER

**B1. With a group-level term, the "cdf" route conditions on rows the fit
weighted differently, so weighted residuals are silently wrong.**
`osa_cdf_loglik()` drops `w` from the density term. That term is both the
row's own mass and the conditioning of every later row on the group effect.
The lane fixed the first and broke the second.
`dev/review-osa-weights.R` (seed 301, poisson, 20 groups of 10, weight 2 on
rows t = 1..3 of each group). The weighted fit and the fit of the
duplicated data have the same estimates to 6 digits, so they are one model:

```
rows t >= 4 (weight 1, after the weighted rows): max|qnorm(hi) weighted - duplicated| 0.327, median 0.0745
weighted fit, rows t >= 4: max|lane - exact weighted| 0.325, max|lane - exact unweighted| 0.00339, max|exact weighted - exact unweighted| 0.328
weighted fit residual vs exact weighted-conditioning residual, rows t >= 4: max 0.25 median 0.0554; rows over 0.05: 74 of 140
```

The lane's residuals match the exact predictive of the UNWEIGHTED posterior
(0.0034) and miss the fitted one by up to 0.33. Weight 0, the common way
to drop rows from a fit, is the sharp case (`dev/review-osa-edge.R`, seed
407, rows t <= 4 given weight 0 and y + 6): the estimates equal the fit
without those rows, but the mean residual of the remaining rows is
**-1.751** against **0.014** without them. No warning. NEWS says the
change makes "each row ... read against its own fitted distribution"; that
is true only with no group-level term. Only the compat note mentions the
unweighted conditioning.

A fix exists and is two lines. Keep `w` on the density term and give each
tail `(w - 1) * ll`:

```r
sum(keep[, 1] * w * ll + keep[, 2] * (ct[["lower"]] + (w - 1) * ll) +
      keep[, 3] * (ct[["upper"]] + (w - 1) * ll))
```

Then `Fx` and `px` are those of the last of `w` copies, and later rows
condition on all `w`. With no group-level term `p^(w-1)` cancels, so
nothing the lane tested moves. Built as `rev-osa-lib/m5`
(`dev/review-osa-out/weights-m5.log`, `tests-m5.log`):

```
rows t >= 4: max|qnorm(hi) weighted - duplicated| 8.27e-07, median 1.26e-07
weighted fit residual vs exact weighted-conditioning residual, rows t >= 4: max 0.00244 median 0.000267; rows over 0.05: 0 of 140
RESULT test-osa-cdf.R pass=41 fail=0 err=0 skip=0 warn=0
```

A weight-0 row gets no meaningful residual of its own under any scheme;
return NA there. Add a test of weight 2 against the duplicated row under
`(1 | g)`. No test in the suite tells the lane's version from m5.

**M3. The cost grows faster than linearly, and 2.6x understates it.**
`dev/review-osa-time.R`, seed 601, poisson `(1 | g)`, groups of 20, one
call per arm, the old call made on the same objective as the lane's
`dev/osa-time.R` makes it:

```
rows  500 groups  25 | fit 0.2s | no group term: cdf 0.09s | (1 | g): cdf 145.5s old 23.1s cdf/old 6.30
rows 2000 groups 100 | fit 0.2s | no group term: cdf 0.70s | (1 | g): cdf 2034.9s old 328.8s cdf/old 6.19
```

So the ratio is 6.2, not 2.6. Four times the rows costs 14 times as much,
and the 2000-row residuals take **34 minutes** after a 0.2 s fit. The old
route grew 14 times as well, so the quadratic shape is TMB's: 3 n
evaluations of an O(n) tape. The extra factor of 6 is the CDF on the tape.
`dev/review-osa-time2.R`, same 500-row design:

```
bernoulli    rows 500 (1 | g): cdf 15.2s old 44.8s cdf/old 0.34
negbinomial  rows 500 (1 | g): cdf 163.0s old 122.9s cdf/old 1.33
```

bernoulli, whose CDF is one term, costs a tenth of poisson (145.5 s). So
the summed count CDFs (`poisson_cdf_log()`, `nbinom_cdf_log()`) are the
cost, not the route. It matters: a poisson GLMM of a few thousand rows is
ordinary, and its default residual call now runs for most of an hour.

With no group-level term the route is cheap (0.09 s at 500 rows). With
one, the cdf tape carries every row's CDF pair on every evaluation (the
indicator is a parameter, so nothing is skipped), and TMB makes 3 n
evaluations, each with an inner Newton solve. NEWS must give the size
dependence. Before release, try a cheaper count CDF on the OSA tape (for
example the regularized incomplete gamma for poisson). Otherwise, give
`subset =` guidance in the help.

**M4. A positive residual above about 8.3 is `Inf`, with a warning that
blames the CDF.** TMB forms `qnorm(Fx - U px)` on the probability scale, and
`Fx` rounds to 1 when the upper tail is below about 1e-16.
`dev/review-osa-out/edge2.log` (seed 410, 2000 rows, one outlier):

```
outlier  +8.0: fullGaussian   7.8973 | trunc() via cdf  7.90569 | (y - mu) / sigma   7.8973
outlier  +8.5: fullGaussian   8.3726 | trunc() via cdf      Inf | (y - mu) / sigma   8.3726  WARN: 1 of 2000 one-step-ahead residuals are not finite: oneStepPredict(method = "cdf") could no
outlier +12.0: fullGaussian  11.6103 | trunc() via cdf      Inf | (y - mu) / sigma  11.6103  WARN: ...
outlier -12.0: fullGaussian -11.5654 | trunc() via cdf -11.5654 | (y - mu) / sigma -11.5654
```

Counts are the same (`edge.log`, seed 401): poisson y = 60 at mu about 3
reads `Inf` where the true residual is 15.24, with the warning "could not
form the one-step CDF on those rows". It could; the row is an outlier.
At +8.0 the value is already 0.008 off. The negative side holds to -37.
This was TMB's formula before too, but the lane now sends every
continuous family and a truncated gaussian through it, and wrote the
warning text. Fix: `oneStepPredict()` returns `nll`, `nlcdf.lower` and
`nlcdf.upper`. Form the residual from the smaller tail, with
`Sx = 1 / (1 + exp(nlcdf.upper - nlcdf.lower))` and
`-qnorm(Sx + U px)` where `Fx > 0.5`, using the same uniforms
(`set.seed(seed); runif(n)`, restoring the RNG state as TMB does). Until
then, the warning must say the value is out of range, not that the CDF
failed.

### MINOR

**m1. Gaussian weights mean precision. Every other family's weights now
mean frequency.** `dev/review-osa-weights.R` (seed 302, weight 3 on half
of 120 rows, data with constant variance):
`mean r_fullGaussian / r_cdf 1.7321 (sqrt(3) = 1.7321)`, and `student()`
on the same data gives the weight-3 rows sd 1.023 against 1.778 for the
gaussian default. brms reads a weight as a frequency (it multiplies the
log-likelihood), and under that reading, with no group-level term, each
copy's residual is `z`. For a gaussian the fitted likelihood cannot tell
the two readings apart, so this is a choice. The help documents it, but
the user should decide (open question 2). glmmTMB 1.1.15.2 gives no hint:
its `simulate()` on a weighted poisson says nothing
(`msgs-after.log`).

Weights in (0, 1), my ruling: NA with a warning is right for a count, and
better than a refusal, which would throw away every other row. It is not
needed for a continuous response. Two points:
- The lane's reason is out of date. `osa_cdf_loglik()` clamps the tail
  factor at `pmax(w, 1) - 1`, so below 1 the tails carry no extra density
  and nothing diverges. A row of weight 0 read NaN only under the
  unclamped `w - 1`.
- With the clamp, a row of weight `w < 1` has `Fx = int F pi / int (F + S)
  pi`, with `pi` the posterior given the earlier rows at their weights.
  That is the one-step CDF of a fresh copy of the row, and at `w = 1` it
  meets the "last of w copies" form. For a continuous response that is
  the whole residual (`U = 0`), so Gamma, lognormal and the rest could
  return it. For a count, TMB's `px` comes from the `w * ll` density
  term, so `px = int p^w pi`, which is not the row's mass. A sound number
  there needs `p` from a call at `y - 1`. NA with a warning is the right
  call for counts.

So the lane's NA is conservative, not wrong. `sc-weights2.log` item 3:
Gamma with weight 0.5 reads NA on 60 rows with the lane's warning, and
weight 1.5 reads finite values on both families.
