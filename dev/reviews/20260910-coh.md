## 2. The new rule. Could not falsify; it predicts all five rungs

The lane's rule is that `(1 | id)` cancels out of a within-subject
contrast and `(1 | id:cond)` carries it, so omitting the crossed term
collapses the interval while omitting the subject term forces its
variance into the crossed term and inflates it. I tried to break it
five ways from `dev/coh-recovery-main.tsv` and could not
(`dev/cohrev-tsv.R`).

1. **The absorption is exact, not approximate.** The crossed-only
   rung estimates `sd(id:cond)` at **0.3955** plus or minus 0.0416.
   The correct model's `sqrt(sd_id^2 + sd_idcond^2)` is **0.3955**, to
   four decimals, and the truth's is 0.4031. The between-subject
   variance goes into the crossed term whole.
2. **The absorption predicts the width.** 0.3955 / 0.1965 = **2.012**
   predicted against a paired mean of **1.920** and a median of 1.885,
   and the per-seed width ratio tracks the per-seed component ratio at
   **r = 0.996**.
3. **`(1 | id)` really does cancel.** Adding it to a model with no
   crossed term moves the contrast's standard error by a paired median
   factor of **0.9965**, range 0.9869 to 1.0069.
4. **Adding it to the crossed-only model halves the error**, 0.5305
   median, range 0.3600 to 0.7593, against 0.1965 / 0.3955 = 0.497
   predicted by the same absorption.
5. **It widens the intercept and not the contrast**, which is the
   half-sentence in the shipped Rd that carried no measurement
   anywhere in the lane's files. Three seeds of the small design
   (`dev/cohrev-intercept.R`): `(1 | id)` multiplies the intercept's
   standard error by **1.678, 1.937 and 2.096** and the contrast's by
   1.005, 1.034 and 1.071.

**Not a degrees-of-freedom artifact.** Both rungs fit the same Wald
interval on the same number of estimated levels; what differs is the
magnitude of the component, measured above. The reviewer's alternative
explanation does not survive point 1.

**One wording risk.** "More random effects is not the rule; the
grouping the contrast varies within is" ships in `?cross_wishart`,
`vignette("coherence")` and `NEWS.md`. Read as a prescription it
licenses `cond + s(freq, by = cond) + (1 | id:cond)`, which is the very
model the lane measured at 1.9 times too wide two sentences earlier.
The sentence is the one a reader will carry. Nit N1 below.

## 3. The bias. Real, and it is a DIFFERENT ESTIMAND. The lane's
attribution is falsified

Every rung of a replicate saw the same data at the same n, so the
rungs difference seed by seed (`dev/cohrev-bias.R`). That is far more
powerful than five means against 0.5, and it also kills the
finite-sample reading: an n-driven explanation cannot bias two rungs
and leave three alone when all five have the same n.

| paired difference | value | se | t |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `cond` minus correct | -0.01969 | 0.00050 | -39.70 |
| `smooth` minus correct | -0.01328 | 0.00050 | -26.74 |
| `id` minus correct | +0.00360 | 0.00078 | +4.63 |
| `idcond` minus correct | -0.00000 | 0.00009 | -0.05 |

**It is the marginal contrast, and the prediction is quantitative.** A
contrast on a logit scale is not collapsible: a model that leaves
variation in the linear predictor unmodeled is consistent for the
population-averaged contrast, which is attenuated by about
`1 / sqrt(1 + 0.346 V)` with `V` the omitted variance. Computed from
the generator's own constants, with nothing fitted, `V` is 0.2410 for
`cond` (bump variance 0.07847 plus 0.35^2 plus 0.20^2) and 0.1625 for
`smooth`:

| rung | predicted bias | observed, paired |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `cond` | -0.01962 | **-0.01969** |
| `smooth` | -0.01349 | **-0.01328** |
| `idcond` | 0 | -0.00000 |

Within 0.4 and 1.6 percent. These rungs are not estimating 0.5 badly.
They are estimating a different quantity well, and no interval width
repairs that. **The fix differs, so the documentation has to say
which.**

**Two statements in the durable record are false.**

- The plan row and `dev/coh-findings.md` say "the two rungs that also
  drop the **SMOOTH** are not purely a width failure". Only one rung
  drops the smooth. The two biased rungs are the two with **no random
  effects at all**, and the correct model of the bias needs the random
  effects in it, as the table above shows.
- `dev/coh-findings.md` says "Dropping the frequency term moves the
  estimate; dropping the subject-by-condition term does not." Paired,
  dropping `(1 | id)` moves it by **-0.01687**, dropping the smooth by
  **-0.00641**, dropping `(1 | id:cond)` by **+0.00360**. The frequency
  term is the smallest of the three, not the driver.

**The correct reading was already in the repository.**
`dev/scale-findings.md`, in the paragraphs above its own ladder table,
already calls this "the attenuated marginal one", already measures it
at "0.016 across the whole ladder, about a fifth of a standard error",
and already says it is small and monotone. This lane's document moved
away from a record that was right. The standing rule about searching
`dev/` before restating a figure applies to interpretations too.

**One residual the model does not explain.** The `id` rung. The
attenuation formula predicts -0.00342 and the paired measurement is
+0.00360, a gap of 0.007 at t = 4.63. It is small in absolute terms,
0.0036 on a truth of 0.5, and it does not touch the width conclusion,
but it is the rung the whole narrative rests on and it should be named
rather than folded into "0.13 of the estimator's own spread".

## 4. The guard. It fires. Constructed independently, and seen to fail

`dev/cohrev-guard.R` and `dev/cohrev-guard2.R` lift the shipped
expression out of `test-coherence.R` and run it through testthat, so a
failure is a failure and not a printed FALSE.

- **A, the test's own seeds with the effect absent from BOTH arms.**
  The two arms' widths are `identical()`, the quotient is exactly 1,
  and testthat reports `Expected min(treat/null) > 1. Actual
  comparison: 1.0 <= 1.0`. **Seen it fail.**
- **B, four seeds the lane never used, 2700:2703, effect absent.**
  Same, exactly 1, fails. So the firing is not a property of the
  pinned seeds.
- **D, the shipped arm at those same fresh seeds.** Passes. The guard
  is not seed-specific in either direction.
- The sign check `min(treat) > 1` passes in both absent arms at
  1.0000000332, so it discriminates nothing, exactly as the test's own
  comment says.

**The threshold is a ratio.** `min(treat / null) > 1` compares two
quantities the run measures against a scale-free boundary. No absolute
tolerance anywhere in the file. The rule holds.

**The mechanism the guard rests on, checked.** The pairing works only
because the generator writes `rnorm(n) * sd` rather than
`rnorm(n, 0, sd)`. Measured: `rnorm(n, 0, 0)` skips the draw and
`rnorm(n) * 0` does not, and `cp_cells()` consumes the same random
stream at `sd_idcond` 0 and 0.5. That last is a one-line assertion
costing zero fits and it is not asserted. Nit N3.

**The false-alarm rate on correct data, for the SHIPPED statistic**
(`dev/cohrev-falsealarm.R`, from the lane's own 40 paired quotients).
The log quotient has mean 0.6210 and sd 0.1685, so 1 sits 3.69 sd
below the mean. Lognormal tail: 1.14e-4 per seed, and the test takes
the minimum of four, so **about 4.5e-4, one run in 2,200**. Model free,
0 of 40 at or below 1 bounds the per-seed rate at 0.088 and the
four-seed rate at 0.31 with 95 percent confidence, which is all the
data alone can say.

**Where the guard stops discriminating.** With the effect PRESENT but
small, `sd(id:cond) = 0.15` at seeds 2700:2703, the shipped assertion
**fails**, `min(treat / null) = 0.999999956`. The discrimination floor
is between 0.15 and 0.5. The lane's calibration table implies this and
nobody wrote it down.

## 1. BLOCKER. "About 1 in 2,200" is one shape's answer, not a
measurement

`dev/cohrev2-falsealarm.R`, on the lane's own 40 paired quotients.

**The 40 points do not choose the shape.** Shapiro-Wilk on `log(q)`
gives W = 0.9651, p = 0.249; on the raw `q`, W = 0.9586, p = 0.150.
Neither is rejected, so a lognormal and a normal both fit the body and
the data cannot prefer one.

**Shapes that fit the same body put the tail orders apart.** Each
fitted to those 40 points, each evaluated at the minimum of four:

| shape | per seed | four-seed | reads as |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| lognormal, the lane's | 1.14e-4 | 4.5e-4 | 1 in 2,202 |
| gamma on the raw scale | 4.41e-4 | 1.76e-3 | 1 in 568 |
| t(5) on the log scale | 2.53e-3 | 1.01e-2 | 1 in 99 |
| normal on the raw scale | 2.80e-3 | 1.11e-2 | **1 in 90** |

A factor of 25 across shapes the data cannot separate.

**Inside the lane's own shape the number is not resolved either.** The
answer sits 3.7 sd into the tail, so it is governed by an sd estimated
from 40 points whose own standard error is 0.0191 on 0.1685. Refitting
the lognormal on 20,000 fresh samples of 40: median 1 in 2,537, **95
percent range 1 in 162 to 1 in 284,172**. Nonparametric bootstrap of
the same fit: 1 in 334 to 1 in 108,531. Three and a half orders of
magnitude.

**With no shape at all**, 0 of 40 gives a Clopper-Pearson 95 percent
upper bound of 0.0722 per seed, so the four-seed statistic fires at
most 0.259 of the time, **about 1 in 3.9**. The rule of three agrees,
0.075 per seed.

The plan row does disclose the construction inline, which is why this
is a precision defect and not a false claim. But two significant
figures on a quantity whose honest interval inside its own model spans
1 in 162 to 1 in 284,000, and whose model-free bound is 1 in 4, is the
manufactured-precision shape the standing rules name. **Fix, in the
plan row and in the `test-coherence.R` comment that repeats it:** say
what was observed first and the fit second, for example "0 of 40 seeds
fired, which bounds the per-seed rate below 0.072 and the four-seed
rate below 0.26 at 95 percent confidence; a lognormal fit to the same
40 puts it near 1 in 2,000, though a normal fit to them gives 1 in 90."

## 2. Nit. The second `V` is a real independent test at a looser grade,
and the reference is not to blame

`dev/cohrev2-checks.R`. The method matches: both arms take a reference
rung with `V = 0` and compute `V` from the same generator constants,
`full` in the main arm and `id` in the null arm where `sd(id:cond)` is
0. Nothing is wrong with the construction.

| arm | rung | predicted | observed | residual | in se |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| main | `cond` | -0.01962 | -0.01969 | -0.00006 | -0.12 |
| main | `smooth` | -0.01349 | -0.01328 | +0.00021 | 0.43 |
| null | `cond` | -0.01653 | -0.01570 | +0.00083 | 0.93 |
| null | `smooth` | -0.01027 | -0.00917 | +0.00110 | 1.16 |

**The null arm's standard error is 1.9 times the main arm's and its
residual is 5.1 times as large.** That is not the same grade of
agreement and the document should not let the two read alike.

**The obvious excuse is excluded.** Both null-arm residuals are
positive and nearly equal, which would be the signature of an offset
reference, but in the null arm `full` minus `id` is -0.00005 with se
0.00010, t = -0.47. The reference is clean, and correcting for it
makes the two residuals marginally worse, +0.00087 and +0.00114.

**The right reading, which neither the lane nor the punch round has.**
`0.346` is a standard approximation, not an identity. Over all four
predictions its demonstrated accuracy on this generator is **about
0.001 on a contrast of 0.5**, and the main arm's agreement in the
fourth decimal is better than the approximation deserves. Say that,
and call the null arm a second `V` that the formula survives rather
than a confirmation.

## 3. Nit. The residual arithmetic is an identity, and saying so is the
point

`r(smooth - id) + r(id) - r(smooth) = 1.73e-18`, and
`all.equal(r(smooth - id), r(smooth) - r(id))` is TRUE. Both the
paired means and the predictions subtract across rungs, so the
residuals subtract exactly. The 0.0002 the lane quotes **is** the
`smooth` rung's own residual, +0.00021, to the last digit.

So the sentence is true and it is arithmetic, not a second check. As
written a reader takes it as independent support for "the residual
sits on one rung". It should say that the residuals are additive by
construction, which is what actually makes the localization
meaningful.
