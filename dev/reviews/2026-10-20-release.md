## 3. `varcorr_trans_rows()`'s 16-ulp rule and the draw-name re-pin

**(a) The threshold.** The rule keeps a row's interval when the
central difference moves it along the NaN parameter by at most
`16 * eps * max(|g|, 1)`. I measured both sides of the threshold
(`rev072-gap.R`):

- On the 5 three-dimensional `us` fits whose correlation parameters
  were lost as "nocurv" (seeds 2, 4, 8, 10 and 11 of `rev072-ulp.R`),
  the rows that do not depend on the lost parameter moved by 0 to
  0.5 ulps. The rows that do moved by 8.3e9 to 6.8e10 ulps.
- The sd rows of `us` blocks of dimension 4, 5 and 6, against a
  correlation parameter at +/-3000, over 200 random points each,
  moved by at most 1.0 ulp.

So 16 sits in a gap of ten decades. The threshold is not tuned to a
test: any value from 2 to 1e9 gives the same verdicts. It is a
rounding allowance, the right kind of test.

The rule can be wrong only for a row that depends on the lost
parameter with a zero first derivative at the stop. That is the delta
method's own blind spot. In the `us` chart, `d r32 / d t21 = (t31 -
t21 t32) / (n2^3 n3)`, which is zero only on the surface t31 = t21
t32. I could not reach it with a "nocurv" loss.

BLAS: `gfun()` multiplies 3 x 3 matrices, where a BLAS changes the
last ulp, and the gap above is 9 decades wider than that. The
release's OpenBLAS 0.3.26 and 0.3.32 runs pass `test-review-v29.R`.

**Release against 0.71.0 on the same 24 fits** (`rev072-ulp.R`, logs
`ulp-rel.txt` and `ulp-r9.txt`; 12 mvbf fits and 12 three-dimensional
fits, all with the correlation run toward 1). On 0.71.0, four of the
fits reported zero-width correlation intervals, such as 0.738455
[0.738455, 0.738455]. The release reports NA on the three of them that
lose a parameter as "nocurv". The fourth, seed 7, is filed below. The sd intervals
keep 6 digits of 0.71.0's. The kept sd rows read the marginal block
`V[kb, kb]` of an indefinite inverse. On the record's case that block
gives an SE of 0.2120 on the log scale, and the converged boundary fit
(t0 = 500) gives 0.2177. The conditional block would give 0.409. So
the marginal block is the better choice.

**Filed, not this rule:** seed 7 of the three-dimensional design (a
code-1 stop, theta_6 = -294.6, nothing lost) gives `cor(x,z)` an
interval of [-0.122416, -0.122416] with `se_t` exactly 0, on 0.71.0
and on the release. A restart at theta_6 = -30 is 1.3e-4 lower in
logLik and reports NA for both affected correlations
(`rev072-seed7.R`). This is 0.71.0's filed flat-ridge item. A cheap
guard would treat `se_t == 0` on a row that is not at its boundary as
NA.

**(b) `test-draws-sd-names.R`.** brms 2.23.0, `y ~ x + (1 + x | g) + (1
| h)`, rstan backend, one chain (probe P11): `variables()` gives
`b_Intercept b_x sd_g__Intercept sd_g__x sd_h__Intercept
cor_g__Intercept__x sigma Intercept lprior lp__`. frmtmb.sample's
draws (P7b) give `... sd_g__Intercept sd_g__x sd_h__Intercept
cor_g__Intercept__x lp__`. The re-pinned order (every `sd_`, then
every `cor_`) is brms's. The draws put `sigma` and `theta_*` before
the `sd_` columns, where brms puts `sigma` after them. The test does
not pin that, and the NEWS says the derived columns are added after
the sampled ones.
