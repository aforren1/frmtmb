## Verdict: MERGEABLE AFTER FIXES

The two root causes are right, and the nested quadrature route is
exact. I checked it against my own iterated integral on five models
(Bernoulli, weighted Poisson, weighted cumulative, a random slope on
the inner level, and `(1 | g)` in `mu` and `sigma`). The
log-likelihood agrees to between 1.7e-8 and 1.0e-5, and the standard
errors agree with a numerical Hessian of the exact integral to 1e-5.
Single-block quadrature is unchanged. The new tests fail on
rellib-r10 for the reasons they name.

Three defects block the merge:

1. The weights refusal fires on two models whose likelihood is
   bounded (finding 1). brms fits one of them cleanly.
2. `quad_passes()` orders the passes by level count, not by nesting.
   A by-split inner factor then gets a false "crossed" refusal or a
   tape of 5 GB on 12 rows (finding 2).
3. Memory for the new route grows without a bound that the depth rule
   controls. Depth 2 uses 12 to 19 MB per row for Bernoulli and 551 MB
   per row for a censored Beta. On the trial merge with cdfs, the
   gated fuzz spec Beta + cens + nested quadrature (seed 20535438)
   passed 21 GB before I stopped it (findings 3 and 4).

## Findings

### 1. HIGH: the weights refusal fires on bounded models

`check_weights_unbounded()` asks only that the spike dpar is "free (not
constant)". The proof also needs the dpar's predictor to reach its
limit at every row at once. That holds when the dpar has an
intercept. It fails for an intercept-free dispersion formula and for
an offset-only one. `dev/fuzzfix-rev-wu.R`, seed 20261021, n = 80,
sum(w) - n = 19.80 (`wu-{base,lane}.txt`):

    FP1 sigma ~ 0 + xs      base ok logLik -137.731 | betad 0.5282 | lane REFUSED "has no maximum"
    FP2 sigma ~ 0 + offset  base ok logLik -143.083                | lane REFUSED "has no maximum"
    sigma = 0.7 (constant)  base ok logLik -143.083                | lane ok -143.083

FP2 has no free dispersion parameter. Its fit equals the constant-
sigma fit to the printed digits. For FP1, the gaussian marginal with
one effect per row has a closed form. The same script maximizes it
from five starts. All five end at -137.7312 with gamma 0.52823, and
the profile falls on both sides (gamma = -50: -1346.77; +50:
-2083.24). frmtmb's base ML fit is that maximum. brms fits FP1 with
b_sigma_xs 0.505 (SD 0.097), max Rhat 1.004 (`wu-brms.txt`). The NEWS
bullet's claim ("a family with a free dispersion grows without bound")
is false for both models.

Fix: require that `rep(1, n)` lies in the column space of the spike
dpar's fixed-effect design, with a relative residual of `qr.resid()`
below a ratio tolerance, and that the design has at least one column.
Fail open otherwise. Add FP1 and FP2 to the "does not fire" test, and
see them fail on the lane as it is now.

### 2. HIGH: pass order follows level count, not nesting

`quad_passes()` runs the block "with the most levels first", as a
proxy for "finest first". A by-split inner factor breaks the proxy:
each split block has fewer levels than the outer block, so the outer
block is integrated first. `dev/fuzzfix-rev-quad-shapes.R` arm
`by_split_inner4` is `y ~ x + (1 | ga) + (1 | gr(cellu, by = f4))`.
Here `cellu` is nested in `ga` and `f4` is constant within a cell.
This is the shape of plots in blocks with a per-treatment plot
variance. The lane gives (`shape-by_split_inner4-lane*.txt`):

    ng 2 (12 rows): ok, 18.6 s, peak 5053 MB   (plain nested on the same rows: 1.4 s, 549 MB)
    ng 4 (24 rows): REFUSED "the blocks ... are crossed" (they are nested), 19.6 s, 2873 MB
    ng 8 (48 rows): killed at the 6000 MB cap

With the passes ordered inner blocks first (`dev/fuzzfix-rev-order.R`,
an `assignInNamespace()` in that process only, `order-*.txt`):

    ng 2: ok 1.9 s, 439 MB, logLik -5.60837559 (the lane's logLik, same digits)
    ng 4: ok 4.7 s, 534 MB, left_random 0
    ng 8: ok 12.1 s, 694 MB, left_random 0

So the defect is the order. `quad_check_depth()` counts two blocks per
row here and does not see the effective depth of 3 or more. Fix: order
the passes by nesting, not by level count. The smallest rows-per-level
first works here. A pairwise nesting test on the `Z` columns of the
blocks is the general form. Add the ng = 4 arm as a test (it fails on
the lane now).

| 24 | 3 | 144 | 33.25 s | 13.08 s | 2613 | 18.1 |
| 48 | 3 | 288 | 62.81 s | 28.91 s | 5590 | 19.4 |

docs give only time ("27 s on 72 rows"), not memory. See the
recommendation on item 1.

### 5. MEDIUM: the refusal's advice does not work, and the prior exemption is too wide

The message (`dev/fuzzfix-rev-msg.R`, `msg.txt`) ends: "Rescale the
weights to sum to less than the number of rows, drop the
observation-level term, or put a prior on sigma". Measured on the lane
(`wu-lane.txt`):

    prior_sigma_t      student_t(3, 0, 2.5) on sigma  ok logLik 533.348 | log sigma -36.83
    prior_sigma_gamma2 gamma(2, 1) on sigma           ok logLik 500.951 | log sigma -36.79
    prior_b_only       normal(0, 5) on b              ok logLik 567.274 | log sigma -36.81
    prior_shape_gamma  gamma(0.01, 0.01) on shape     ok logLik -92.5793 | log shape 6.899

A prior on sigma bounds the MAP objective only if its density goes to
0 faster than sigma^(sum(w) - n). Neither common prior does this, and
the lane fails open for any prior, including one on `b` only. A
gamma(0.01, 0.01) prior on shape does bound it, but only at a log shape
of 6.9, which is a prior-dominated value. "Rescale the weights to sum
to less than the number of rows" asks the user to change the
inference by an arbitrary factor. Also, the message names the
`ar1(tt + 0 | g)` term as "`tt + 0 | g`", without its wrapper.

Fix: remove the prior advice, or state the condition. Advise
dropping the observation-level term, and explain that a weight
above 1 counts a row more than once, so an effect of its own then
stands for replicates with no residual variance. Print the term as
the user wrote it. Keep the fail-open for priors, but do not say
in NEWS that a prior is the remedy.

### 6. MEDIUM: frmtmb.sample skips the check on a claim that is false for its default priors

The lane skips the check for `dry_run = "objective"` because "the
sampler's own prior can bound what the likelihood does not".
`dev/fuzzfix-rev-wu-sample.R` (`wu-sample-lane.txt`, 2 chains of
1000, default priors):

    sample sum(w) > n        sigma q05/50/95 5.71e-05 6.21e-05 0.000106 max rhat 2.497
    sample sum(w) < n        sigma q05/50/95 0.824 1.12 1.32 max rhat 1.024
    fit route, ML fit first  ERROR The weighted likelihood of this model has no maximum ...

The posterior is improper. Near 0 it goes like sigma^-(sum(w) - n)
times a prior that is finite at 0, so it diverges for an excess of 1
or more. It is not a false positive, but the comment is wrong, and the
formula route returns draws that have not mixed. brms behaves the same
way (Rhat 2.27). Fix: run the check on the formula route too, and
fail open only when a user prior is on the spike dpar. Or warn with
the same text. Correct the comment.

## Item 1: recommendation on quadrature over several blocks

**Measured.** The iterated route is exact (the table above), and on
small data it is the only exact nested AGQ among lme4, glmmTMB and
GLMMadaptive. Its cost is set by TMB's non-adaptive GK rule. Time and
memory are linear in rows at a constant about 20 times (time) to 30
times (memory) that of one block for Bernoulli, and about 30 times
more for a censored Beta. The block count limit does not bound that
cost (findings 3 and 4). The level-count order adds a further blowup
(finding 2). lme4 refuses nAGQ > 1 for more than one scalar term, and
glmmTMB has no AGQ.

**Options.**

- (a) Keep the lane as it is. Reject this: memory is unbounded on a
  shared machine, and the merged gated plan cannot run.
- (b) Refuse more than one block, as lme4 does, and keep
  `importance` as the route for blocks over one factor. This is safe
  and has a precedent, but it drops a correct capability that the
  compat row promised.
- (c) Keep the exact route. Fix the pass order (finding 2). Replace
  `quad_check_depth()` with a size guard: build the Laplace tape,
  read its operation count per row, multiply by the measured factor
  for the depth (calibrate it from the table above and the
  censored-Beta point), and refuse above a budget, for example
  `frmtmb_control(quad_max_gb = 4)`, with the prediction in the
  message. Keep depth 3 refused. Its 49 GB at 32 rows makes it useless
  at any size.

**Recommendation: (c) in this lane if the order fix and the guard can
be measured this round. Otherwise (b) now, with (c) filed.** (c)
alone keeps the capability and makes the refusal a statement about
cost that the user can read and change. Either way, the docs state
the memory per row.

## Item 2: recommendation on the unbounded-weights refusal

**Measured.** In the cases it targets the refusal is right, and the
alternative is a NaN gradient or a logLik of 1e11. glmmTMB and brms
both fail silently on the same models. The derivation behind it holds
for every family in the table. For a location-scale density the row
factor is bounded below by c sigma^(1 - w), and the shape families
concentrate the same way. It holds when three conditions hold: (i)
the dispersion can reach its limit at every row at once, (ii) each
row's mean can reach its observation, and (iii) the effects' prior
density is positive and finite there. The lane checks (ii) and (iii)
but checks (i) only as "not constant", which causes finding 1.

**Recommendation: keep it, with three changes before merge.**

1. Add condition (i): the constant lies in the span of the spike
   dpar's design, and the design has a free column (finding 1).
2. Fix the message: no prior advice that does not work, and the term
   as the user wrote it (finding 5).
3. Check the `frm_sample()` formula route too, or warn there, and
   correct the comment (finding 6).

Keep the fail-open cases (prior, `se()`, `cens()`, several responses,
`mi()`, residual correlation). A missed refusal leaves today's
behavior. A wrong refusal blocks a valid model.

The stated reason is right. For an owned effect,
`h(u) = w log f(y | u) + log N(u; 0, tau^2)` is piecewise linear plus
the prior. The mode sits at the kink, or on a linear piece, and in both
places the Hessian the Laplace approximation uses is `1 / tau^2`. The
approximation is then `exp(h(u_hat)) sqrt(2 pi) tau`, which grows like
`sigma^-w` per row and like `sigma^-sum(w)` over all rows, for any
weights. With several rows per level the other rows' residuals keep
`h(u_hat)` going to minus infinity, which is why the group arms,
including partly singleton groups, are bounded and are correctly not
refused.
