### 0.1 The `NaN` gradient: the justification is wrong

The lane's `?frm_lincmt` "Boundary" section says the `NaN` is correct:

> at a double root the eigenvalues have a square-root branch point, so
> the derivative with respect to any parameter that SPLITS the root is
> genuinely unbounded

and `dev/lincmt-findings.md` adds "the concentration is smooth there
only because the eigenvalue and coefficient derivatives cancel, and
that cancellation cannot happen once one factor is `Inf`."

**The branch point is in the EIGENVALUES and it is not in what
`frm_lincmt()` returns.** The central compartment's impulse response is
a symmetric function of the three roots, hence a function of the
characteristic polynomial's coefficients, which are polynomials in the
rate constants. The residue formula's poles at a double root are
removable. So the trajectory is analytic in the rate constants AT the
tangency and its derivative there is finite. Measured three ways
(`dev/rev-lincmt-f6.R`), at the lane's own construction, `k13 = 1e-300`
with `k31` exactly on the slow root:

| quantity | value |
|---|---|
| tape gradient | `NaN`, `NaN`, 3.5969724 |
| central difference, h = 1e-4 | -9.2779361, 1.95e-10, 3.5969724 |
| central difference, h = 1e-5 | -9.2779361, -1.95e-09, 3.5969724 |
| central difference, h = 1e-6 | -9.2779361, 0, 3.5969724 |
| the tape at dk = 1e-8 | -9.2779361, -2.32e-08, 3.5969724 |
| 300-bit reference, step 1e-30, d/d(log k31) | 0 |

Two components are `NaN`, not one, and `d/d(log ke)` is **-9.2779361**,
stable to eight significant digits across three decades of step size
and equal to what the tape itself returns one part in 1e8 away. A
derivative that a central difference resolves to eight digits is not
unbounded. The lane's own `dev/lincmt/lincmt-f6.R` printed the evidence
against its conclusion: its "FD spread" column, the central
difference's own step sensitivity, reads **9.573e-10** at the tangency,
which is the column saying the difference knows its own answer there.

**And the padding it rejected costs nothing at that point.** The
findings say "padding the `acos()` argument away from one perturbs the
eigenvalues by the square root of the padding and would cost the 5e-13
the value currently holds". That is asserted, not measured. Measured
(`dev/rev-lincmt-f6c.R` section C), holding `arg` below `1 - d`:

| clamp `d` | gradient | value error / scale vs `frm_ode()` at 1e-12 |
|---|---|---|
| shipped | `NaN NaN` 3.59697 | 5.181e-13 |
| 1e-12 | -9.27794, -2.89e-11, 3.59697 | 8.977e-12 |
| 1e-14 | -9.27794, -7.80e-11, 3.59697 | **5.177e-13** |
| 1e-16 | -9.27794, -4.32e-09, 3.59697 | 5.175e-13 |

At `d = 1e-14` the gradient is finite and right and the value error is
5.177e-13 against the shipped 5.181e-13. The padding costs nothing
because it perturbs the two colliding roots SYMMETRICALLY and the
trajectory is a symmetric function of them.

So the justification in `?frm_lincmt` and in the findings is false as
applied to `frm_lincmt()`'s output, and the fix that was rejected
without measurement works at the point the section is about.

The findings call it "a bit-level knife edge rather than a

**In that wider region the VALUE is wrong, and wrong where nothing
fires.** `dev/rev-lincmt-f6e.R`, seed 4242, 25 draws from a ten-decade
box that make the gradient `NaN` and 25 from the same box that do not,

the trajectory's own maximum:

| set | median | worst |
|---|---|---|
| gradient `NaN` | 5.12e-07 | 2.87e-03 |
| gradient FINITE, the control | | **1.33e-04** |

The control is the finding. Where the gradient is finite, nothing warns
and the fit proceeds, and the value is wrong by up to 1.3e-04 of the
trajectory's own scale, four decades outside the item's 1e-8 bar. The
`NaN` is a symptom of a neighbourhood in which the cubic's two small
roots have lost their precision, not a boundary that guards a right
answer.

That also settles the padding: `dev/rev-lincmt-f6d.R` section B applied
the clamp to 40 such draws. It made every gradient finite and changed
the VALUE by a factor of 2.8e+02 to 2.5e+06 on them, while changing
nothing at all (0.000e+00) on 400 ordinary draws. So the clamp is free
where the collision is genuine and destructive where the roots were
already garbage. **It is a fix for section 0.1 and not for 0.2.**

**Where the region begins, and where it does not.** The 300-bit

`dev/rev-lincmt-f6g.R` (seed 1234, 60 draws per cell) uses `frm_ode()`

| 1e-12 | 6.91e-11 | 6.97e-12 | 7.00e-08 |

**It does not degrade at all.** Driving the peripheral rate constants

reachability answer, by construction in the direction that matters
rather than by "no test reaches it".

### 0.4 What the right behaviour is

**Not the clamp, on its own.** The clamp is the right diagnosis of
0.1 and the wrong prescription for the file, because the same two
tokens that cost nothing at a genuine double root are catastrophic in
0.2's region: 2.8e+02 to 2.5e+06 of the trajectory's own peak on the
40 draws measured. Telling the two apart needs a test on whether `pp`
and `qq` still carry relative precision, which is a comparison on an
AD value, which is the constraint this whole file is written around.
So a safe fix is real work and not a punch-round edit. I say that
plainly because the round 1 review's job is not to hand back a
one-liner that trades a loud failure for a silent one.

What I would do, in order of what it buys:

1. **Correct the two sentences that are false.** `?frm_lincmt`'s "the
   derivative with respect to any parameter that SPLITS the root is
   genuinely unbounded" is false of what the function returns: the
   derivative is -9.2779361 and a central difference resolves it to
   eight digits. The findings' "padding the `acos()` argument ... would
   cost the 5e-13 the value currently holds" is false by measurement,
   5.177e-13 against 5.181e-13. Both are load-bearing, because they are
   the reasons given for not fixing it.
2. **Say what the `NaN` actually means, and what the accurate domain
   is.** A user reading the Boundary section today learns that a `NaN`
   means a double root. Measured, it far more often means the rate
   constants are eight or more decades apart, and that the value
   nearby is already wrong by up to 1.3e-04 of the trajectory's scale
   with nothing firing. Two sentences would carry it: no `NaN` and no
   degradation above the solver's own tolerance was found while the
   rate constants span six decades or fewer, including with the
   peripheral rates twelve decades below the absorption rate; beyond
   an eight-decade spread that includes the FAST rates, both appear,
   and there `frm_ode()` is not a usable reference either.
3. **Do not build a runtime check for it.** I considered recommending
   one, on the numeric path where the spread is data, and the
   measurement in 0.2 argues against it: the direction an optimizer
   travels is clean to 6.9e-11, so a check would fire only on
   parameter vectors that are not drugs. A stated domain is the
   proportionate answer.
4. **Leave the tape-side fix as an item if anyone wants it**, with the
   measurement above as its check. It is not urgent on this
   evidence.

### 0.11 What a raised DEFAULT should be. My read

Asked for, and neither of us had settled it. My answer is that
**raising the constant is the wrong lever, and the lever that is right
costs less than the status quo on the common case.**

**Why the constant is the wrong lever, with its cost.** On the Phase 0
design the solve count is 33 per subject per evaluation, 20 of them the
run-in and 13 the dosing segments, so the cost of `n_ss` is
`(13 + n) / 33` of 3717 s. Coverage at a 1e-8 shortfall needs
`n * lambda_z * ii >= 18.4`:

| `n_ss` | Phase 0 cost | covers a terminal half-life up to |
|---|---|---|
| 20, today | 3717 s | 0.75 dosing intervals |
| 50 | 7100 s | 1.9 intervals |
| 100 | 12700 s | 3.8 intervals |
| 266 | 31400 s | 10 intervals |
| 307 | 36000 s | 11.6 intervals, the 139 h q12 case |

To cover the models that motivate the item at all, a blind constant
costs an order of magnitude, and it charges it to every `ss` fit
including the large majority that never needed it. No constant is both
correct and affordable, which is why "raise the default" has no good
answer.

**The lever that works.** `n_ss` has to be fixed before the tape is
built. It does NOT have to be fixed blind: the starting values are
known then, and `ode_run_in()` already computes, on the numeric path,
exactly the quantity needed. The contraction ratio

    r = |y[n] - y[n-1]| / |y[n-1] - y[n-2]|

estimates `exp(-lambda_z * ii)` for any system with a dominant mode,
without knowing the model, which matters because `frm_ode()` takes an
arbitrary `dyn`. So:

- default `n_ss = "auto"`: at tape-build time run the run-in
  numerically at the starting parameters for about five cycles per
  group, form `r`, take
  `n_ss = ceiling(log(1e-9) / log(max(r)))` over groups, floor it at a
  handful and cap it at a budget;
- if the cap binds, WARN once, with the number:
  "this schedule needs `n_ss = 2100` to reach 1e-09; it is capped at
  500, which leaves the steady state 1.2e-02 short. Raise `n_ss`,
  or use `frm_lincmt()` for a linear model." That converts the silent
  wrong answer into a loud one, which is Rule 3's ordering, and it is
  the whole point of the item;
- keep any whole number working exactly as it does now.

**Its cost, and why it is negative on the common case.** Five numeric
solves per group, once, at tape build, against a fit of thousands of
evaluations. And where 20 is more than enough it picks FEWER: the
Phase 0 design has `ke * ii = 1.8`, so `r = 0.165` and
`log(1e-9) / log(0.165) = 11.5`, giving `n_ss = 12` against today's 20.
That is 25 solves per evaluation instead of 33, so that fit gets about
**24 percent faster** while becoming correct by construction. The
models that need 300 cycles pay for 300 cycles; the models that need
12 stop paying for 20.

**What I have not measured**, and it is the design's own risk: the
parameters move during the fit, so an `n_ss` chosen at the starting
values can be too small at the optimum. Two mitigations, neither
measured here: choose the tolerance with headroom (1e-9 rather than
1e-8), and repeat the numeric check at the converged estimates, where
`diagnose()` already has somewhere to put the answer. A lane taking
1.0d should measure how far `r` moves between the start and the optimum
on the designs in `dev/scale-findings.md` before choosing the headroom.

**The POINTWISE relative error is unbounded.** It reaches 1.0, that is

a region whose pointwise error grows without limit.

the absolute error is fixed at eps of the peak, and the pointwise
relative error is that divided by how far below the peak the point sits.

crossed only beyond seven decades below `Cmax`. No assay reports there,
and the row would be below the limit of quantification long before.

This is a stronger statement than the findings make and it is the one
the record should carry. The findings say the error is bounded "at a
point I can explain and left deliberately"; what is true is that the
error is bounded in the measure that matters and unbounded in the
measure that does not, and which is which can be said in one line.

### F6 (NIT): the gradient sweep does not contain the coalescence it names

`dev/lincmt-findings.md` says the 72 gradient cases include a point
"where EVERY rate constant is exactly equal, so that every pair of them
coalesces at once". For a three-compartment mammillary model that is
not a coalescence of the DISPOSITION: with
`ke = k12 = k21 = k13 = k31 = r` the characteristic polynomial is
`L^3 - 5 r L^2 + 5 r^2 L - r^3 = (L - r)(L^2 - 4 r L + r^2)` and the
eigenvalues are `r`, `(2 - sqrt 3) r` and `(2 + sqrt 3) r`, which are
distinct. Confirmed against the code: at `r = 0.3`, `lincmt_disp()`
returns 1.119615242, 0.300000000 and 0.080384758 with coefficients
0.788675, -1.7e-32 and 0.211325. The mode at `r` carries nothing,
because `k21 == k31` puts a zero of the numerator on it. What that
point does coalesce is `ka` with one eigenvalue, which is the
`lincmt_diff()` singularity and IS the one being tested. The
eigenvalue-eigenvalue coalescence is not in the sweep, and
`lincmt_disp()`'s cubic goes through `acos()`, whose derivative is
infinite where a double root puts its argument.

**The gap is real and the code survives it.** `dev/rev-lincmt-grad.R`
sections C and D drive `k13` from 1e-2 to exactly 0 with `k31` pinned
on the slow root of the reduced quadratic, which forces a genuine
double root:

| `k13` | eigenvalue split | finite gradient | rel vs central difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1e-2 | 3.4e-2 | TRUE | 3.87e-10 |
| 1e-6 | 1.1e-4 | TRUE | 2.28e-10 |
| 1e-14 | 1.1e-8 | TRUE | 4.88e-10 |
| 1e-300 | 2.8e-16 | TRUE | 2.52e-09 |
| 0 exactly | 2.8e-16 | TRUE | 2.52e-09 |

and the VALUE there is right too: `dev/rev-lincmt-degen.R` section A
puts `frm_lincmt()` within **4.34e-16 of the peak** of the 300-bit
reference at `k13 = 0` with `k31` exactly on the double root, over an
eight-point grid, where `frm_ode()` at `atol = rtol = 1e-12` is within
7.7e-12. The reason it survives is structural rather than lucky: the
identity `(l2 - k31)/(l2 - l3) + (l3 - k31)/(l3 - l2) = 1` makes the
SUM of the two colliding coefficients exact whatever the split, and
interlacing bounds `|l - k31|` by the split so neither coefficient can
grow. Sweeping `k31` across the resonance (section D) and reaching the
same collision from `k12` (section E) stay at 3.8e-14 pointwise and
2.1e-15 of the peak.

## 5. `n_ss`, and my severity judgment

**S1. This should be its own item, and the two reasons the findings
give for it not being one both fail on measurement.**

### The shortfall is unbounded, not 0.4 percent

`dev/rev-lincmt-nss.R` section A. Two compartments, oral, one `ss` row,
`frm_lincmt(n_ss = 20)` against `n_ss = Inf`, which is exactly the
truncation `frm_ode()` performs, worst over one dosing interval
relative to the trajectory's scale:

| `t_half_z` | `ii` | `lam_z ii` | `n_ss = 20` | 40 | 80 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 23.2 | 8 | 0.239 | 3.94e-03 | 3.32e-05 | 2.37e-09 |
| 42.5 | 12 | 0.196 | 5.12e-03 | 1.02e-04 | 4.09e-08 |
| 107.1 | 24 | 0.155 | 1.18e-02 | 5.29e-04 | 1.06e-06 |
| 264.6 | 24 | 0.063 | **9.02e-02** | 2.57e-02 | 2.08e-03 |
| 669.9 | 24 | 0.025 | **2.09e-01** | 1.27e-01 | 4.72e-02 |

and one compartment, `ii = 12` (section B): a half-life of 69 h is
8.3e-02 short and 139 h is **2.89e-01** short. The 3.96e-03 the lane
reports (reproduced exactly, 0.00396) is the benign end of this table,
not a typical value. The shortfall is of order
`exp(-n_ss lambda_z ii)` times the share of the trajectory the slowest
mode carries, so it goes to 1 as the drug's terminal half-life grows
past a few dosing intervals. Monoclonal antibodies, amiodarone and
bisphosphonates all live in the bottom rows.

### The documentation is wrong by an order of magnitude

`?frm_ode`'s "Steady-state dosing" says today:

> At the default `n_ss = 20` that is `1e-21` for a drug eliminated over
> its dosing interval and only a percent or two for one whose half-life
> is many intervals long.

A half-life of ten dosing intervals is `k ii = 0.0693`, so the
shortfall after twenty cycles is `exp(-1.386) = 0.25`. **Twenty-five
percent, not a percent or two.** The sentence holds at a half-life of
about three intervals, not "many".

### The warning the user is told to read understates the error

`ode_run_in()` compares the last two cycle-start states. After `n`
cycles the distance to the limit is `r^n` and the movement it reports
is `r^(n-1)(1 - r)`, `r = exp(-lambda ii)`, so the ratio is
`(1 - r)/r`, about `lambda ii`. Measured
(`dev/rev-lincmt-nsswarn.R`, one compartment, `ka = 1`, `ii = 12`,
`n_ss = 20`, `frm_ode()` at `atol = rtol = 1e-12`):

| half-life | `k ii` | warning prints | true shortfall | understatement |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 13.9 | 0.60 | 5.05e-06 | 4.02e-06 | 0.8x |
| 34.7 | 0.24 | 0.00225 | 0.00695 | 3.1x |
| 69.3 | 0.12 | 0.0127 | 0.0834 | 6.6x |
| 138.6 | 0.06 | 0.0267 | 0.2888 | **10.8x** |

It fires, which is to its credit, and it understates the error by more
and more exactly as the error grows.

### The bias moves estimates, and by a lot

`dev/rev-lincmt-nss.R` section C. Simulate from the EXACT steady state,
then fit the same data twice through `frm_lincmt()`, once at
`n_ss = Inf` and once at `n_ss = 20`, so the only difference between
the two fits is the run-in truncation `frm_ode()` performs. Two
compartments oral, terminal half-life 107 h, `ii = 24`, 30 subjects,
random effects on `lke` and `lka`, seed 101:

| parameter | `n_ss = Inf` | `n_ss = 20` | on the natural scale |
|---|---|---|---|
| `lke` | -2.32577 | -2.44205 | `ke` -11.0 percent |
| `lka` | -0.03498 | -0.05452 | `ka` -1.9 percent |
| `lk12` | -1.62225 | -1.53860 | `k12` +8.7 percent |
| `lk21` | -5.54547 | -4.39225 | **`k21` x 3.16** |
| `lV` | 2.30919 | 2.28177 | `V` -2.7 percent |
| logLik | -20.8337 | -22.4216 | 1.59 units |

On the package's own fast schedule (`ke = 0.2`, `ii = 8`, seed 102)
everything moves by under 0.3 percent, which is why the lane did not
see this.

One seed and one dataset, so this is a demonstration that the bias is
large enough to move a structural parameter by a factor of three, not a
recovery study.

### Verdict on `n_ss`

It is a silent wrong answer in Rule 3's sense. During a fit no check
can run, the bias is systematic and the same direction in every group,
and its size is set by a quantity the user is not told to compute. The
findings decline to open an item because it "is documented and the
numeric path warns". The documentation understates it by an order of
magnitude and the warning understates it by up to an order of
magnitude, so neither reason survives. It is also not moot for a
nonlinear or non-compartmental system, which is precisely where
`frm_lincmt()` cannot serve and `frm_ode()` is the only path.

**Open it as its own item.** It is NOT a blocker for merging this
branch: it is pre-existing in `frm_ode()`, this lane neither caused it
nor worsened it, and finding it is to the lane's credit.

**What a user of `frm_ode()` with `ss` rows should be told today**, in
`?frm_ode` and in the vignette:

- `n_ss = 20` is enough only when the SLOWEST disposition eigenvalue
  times `ii` is above about 0.2, that is when the terminal half-life is
  under about three dosing intervals. Correct the "a percent or two"
  sentence, which is wrong by an order of magnitude.
- Choose `n_ss` so that `n_ss * lambda_z * ii` is at least 20, which
  puts the shortfall at 2e-9. On the 107 h, `ii = 24` design that is
  `n_ss = 130`, not 20.
- Do NOT choose it from the warning. The warning reports the
  cycle-to-cycle movement, which understates the distance to the limit
  by about `1 / (lambda_z ii)`. Compare `n_ss` against `2 n_ss`
  numerically instead, or fit the linear case with `frm_lincmt()`,
  whose default has nothing to truncate.

## 8. The three shipped-documentation defects

These are the only findings I would hold a merge for, and each is a
one-line edit.

**F1 (BLOCKER, documentation).** `vignettes/ode.Rmd` says the Phase 0
design "takes **57.3 s**". That number appears nowhere else in the
repository and matches no measured arm: the record has 34.8 s and
55.0 s at `n_ss = Inf` and 118.5 s and 119.1 s at `n_ss = 20`. A
shipped figure with no construction behind it is the thing the lane
rules exist to prevent.

**F2 (BLOCKER, documentation).** The same vignette says of the two-
compartment schedule "twenty cycles are still 0.2 percent short". The
lane's own record says 3.96e-03, and I measure **0.00396** on exactly
the schedule the vignette names (`dev/rev-lincmt-doc.R`). 0.2 percent
is the CYCLE-TO-CYCLE MOVEMENT, 0.0023, which `dev/lincmt-findings.md`
explicitly separates from the distance to the limit: "Two numbers, not
one". The vignette conflates them, and it conflates them in the
direction that makes the defect look half its size.

**F10 (NIT, documentation).** The same paragraph says `frm_lincmt()`
"returns the same estimates to every digit". The kept log has `ka`
0.9841612 through the closed form against 0.9841609 through the solver,
which agree to seven digits, not to every digit. The findings state it
correctly; the vignette rounds the statement up.

**F3 (NIT, documentation).** `?frm_lincmt`'s "Accuracy" section quotes
"the worst relative error through every coalescence down to exact
equality is 7.2e-15" and does not quote the 7.07e-11 from the wide
parameter box, which NEWS does quote. A reader takes the 7.2e-15 as the
global worst. Better: state the bound measured in section 1, which is
both true everywhere and more useful, namely that the error is a few
parts in 1e11 of the trajectory's own maximum and the pointwise
relative error at a point `10^-d` of `Cmax` is at most about that times
`10^d`.
