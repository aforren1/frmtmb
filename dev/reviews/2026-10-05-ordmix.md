# Review of lane ordmix (2026-10-05)

Worktree `frmtmb-wt-ordmix`, branch `wt-ordmix`, uncommitted against
9e902909 (frmtmb 0.67.0). Lane build `wt-ordmix-lib`, base build
`rellib-r5`. Reference brms 2.23.0, run, not recalled. Every script and
log named here is in `dev/` of the worktree, prefixed `ordmix-rev-`.
The copy of the Stan cache (`dev/ordmix-rev-stan-cache`) was removed
after the gated runs.

## Verdict: NOT MERGEABLE

The likelihood and the post-fit arithmetic are right. On 16 shapes, 11
of them new (three components, mixed links, `disc2 ~ z`, `mu2 ~ x +
cs(z)`, weights, offsets, unequal per-group threshold counts under both
orders, a hurdle mixture with `hu1 ~ z`, `hu2 ~ x`, `disc1 ~ 0 + z`),
the log density equals brms's compiled program to at most 1.6 ulp
wherever both are finite. Plain ordinal fits are unchanged to the bit
on 484 outputs of 44 models. Both base defects are real on rellib-r5
and fixed. Two things block the merge:

1. Two of the three new flat-direction warnings fire on identified
   models, 60 times in 60 fits.
2. Degenerate maximum-likelihood fits are common (22 of 160 default
   two-component fits on the lane's own data-generating process) and
   mostly silent (15 of 22 give no warning at fit time). The record
   does not show this. One three-component fit stops at an infinite
   gradient, reports convergence, and gives no warning.

## Blockers

### B1. The flat-direction warnings fire on identified models

Script `dev/ordmix-rev-falsealarm.R`, driver `dev/ordmix-rev-fa.sh`,
logs `dev/ordmix-rev-fa-log/<model>.txt`. Seeds 1..20, n = 500, every
warning recorded, nothing suppressed.

(a) The shared-threshold warning (`mixture_ord_ident_check()`, third
branch) tests `order = "mu"`, no `mu<k>` predictor and every `disc<k>`
held. It does not test whether the components are the same
distribution. Components of different families, or of one family with
different links, are different distributions at shared thresholds, so
`theta` is in the likelihood. The data have `theta1` moving with `z`:

    model                                         warned  SEs finite and < 5
    mixture(cumulative(), sratio(), "mu"),
      bf(ysh ~ 1, theta1 ~ z)                     20/20   16/20
    mixture(cumulative(), cumulative("probit"),
      "mu"), bf(ysh ~ 1, theta1 ~ z)              20/20   16/20
    control: mixture(cumulative(), cumulative(),
      "mu"), same formula (truly flat)            20/20    0/20

The warning text says "the components are one distribution and the
mixing weights are not in the likelihood". That is false for the first
two rows. The control shows the guard is right only when the
components are identical (same family, same link, same held `disc`).

(b) The hurdle warning (second branch) does not read the priors. With
the priors the warning itself recommends, `set_prior("beta(4, 16)",
class = "hu1")` and `set_prior("beta(8, 12)", class = "hu2")`, the fit
is identified (all standard errors finite and below 5 in 18 of 20), and
the warning fires in 20 of 20. The first branch (no predictor
anywhere) has the same shape: it counts `length(fit$opt$par)` and reads
no prior (by reading, not measured). `ord_fit_check()` already has the
pattern to copy: its `pinned` test skips the warning when a prior
entry holds the flat direction.

What holds: none of the three warnings fired on 7 models x 20 seeds of
identified designs with predictors (`cum2`, `probit2`, `cum_sr_clog`,
`mu2`, `hurdle_huz`, `sr_acat_th`, `gr_cum2`) or on the plain controls
(`plain_cum`, `plain_probit`: 0 warnings of any kind in 40 fits). No
plain ordinal model can reach them: the check hangs on the mixture
family's `post$fit_check` only, and the 67 ordinal test files give
identical counts on both builds (below).

Required: the third branch tests that the components are identical;
the first two skip when a prior entry holds `hu<k>`, `theta<k>` or the
thresholds; one test per absent case (different families, different
links, priors on `hu<k>`).

### B2. Degenerate fits are common, mostly silent, and missing from the record

Script `dev/ordmix-rev-degen.R`, logs `dev/ordmix-rev-degen-log/`. The
lane's own data-generating process of `dev/ordmix-ident.R` (mode
"none"), seeds 1..40, default start. "Degenerate" means any
`|estimate| > 30` or a non-finite standard error. Each degenerate fit
is compared with the best of 6 other starts (seed `500 + s`):

    model, n          degenerate  warned at fit  degenerate above best
                                                 non-degenerate start
    cum + cum, 300       5/40          2              4
    cum + cum, 500       5/40          1              3
    cum + sratio, 300    7/40          3              7
    cum + sratio, 500    5/40          1              2
    total               22/160         7             16

In 16 of 22 the degenerate point has the higher log likelihood, so it
is the supremum of the likelihood (a component runs off to a step
function of `x`), not a bad start. brms's priors keep its sampler off
that boundary; a maximum likelihood fit has nothing that does. A fit
of this kind prints thresholds such as `-2018.79, -1540.98, 13927.86`
and a slope of `-7509.32` with `NaN` standard errors
(`dev/ordmix-rev-misc.R`, section (f), seed 20261095, n = 300).

The record does not show it. `dev/ordmix-ident.R` ran seeds 1..20 at
n = 500, which contain none of the degenerate seeds at that n (22, 26,
30, 35, 37 for `cum + cum`), and its `quiet()` wrapper suppressed every
warning, so it could not have seen a warning either. The findings name
a degenerate optimum only for three components on two-class data.

Two related cases:

- `mixture(cumulative("probit"), sratio("cloglog"), acat())`, case
  `r_three_mixlink` of `dev/ordmix-rev-lpcheck.R` (data seed 20261005 +
  31). The default fit returns `convergence = 0` ("X-convergence (3)"),
  no warning, the gradient at its own optimum is `Inf` (`tau_raw1`,
  `beta[1]`), and brms's gradient there is 0.542. At the third
  perturbed point frmtmb's density is `NaN` and brms's is
  `-549.423887080409`. Causes, both pre-existing on rellib-r5:
  `check_convergence()` (`R/fit.R`) skips the gradient check when the
  gradient is not finite (`is.finite(g) && g > control$grad_tol`), and
  the probit's log-odds form `log(pnorm(eta)) - log(pnorm(-eta))`
  (`R/links.R`) underflows at `|eta| > 38.2`. A plain probit fit gives
  `NaN` there on both builds (`dev/ordmix-rev-probit-sat.R`: slope 20,
  `max |tau - eta|` 59.2, `fn = NaN`), but only a mixture's degenerate
  component reaches that region at its optimum. The same data with the
  logit in component 1 stops at "NA/NaN gradient evaluation".
- Three components with mixing predictors collapse a gap at the default
  start (`r_theta3`, `r_theta3b`). brms cannot take that point
  ("ordered_free: ... The element at 3 is 0.410237, but should be
  greater than the previous element, 0.410237"). On `r_theta3` the
  default fit ends at -533.555 with one threshold at `Inf`, and the
  best of 12 starts is -523.760 (`dev/ordmix-rev-gapfloor.R`).

Required: a fit-end warning for a degenerate component (and a warning
for a non-finite gradient at the optimum in `check_convergence()`,
measured against the whole suite for false alarms), and the record and
the `mixture()` documentation corrected: say that maximum likelihood
for an ordinal mixture often has its supremum at a degenerate
boundary, and that several starts are needed, more so for three or
more components.

## What the gap floor does (task item 2)

`dev/ordmix-rev-gapfloor.R`, log `dev/ordmix-rev-log-gapfloor.txt`.

- It changes no value away from a collapsed gap: on 100000 random pairs
  (seed 1, `a ~ N(0, 3)`, gap `10^U(-15, 1)`), `ord_log_interior(a, b,
  TRUE)` and `ord_log_interior(a, b, FALSE)` are identical in all
  100000, in plain doubles. At a gap of 1e-300 both give -692.1618. At
  `a == b` the floor gives -692.2036 where the plain form gives `-Inf`.
- So it binds only where two thresholds are the same double, and there
  it changes the mixture's value by at most `exp(-692)` relative,
  because another component carries the row. It does not move a
  correct optimum. It turns the 0 x Inf gradient (NaN) into a finite
  one, which is what lets the optimizer stop AT the degenerate
  boundary instead of failing loudly. Of the 20 `gr_cum2` fits, 2 end
  with a collapsed gap (seeds 8 and 14; one log increment at -2346),
  both with non-finite standard errors and no warning at fit time.
- brms has no analogue: its `ordered` type keeps every gap at
  `exp(raw) > 0`, Stan's `log_sum_exp` has the same 0 x Inf gradient
  at a `-Inf` component term, and its priors keep the sampler off the
  boundary.
- The lane's reason for keeping the floor out of plain fits, "the
  floored form is not bitwise equal", is not what the plain-double
  measurement shows. Keeping it out is harmless; the stated reason is
  unmeasured (m4).

Conclusion: the floor binds only at a degenerate boundary. Document it
as such, and let B2's warning say when a fit ends there.

## Minor

m1. NEWS puts `mixture(order = "mu")` with ordinal components under
"Breaking changes". Ordinal components were refused outright before,
so this is a new feature.

m2. Two absolute tolerances on unbounded quantities:
`test-ordinal-mixture.R:262` (`abs(diff(tk)[2] - diff(tk)[1]) < 1e3 *
eps`, thresholds) and `:424` (`abs(bs[[1]] - bs[[2]]) <
sqrt(.Machine$double.eps)`, slopes). Use a ratio to `max(abs(tk))`, or
`identical()` where the claim is identity. The `1e3 * eps` bounds on
probability differences are bounded by 1 and have base precedent.

m3. `test-ordinal-mixture-draws.R:49` and `:175` wrap `frm_sample()` in
`suppressWarnings()`, which `helper-warnings.R` says hides warnings
nobody expected. Precedent exists in 30 frmtmb.sample test files, so
this is not new to the lane.

m4. The findings keep the gap floor out of plain fits because "the
floored form is not bitwise equal to `logspace_sub(-b, -a)`". In plain
doubles the two are identical on 100000 of 100000 pairs (the gap floor
section). On the tape it is not measured. Keeping the floor out is
harmless; correct or measure the stated reason.

m5. The `cs()` refusals. brms says "Category specific effects are only
supported for the main parameter 'mu'" for `sigma ~ cs(w)` on a
gaussian; frmtmb says "cs() needs an sratio, cratio, or acat family, or
hurdle_cumulative()". For a plain family the new message adds "(mu1,
mu2, ... of an ordinal mixture)", which reads oddly there.

m6. NEWS and the compat row say the density equals brms's "at the
optimum and at three perturbed parameter vectors to at most 2.5 ulp".
That holds for the 20 listed shapes. On `r_three_mixlink` one
perturbed point is `NaN` against brms's finite value (B2). Qualify the
claim: the probit saturates past `|eta| = 38.2`.

m7. `test-ordinal-mixture.R:264` says the Links line is "as brms shows
it" and pins `theta1 =`. brms omits `theta1` when it has no predictor
(`dev/ordmix-rev-links.R`: `mu1 = probit; mu2 = logit`). frmtmb lists
every dpar for gaussian mixtures too (pre-existing,
`dev/ordmix-rev-links2.R`), so the test pins frmtmb's convention, not
brms's. Fix the comment.

m8. `draws_to_natural()` empty block: real, pre-existing, on both
builds (`dev/ordmix-rev-emptyblock.R`). A binary response with
`cumulative(threshold = "sum_to_zero")` has an empty block whose name is
`b_Intercept[1]`. `frm_sample()` stores only `b_x lp__`; brms stores the
0 as `b_Intercept[1]`. Driven by hand on a matrix with columns `b_x`,
`b_Intercept[1]`, `lp__`, the inverse returns a matrix with 0 columns.
The forward step omits the column, so the inverse is unreachable from
stored draws today. Fixing the forward step alone would make it
reachable. Fix both together with `x[seq_along(x) > R]`, the idiom of
the `brms_par_labels()` fix.

m9. A multivariate model with one ordinal-mixture response fits
(`dev/ordmix-rev-misc.R`, (a)), and brms builds it
(`dev/ordmix-rev-brms-sem.R`, 1m). Neither the lane nor this review
compared its density with brms's; the lane tests its names only. My
identity check (the mv log likelihood against the sum of the two
univariate fits) differed by 3.1e-5 because the two fits reached
different points of a degenerate likelihood (fitted() differs by
0.249), so it settles nothing.

m10. The ported-suite ledger: no row flips (the gated brms-suite
files passed in the run below), as the lane says.

### User-decision items, with brms run

- **Hurdle beside a non-hurdle component.** brms builds
  `mixture(cumulative(), hurdle_cumulative())` and `standata()` accepts a
  response with zeros (`nthres 3`, `Y` 0..4). Its compiled program
  cannot evaluate it: "ordered_logistic: Random variable is 0, but must
  be in the interval [1, 4]" (`dev/ordmix-rev-brms-hurdlemix.R`). On a
  response with no zero it evaluates (log_prob -425.1999), and then
  the hurdle probability has no data. The two components read the
  categories 1..K the same way; the hurdle's support adds 0. The
  lane's message says that accurately. Refusal is defensible: brms's
  model either fails or has an empty hurdle.
- **`groups = ` with ordinal components.** brms has no `groups`
  argument. Refusing is consistent.
- **`cs()` on `cumulative()`.** brms fits it, with "Category specific
  effects for this family should be considered experimental". The
  lane is right that the compat row's reason ("not identified") is
  inaccurate; the hazard is crossing thresholds. The density path now
  exists (`ord_cumulative_logpmf_cs()`). By the tiebreaker, match brms:
  lift the refusal and say what a crossing row gives, as the hurdle
  now does. A user decision.
- **Crossing `cs()` thresholds.** See the post-fit section: NaN and NA
  are defensible against brms's negative probability.
- **The flat-direction warnings.** B1.
- **No multi-start for `order = "mu"`.** It is broader than `"mu"`: B2
  and the three-component cases.

### B5. The sharpening cut is an absolute log-likelihood, so the weight scale moves it

`ll_at()` sums `wts * ...` and the cut is a fixed 0.1. With constant
weights the estimates do not change, but the sharpening scales with
the weights.

Script `dev/ordmix-rev3-b3.R`, the lane's process, n = 500, seeds
1..20:

- **Weights 1/n (summing to 1):** all 20 fits warn "a step function of
  its predictors ... its slopes and thresholds run off". 11 of them are
  sound by the review's label: every SE finite and both slopes within
  50% of the truth (relative error 0.10 to 0.40). Their sharpening is
  -0.012 to -0.07.
- **Weights 0.1:** 0 of 20 warn.
- **Weights 10:** the review's five degenerate fits
  (`dev/ordmix-rev3-wdegen.R`) still warn (their sharpening is about
  1e-5 at any scale). So scaling up costs no catches, and scaling down
  creates false alarms.

Required: measure the sharpening per unit of mean weight (divide by
`mean(wts)`, or use `wts / mean(wts)` in `ll_at()`), plus a test on a
fit with weights 1/n. The cut of 0.1 then means what the lane
measured on unit weights.
