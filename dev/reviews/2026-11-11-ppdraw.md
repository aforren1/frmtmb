### m3. MINOR. The threshold's margin to interior estimates is smaller than recorded

Findings: interior near-end estimates change by "0.0219 and up", "1e-4
sits ... two below the second" group. G3 (`ppdraw-review-g.R`, gaussian
`(1 | g)`, 6 groups of 20, true sd 0.1, seeds 1 to 40): interior group

interior fits will fall below any threshold. Where the gain is below

over the limit at most about 1e-4", not "at the end of the range".

optimizer's last gradient. `ppdraw-review-d.R` (zero_inflated_poisson,

last gradient 5.3e-6 and 3.4e-4), not held, z -5.45 and -5.29: the

**The cliff at `draw_edge_c` is real but small, and does not depend on
the seed path.** A side is steep when the log-likelihood falls by 2 or
more at 1 conditional sd. A steep side's scale is then at most `v[2] / 2
<= 0.5`, so a side jumps from scale 1 to about 0.5 at the threshold.
`ppdraw-short-cliff.R` takes the lane's "slope sd 0.05 seed 5" data and
scales the group-slope component by lambda = 0.5 to 2. Logs:
`ppdraw-short-cliff-lane.txt` and `-base.txt`.

| lambda | fall at 1 csd | changed | new level at x = 2: sd, w98 (lane) | w98 (0.81.0) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.30 | 2.163 | TRUE | 1.106, 5.173 | 5.569 |
| 1.40 | 2.083 | TRUE | 1.109, 5.182 | 5.581 |
| 1.50 | 1.936 | FALSE | 1.226, 5.562 | 5.562 |

Below the threshold the lane's width is flat at 5.18 to 5.19. Across it,
the width steps by 7% where 0.81.0 is smooth. The true w98 of that
family is about 5.70. The step is set by a single objective evaluation
(fall >= 2). Its statistic moves by about 0.1 between neighboring data
sets (2.141 at lambda 1.0, 2.226 at 1.1), so a perturbation of a few
ulps cannot flip it unless a fit lands within about 1e-8 of 2. The
lane's ulp run (21 perturbations) sits far from the cliff (r 0.11 and
0.16) and says nothing about it either way. The coverage table above
shows the cliff costs no calibration on average. MINOR: a continuous
scale (for example `min(1, t2 / (2 csd))`, reaching 1 where the normal
law does) would remove the step.

**Departures.**
- (a) csd as the reference: sound. The one-parameter slice of a
  quadratic objective falls as t^2 / (2 csd^2), so r is exactly 1 under
  the normal law whatever the correlation. Against the marginal sd, a
  correlated interior parameter reads as steep.
- (b) The smaller of the single-scale and the likelihood-matched value:
  acceptable. It is the narrower of two heuristics and was tuned on
  three cases, but the coverage table shows no undercoverage from it.
- Documentation overclaim, MINOR: `draw_edge_map()` multiplies `v`
  (distances in units of csd) by `s` (the draw sd). The cap "none goes
  further than where the log-likelihood falls by z^2 / 2" therefore holds
  only where s = csd. Where s > csd it is looser by s / csd. The scaling
  is relative, which is defensible for a marginal draw, but the comment
  and NEWS state the absolute version.

**New defect, MAJOR (one line): `predict()` leaks base R's "NaNs
produced" warning, and a rounding decides when.** `draw_edge_csd()` takes
`1 / sqrt(diag(H))`. On a `(1 + x | g)` fit whose correlation the SE
check holds at its limit, that diagonal entry is +-1e-9. Where it is
negative, `sqrt()` warns, and the warning escapes `predict()`
unclassed. `ppdraw-short-nan2.R` (cliff family, 100 draws, no
suppression), log `ppdraw-short-nan2-lane.txt`: the warning appears on 6
of 16 fits (lambda 0.5, 0.6, 0.7, 1.6, 1.7, 2.0), with the entry at
-4.5e-10, -1.7e-9, -7.9e-11, -1.2e-9, -1.7e-9 and -1.8e-9. The other 10
fits have it at +3.9e-11 to +2.2e-9 and stay silent. A singular random
slope correlation is a common model. Any test that lets no warning
escape would go red on the sign of a 1e-9 entry. Fix:
`sqrt(pmax(diag(H), 0))`; the `is.finite(cs)` filter after it then
drops that position.
