## 3. The tier's teeth


The tier runs clean at 435 assertions, 0 failures, in 6 to 17 s

### Harness note, and why the lane's failure counts are not reproducible as printed

`fam_gaussian`'s `sim` slot reads the `se()` aterm through
`resid_sd(sigma, aterms)`. A mutation harness that REPLACES the slot
with a two-parameter `rnorm()` therefore also breaks the `se()`,
`se(sigma = TRUE)` and `mi()` blocks whether or not the mutation
itself is detectable: an unmutated replacement of that shape raises 8
failures on its own. The counts below mutate the DPARS and hand them
to the family's own `sim`, so `delta = 0` raises zero and every
failure is the mutation's. Failure counts are a property of the
harness, not of the tier, and the lane's table should say which
harness produced them.

### Re-run of four of the lane's nine

The two Gamma/hurdle/compois families have no aterm in their `sim`, so

### The sensitivity calibration, which is the answer to the SIM_ALPHA question

Sweeping the gaussian sd by a multiplicative factor:

| sd factor | Failures |
| --- | --- |
| 1.00 | 0 |
| 1.01 | 0 |
| 1.02 | 2 |
| 1.03 | 4 |
| 1.05 | 7 |
| 1.10 | 11 |

**The detection floor sits between a 1 and a 2 percent sd error**, at
`SIM_ALPHA = 1e-6` and `SIM_N = 4000`. Catching a 5 percent inflation
is right: a simulator whose spread is 5 percent wrong is defective, not
merely unlucky. And the floor is the number that settles the alpha
argument, because it shows the small alpha is costing essentially no
detection. A convention slip is a 20 to 100 percent error, an order
of magnitude above what this tier still sees.

### The sweep, and what it found

Scanned every `.R`, `.Rmd` and `.Rd` under `tests/`, `vignettes/`,
`R/`, `man/`, `extensions/` and `inst/`: 44 `frm_simulate()` calls
carry an explicit `seed =`, and 6 of them name a value that also
appears in a `set.seed()` in the same file. Read in context, four are
false positives (the `set.seed()` is elsewhere in the file and did not
make the covariates that call consumes, or the data has no random
covariate at all). Two are the real pattern:

**1. `vignettes/inputs.Rmd:152-157`, INTRODUCED BY THIS LANE.**

```r
set.seed(1)
d <- data.frame(y = 0, x = rnorm(30), g = factor(rep(1:6, 5)))
...
d$y <- frm_simulate(..., nsim = 1, seed = 1)[[1]]
```

This is exactly the trap `dev/simulate-findings.md` describes and
claims to have removed. It is not a near miss. Cutting the seed-1
normal stream and correlating the recovered residual against it at
every offset:

```
offset  6 : cor = 1.00000   <== the SAME DRAWS
(every other offset in 0..12: |cor| <= 0.374)
```

The draw restarts the same stream, spends its first six normals on the
group effects, and hands positions 7 through 36 to the residuals, so
`e_i` IS `x_{i+6}` for 24 of the 30 rows, exactly. The visible damage:
the residual sd comes out 0.919 against the 1.0 asked for, and a fit of
the generating model returns a slope of 0.200 against the generating
0.4, a 50 percent attenuation. Nothing degenerate is PRINTED, because
the chunk prints only `names(spec$responses$y$dpars)` and the frame's
structure, which is why it was not caught. The fix is one character
class: give the draw its own seed, as all eight test fixtures do.

**2. `vignettes/frmtmb.Rmd:273-279`, PRE-EXISTING, not this lane's.**

`set.seed(1)`, `x = rnorm(120)`, then
`frm_simulate(form, dd, nsim = 3, seed = 1, ...)`. Same pattern. Here
the damage is small because `nsim = 3` walks the stream forward, so
only the first draw overlaps and only partly: the recovered residual
correlations against `x` are 0.058, -0.013 and -0.027. Out of this
lane's scope, but it is the same defect in a file the lane edited, and
the lane's own reasoning says the pattern is wrong wherever it appears.

The other four flagged sites are clean and need no change:
`test-simulate-ergonomics.R:53` (covariates from `set.seed(21)`, draw
at 4), `test-simulate-ergonomics.R:133` (no `set.seed()` before the
covariates), `vignettes/frmtmb.Rmd:240` (the lane's converted mixture
chunk: its data has NO random covariate, `g` is
`factor(rep(1:40, each = 5))`, so there is nothing to alias with) and
`vignettes/frmtmb.Rmd:288` (covariates from `set.seed(1)`, draw at 2).

### The mixture chunk: class membership IS by group

`groups = ~g` is doing what the chunk says. The components sit at -1
and 2 with sd 0.8, so a midpoint rule at 0.5 recovers each row's
component:

| Statistic | Converted chunk (`groups = ~g`) | Same call without `groups =` |
| --- | --- | --- |
| groups whose 5 rows share one component | 33 of 40 | 1 of 40 |

The 7 apparently-mixed groups are the CLASSIFIER's error, not the
simulator's: at 1.875 sd from the midpoint the expected number of

means would concentrate near the mixture mean instead, which is what
the `groups =`-free control shows.

## Is SIM_ALPHA = 1e-6 the right number?


Yes, and both sides of the trade are now measured rather than argued.

**The false-alarm side.** Instrumenting `testthat::expect_lt()` while
the tier runs: of its 435 assertions, **251 are statistical** and could
false-alarm on a reseed: 87 chi-square, 77 mean, 77 variance, 10
`P(Y = 0)`. Over 251 independent tests:

| alpha | P(at least one false alarm per reseed) |
| --- | --- |
| 1e-6 (as written) | 0.0003 |
| 1e-4 | 0.025 |
| 1e-3 | 0.222 |
| 0.01 | 0.920 |
| 0.05 | 1.000 |

The lane's argument, that a fixed seed makes a false alarm PERMANENT
rather than flaky, so somebody has to sit down and work out which end
is wrong, is the right frame, and 251 is why. At a conventional 0.05
the tier would essentially never be green after any change that
perturbs the stream; even 1e-3 is a one-in-five chance of a permanent
false alarm.

**The detection side.** From section 3, the tier still catches a
gaussian sd wrong by 2 percent, and every convention slip tried lands
between 2 and 12 failures. A shape/scale swap, a reversed gate or an
ignored dispersion is a 20 to 100 percent error, one to two orders of
magnitude above the floor.

So the small alpha buys a factor of about 3000 in false-alarm
probability and costs, at most, the ability to see errors between 1 and
2 percent. Nothing in this package's simulator conventions lives in
that band. `SIM_ALPHA = 1e-6` is right, and the same constant in the
ddm tier (`DDM_ALPHA = 1e-6`) is right for the same reason.

The one thing the file should say and does not: the argument depends on
the assertion COUNT, so the constant is not free to stay put if the
tier grows by an order of magnitude. One sentence naming 251 would make
that check-able later.

## Beyond the brief: four things I measured that the lane did not


### (a) The `rgamma(shape = 0)` assumption holds

The Tweedie's point mass at zero rests on `rgamma()` returning an
EXACT zero at shape zero. Verified: 1e5 draws at `shape = 0` are all
exactly 0, none NaN, and a mixed shape vector `c(0, 0, 2, 0)` returns
`0.000, 0.000, 7.298, 0.000`. The comment's claim is right and the
mechanism is load-bearing, so it is good that it says so.

Tweedie edges are all clean, no NA anywhere: `mu = 0.01` (P0 0.821),
`mu = 200, phi = 0.1` (mean 200.06), `p = 1.01` and `p = 1.99` (mean
1.990 both), `mu = 1e-8` (P0 0.9997).

### (b) A hurdle_poisson corner where the positive part draws a zero

By definition the cleared hurdle draws from a support starting at 1, so
`P(Y = 0)` must equal `hu` for EVERY `mu > 0`. It does not below
`mu ~ 1e-14`:

| mu | P(draw = 0) | hu | correct? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1e-8 | 0.2973 | 0.3 | yes |
| 1e-14 | 0.4293 | 0.3 | no, atom inflated 43 percent |
| 1e-16 and below | 1.0000 | 0.3 | no, nothing clears the hurdle |

The code comment says the `pmin(u, 1 - .Machine$double.eps)` guard is
about "a mu near the double epsilon". The guard does stop the NA, but
the failure starts about two orders of magnitude EARLIER than the
comment's reasoning covers, and for a different reason: the loss is in
`p0 + u (1 - p0)` itself, where `1 - p0 = 1 - exp(-mu)` is about `mu`,
so near 1 the double grid holds only a handful of representable values
and `qpois()` rounds the smallest of them down to 0.

Not a blocker: `mu = 1e-14` under a log link needs an intercept of -32,
which no fit reaches. But the one-line fix is exact and free, because
the support genuinely starts at 1 (clamp the positive part with
`pmax(..., 1L)`), and it makes the comment true.

### (c) compois costs 240x a Poisson when the predictor is continuous

The `sim` slot caches one bisection solve per distinct `(mu, nu)` pair,
and its comment justifies that with "a design has far fewer distinct
pairs than rows". That is true of a FACTOR design and false of a
continuous one, where every row is its own pair. Measured:

| design | cost |
| --- | --- |
| n = 2000, 2 distinct pairs | 0.011 s |
| n = 2000, 6 distinct pairs | 0.023 s |
| n = 200, 200 distinct pairs | 0.187 s |
| n = 1000, 1000 distinct pairs | 0.537 s |
| `frm_simulate(y ~ x)` with continuous x, n = 1000 | **1.43 s** |
| the same design under `poisson()` | **0.006 s** |

That is 240x, and it is linear in n: 0.54 ms per row, so n = 10000 is
about 14 s per draw and a 1000-draw power-analysis loop is hours. The
caching is right; the comment overstates when it applies. The mean is
smooth and strictly monotone in the rate at fixed `nu`, so the cheap
remedy is to solve on a grid of `log(mu)` per distinct `nu` and
interpolate, rather than to bisect per row.

Neither (b) nor (c) is a correctness defect in normal use, and neither
should block the lane. Both belong in the punch list because in each
case a COMMENT claims more than the code delivers, and comments are
what the next person will trust.
