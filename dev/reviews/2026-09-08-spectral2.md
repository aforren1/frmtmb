## 0. THE AR(2) RESOLUTION. No released number is wrong.

**Lift this section straight into the record. Do not correct
`NEWS.md`.**

The lane reported that the 0.54.0 record's AR(2) resonance figure "is
not reproducible", and that a shipped claim was therefore false. It is
reproducible. The disagreement is a series length that the record left
out of one sentence and that the lane did not go looking for.

**Where the record's construction is written down.** It is not missing.
`dev/reviews/2026-09-08-freq.md:86` carries the freq lane's own review
table, and its AR(2) row names the length:

```
| AR(2) resonance r = 0.9, 0.98, 0.995 | 1023 | 0% |
```

1023 ordinates, that is, a 2048-point series. `dev/freq-findings.md`
then repeated the cell in prose without repeating the length, and put
"at 127 ordinates" beside the AR(1) clause in the same sentence. The
lane read the 127 forward onto the AR(2) clause and measured there.

**Measured independently, on the reference 0.54.0 build** (the shipped
`whittle()$valid_y`, so the lag-1 rule), 1000 replicates a cell,
`arima.sim(list(ar = c(2 r cos(2 pi f0), -r^2)))`, untapered
`frm_periodogram()`:

| construction | nf | refused, 0.54.0 rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| r = 0.9, f0 = 0.1, n = 2048 | 1023 | **0 / 1000** |
| r = 0.98, f0 = 0.1, n = 2048 | 1023 | **0 / 1000** |
| r = 0.995, f0 = 0.1, n = 2048 | 1023 | **1 / 1000 (0.1%)** |
| r = 0.9, 0.98, 0.995, f0 = 0.25, n = 2048 | 1023 | **0 / 1000 each** |
| r = 0.995, f0 = 0.1, n = 256 | 127 | 162 / 1000 (16.2%) |
| r = 0.995, f0 = 0.1, n = 512 | 255 | 59 / 1000 (5.9%) |
| r = 0.995, f0 = 0.1, n = 1024 | 511 | 11 / 1000 (1.1%) |
| r = 0.995, f0 = 0.1, n = 4096 | 2047 | 0 / 1000 |

At the length the record used, 0% is what the rule gives. Over 200
replicates, which is what the record ran, P(0 refusals) is 0.82 at
f0 = 0.1 and 1.00 at f0 = 0.25.

The record's neighboring cell reproduces too: a 1/f background with a
10 Hz alpha bump at 1023 ordinates is 0 / 1000 at amplitude 1, 3 and
10, on the same build.

**Why the rate falls with length**, which is the physics the lane read
backwards. A near-unit-root resonance on a SHORT record is not
resolved: the pole's sidelobes dominate the band, the periodogram is
leakage rather than signal, and a leakage floor is exactly what this
refusal is built to catch. Lengthen the record and the pole is
resolved, the true spectrum rises above the floor, and the ordinates go
back to being independent exponentials. The same mechanism explains the
lane's burn-in sweep, which it could not account for: a longer burn-in
gives the sharper stationary resonance, hence more leakage, hence a
HIGHER rate. Both observations are one effect.

**Candidates I tested and eliminated rather than assumed:**

- *Innovation variance.* Cannot matter, analytically. The statistic is
  `var(diff(log y))`, and multiplying `y` by a constant adds a constant
  to `log y` that the differencing removes. Confirmed numerically:
  2.884671 at sd 1 and 2.884671 after multiplying the ordinates by 1e6.
- *`arima.sim` versus `filter()`, and the burn-in.* At 127 ordinates,
  r = 0.995: `arima.sim` default 15.4%, `filter` with burn-in 0 / 20 /
  100 / 500 / 5000 gives 2.1% / 5.4% / 13.0% / 16.7% / 15.1%. No
  setting reaches 0, and the sweep rises rather than falls.
- *`r` as a bare coefficient* (`phi1 = r`, `phi2 = -r^2`) rather than a
  pole modulus: 0.0% / 0.2% / 3.5% at 127 ordinates for r = 0.9 / 0.98
  / 0.995. Closer to 0 but not 0, and it is not what "resonance at
  radius r" means.
- *Resonance frequency.* It matters a great deal and the record does
  not state it. At 127 ordinates, r = 0.995, `filter` burn-in 500:
  f0 = 0.02 gives 67.0%, 0.05 gives 37.4%, 0.10 gives 15.8%, 0.15
  gives 5.7%, 0.25 gives 0.6%, 0.40 gives 17.5%. At 1023 ordinates,
  f0 = 0.0391 (10 Hz at 256 Hz sampling) still gives 13.3% at
  r = 0.995, so the record's 0% at 1023 also implies a resonance that
  is not right up against DC. Worth recording; it does not change the
  conclusion.
- *Retaining a band.* Keeping the low 63 or 127 ordinates of a longer
  record gives 0%.
- *A taper.* Not claimed by the record, and gives 0.4% to 1.1%.

**Conclusion, definite.** The record's construction is recoverable, it
is stated in `dev/reviews/2026-09-08-freq.md`, and it gives 0%. The
released `NEWS.md` sentence "0 percent on AR(1), AR(2) and
1/f-with-a-peak spectra" is TRUE at the lengths at which it was
measured. It is length-conditional and reads as unconditional, which is
a documentation weakness rather than a false number, and the same
bullet already documents that a near-unit-root spectrum "fires
occasionally". No user-facing correction is required. I have added the
lengths to `dev/freq-findings.md` so this cannot happen again, and
withdrawn the lane's non-reproduction entry.

For completeness, the lane's own 127-ordinate measurement is sound: I
get 16.2% at lag 1 and 4.6% at lag 3 against its 16.6% and 5.4%. Its
two documents disagreed with each other about the length ("nf 127" in
`dev/freq-findings.md`, "a 255-point AR(2)" in
`dev/spectral2-findings.md`); those are the same cell if "255-point"
means a 255-sample series, and the ambiguity is part of how this went
wrong.

## Findings

### 1. The justification is false outside segment averaging (HIGH)

This is the finding consolidation must read.

The lane's central argument is that neither error the refusal exists
for travels through the differencing lag, because "an averaged
periodogram declared raw is deflated through its MARGINAL shape:
ordinates of shape k have `var(diff(log I, lag)) = 2 trigamma(k)` at
every lag, because they are independent across frequency whatever the
averaging. No lag can see around that and no lag helps it."

That is true of SEGMENT averaging, which is what
`frm_periodogram(segments =)` does and the only kind the lane measured.
It is false of averaging ACROSS FREQUENCY, which is the other standard
way to get non-raw ordinates and the one a neuroscience audience is
most likely to arrive with. There the ordinates are genuinely
correlated, the correlation was doing part of the detection at lag 1,
and lag 3 gives three bins of it away.

### 5. The binomial probability in the test comment is wrong (LOW)

The comment on the rate assertion said the slack of 3 puts
"P(count > 3) below 1e-10". `pbinom(3, 1000, 1e-4, lower.tail = FALSE)`
is **3.825e-06**, and the two-sample version, `P(hann - none > 3)` with
both at 1e-4, is 3.4e-06 over 1e7 simulated pairs. The claim in
`dev/spectral2-findings.md` repeats it.

The slack is nonetheless a genuine binomial slack rather than an
absolute tolerance, because it is derived from the calibrated rate and
the sample size, and 1 run in 260000 is tight enough to catch a
regression: the unfixed rule produces 11 to 30 refusals per 1000 over
30 seed sets (mean 19.9), never fewer than 11, so the assertion has an
eight-refusal cushion below the smallest failure it must catch.

The CI worry named in the task does not apply here. Both counts are
produced under `set.seed()`, so the assertion is deterministic given the
R version and RNG kind, not a timing- or machine-dependent threshold
like the three that have broken before. I re-ran the two seeded counts
twice in one process: identical.

**What I did.** Corrected the number in the test comment.

## Suspicions I chased and disproved

- **That the released `NEWS.md` claim was false.** It is not; section 0.
- **That the innovation variance or the generator explained the AR(2)
  gap.** Neither can: the statistic is scale invariant, and no burn-in
  or generator reaches 0% at 127 ordinates.
- **That the threshold curve was quietly wrong at the ends.** It is
  not, at 24, 32, 512, 1024 or 2048 ordinates, for k = 1, 2, 4 and 8.
- **That the wider step would let a genuinely averaged estimate
  through.** For every multitaper and Daniell estimate declared raw at
  127 ordinates or more, lag 3 still refuses 99.4 to 100 percent of
  them. The hole is below about 100 ordinates only, and only for
  frequency smoothing, which is finding 1.
- **That the lag change moved segment-averaging detection.** It does
  not, at any of twelve cells.
- **That the slack of 3 was an absolute tolerance in disguise.** It is
  derived from the calibrated rate and the sample size, and the run is
  deterministic under `set.seed()`, so the CI failure mode that broke
  `test-deriv.R`, `test-gratia.R` and the fuzz harness does not apply.
- **That `test-structure.R` and `test-compat.R` regressions were the
  lane's.** They were my harness; both pass cleanly with the package
  namespace as the test environment.
- **That the `checking examples` NOTE was contention, as the lane
  concluded.** It is not, but it is not the lane's either; finding 9.
