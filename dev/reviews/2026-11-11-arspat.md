### MAJOR-1. `fcor()`/`sar()` + `mi()` on `student()`: the Laplace error is a closed-form function of nu, not flagged, and not measured

Claim 2 says "approximate student". It is approximate by exactly

    c(nu, n, m) = lgamma((nu + n)/2) - lgamma((nu + n - m)/2) + (m/2) log(2/(nu + n))

where `n` is the joint dimension (every row) and `m` the number of missing
values that no other density reads. Reason: the joint is one multivariate
t, and the Laplace value of its integral over `m` coordinates is the exact
margin times `exp(c)`; the quadratic form of the observed rows cancels.
So `c` does not depend on the data, beta, sigma or rho.

Reproducer `dev/reviews/arspat-review-laplace.R` (log `-laplace.log`):
the objective at the fit's estimate with nu moved to 3, 5, 10, 30, against
`mvtnorm::dmvt()` on the observed rows (n = 60). fcor and sar give the
same differences, and they equal `c` to every printed digit:

| m | nu 3 | nu 5 | nu 10 | nu 30 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | -6.082e-02 | -5.891e-02 | -5.462e-02 | -4.230e-02 |
| 10 | -5.066e-01 | -4.900e-01 | -4.530e-01 | -3.478e-01 |
| 20 | -1.981e+00 | -1.911e+00 | -1.757e+00 | -1.330e+00 |

(the closed form, computed alone: -6.0822e-02, ..., -1.3302e+00.)

Effect:
- beta, sigma and rho are exact at a given nu. `logLik()`, AIC and LRTs
  are low by `|c|`, and nu's profile is tilted (c rises with nu), so nu
  is biased upward.
- Under ML with no prior, nu runs to about 1e10 (one t vector does not
  identify nu; 12 seeds, `-laplace2.log`), so `c` is about 0 and nothing
  shows. With brms's default prior on nu, `prior = set_prior("gamma(2,
  0.1)", class = "nu")` (`-laplace3.R`, n = 60, m = 20, seed 4): nu = 28.11,
  `logLik - exact = -1.3614`, closed form -1.3614. `mi_laplace_note()`
  measures `tk = -0.927`, which is under its `|tk| > 1` gate, so no warning.
  `summary()` says nothing (0 lines with "Laplace" or "approx", 
  `-spatial.log`).
- The lane's L1 table has no student `fcor()`/`sar()` case, and the lane
  did not measure this error.

Fix: when every missing value of a registered term's response is unread
by other densities, add `-c(nu, d, m)` to the term's student log-density.
Then the fit is exact for any registered term whose joint is a multivariate
t. For `fcor()` alone, cutting `M` to the observed rows also gives the exact
fit, as the gaussian test already shows. Add a test against `dmvt()` at a
fixed nu, as in the reproducer.

Related, minor: a value that another density reads (ar() family, `mi(y)`
as a predictor) stays latent on student under Laplace, and
`summary()` does not say so either (`-detect-after.log`, "mvbf student").

### MINOR-2. 3897: "rho's interval from the real parts, as spatialreg reads it" is not spatialreg's rule

spatialreg (`R/jacobian_setup.R`, lines 129 and 151, read via `gh api`):

    if (is.complex(eig)) eig.range <- 1/range(Re(eig[which(Im(eig) == 0)]))

That is the range of the REAL eigenvalues only. The lane takes the range of
the real parts of every eigenvalue. The lane's interval is valid: on it,
every real `1 - rho lambda > 0` and every complex pair adds a positive
`|1 - rho lambda|^2`, so `det(I - rho W) > 0`. But it can be narrower than
spatialreg's on the side where a complex real part is more extreme than every
real eigenvalue. For a nonnegative W the upper end is the same (Perron
root). The wording is wrong in `sar.Rd` ("The range of rho"), the spatial
NEWS, the code comment in `sar_build()`, and the backlog CLOSED line.
On the lane's own test W (3-NN, n = 40, seed 21) the two rules agree: min
Re = -0.5944 is a real eigenvalue (`-spatial.log`), so the test cannot
tell them apart.

The log-determinant itself is right. `sar_logdet()` against `determinant()`
(`-spatial.log`): |diff| <= 6.0e-14 at rho in {-1.514, -0.5, 0, 0.3, 0.9,
-1.666}, 1.06e-10 at lo (1 - 1e-6) (log det -27.69), and 9.6e-10 at hi
(1 - 1e-6) (log det -46.89). Sign +1 everywhere. Accepting a W that brms
refuses (brms-37) is an extension beyond parity. The docs say that brms
refuses it, so it is documented.

Fix: either use spatialreg's rule (with the lane's `1e-8 * max Mod`
threshold instead of `Im == 0`), or keep the lane's rule and say that it
is a sufficient interval that can be narrower than spatialreg's.

### 2. MINOR-2: rho's interval

1e-8 max Mod`, which is spatialreg's rule with a tolerance.
`-short-b.log` covers 15 W: the lane's 5 x 5 construction (interval
(-5, 1)), directed kNN graphs k = 1..4 with 3 seeds each (n = 40, up to 12

and a random nonnegative 20 x 20 (12 complex).
- Over 2001 interior points per W, `determinant()$sign` is +1 everywhere.
- Interior `|sar_logdet - log|det||` is at most 4.5e-12.
- At 1e-7 inside each end the difference is at most 3.8e-8, except
  knn2_s1 at 1.2e-4. That W has repeated eigenvalues at the end, so the
  log-determinant there is near-singular in both computations. This is
  not a defect at any rho the optimizer reaches.
- Just outside the ends the sign turns -1 in 18 of the 30 ends. At the
  other 12, the bounding eigenvalue repeats an even number of times (for
  example, +-1 on 1-NN graphs, 1 on graphs with two components), so the
  sign does not change, but the end is still where I - rho W turns

The docs (`?sar`) and the spatial NEWS now say "the real eigenvalues alone,

### 3. MINOR-3: se() beside ar(cov = FALSE): the facts

`-short-b.R` part C, on the lane and on rellib-r20 (`-short-c-after.log`,
`-short-c-before.log`), plus brms 2.23.0 `make_stancode()`:

| family | term | lane | rellib-r20 | brms 2.23.0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| gaussian | ar, ar p = 2, ma, arma (cov = FALSE), se(sigma = TRUE or FALSE) | refused | refused | refused |
| student | same | refused | refused | refused |
| skew_normal | ar(t, g), se(sigma = TRUE) | fits, -168.3539934 | fits, -168.3539934 | refused ("Please set cov = TRUE in ARMA structures when including known standard errors.") |
| skew_normal | ar(t, g, p = 2), se(sigma = TRUE) | fits, -168.2879229 | same | refused |
| skew_normal | ar(t, g), se(s) (sigma = FALSE) | fits, -178.7750438 | same | refused |
| skew_normal | ma / arma (cov = FALSE) | refused (MA on a latent residual, in brms's words) | same | refused |

Scope: only families that take `se()` and are not gaussian or student,
which among the built-ins means `skew_normal()`. A custom family with
`accepts_aterms = "se"` goes the same way. This behavior predates the
lane.

What frmtmb computes (from the code):
- `check_autocor_response()` sends a latent-residual family to
  `check_autocor_latent()`, which has no `se()` check.
- The `se()` refusal is in `check_autocor_cond()`, which only gaussian
  and student reach.
- The model is then brms's own `cov = FALSE` latent residual for
  skew_normal. brms's program without `se()` (`-short-c-after.log`):
  `err = sderr * zerr`, `zerr ~ std_normal`, `mu += err` and `mu[n] +=
  Err[n, 1:Kar] * ar` with `Err` lagged `err`. frmtmb's "condar" latent
  chain is the same.
- Each row then has skew_normal's own density with scale
  `sigma_with_se()` (R/families.R line 1194): `sqrt(sigma^2 + se^2)`
  under `sigma = TRUE`, `se` alone under `sigma = FALSE`. That is how
  brms treats `se()` on skew_normal everywhere else, including beside
  `cov = TRUE` (L1 2.8e-14 per the lane).

So it is a defined model: two pieces, each brms's, composed per row. It is
not an accident of indexing. brms refuses it with a family-blind check
written for the response-residual form. In that form, under cov = FALSE,
`Err = Y - mu` would carry the known error into the lag, and the refusal
is about that.

For the user's decision:
- Keep: frmtmb fits a model brms refuses. It needs a compat note, which
  the lane wrote as "an open parity question".
- Refuse: one condition in `check_autocor_latent()`, in brms's words.
