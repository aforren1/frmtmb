## Verdict: NOT MERGEABLE

One blocking finding, B1: the second derivative of a curve is now
wrong wherever the extra covariance is nonzero, on the default step.
It is a regression on an exact `gp()` past its positions (base gave a
finite, understated answer; the lane gives 0) and a refusal turned into
a wrong answer at an unseen grouping level. Everything else that I
attacked holds, with the minor findings listed. The fix for B1 is
local to `frmtmb.spline` (the stencil applied to `extra_cov`).

## Blocking

### B1. `frm_curve_deriv(order = 2)` loses the extra covariance to rounding

The stencil differentiates a covariance with the step tuned for the
design, and at order 2 the variance collapses to noise.

`frm_curve_deriv()` now adds `L (E - Ew) L'`, with `L` the stencil of
the step tuned for differencing DESIGN rows (`sp_eps()`: 1e-6 of the
range at order 1, 1e-4 at order 2). Differencing a covariance divides
its rounding by `e^2` at order 1 and by `e^4` at order 2, so at order 2
the rounding of `E` (about 1e-16 of `|E|`) becomes about `|E|` itself.

`dev/gpby-rev-deriv2.R` (log `dev/gpby-rev-deriv2.txt`) and
`dev/gpby-rev-deriv2b.R` (logs `-lane.txt`, `-base.txt`):

* An unseen level of `y ~ t + (1 + t | g)` (25 groups, seed 23). The
  curve is linear in `t`, so the second derivative carries no new-level
  variance: closed form 0. Lane: `.se` range `[0, 1.495]`. Base
  refused this call (`sp_new_level_stop()`), so a refusal became a
  wrong answer.
* An exact `y ~ gp(x)` (50 points on [0, 4], seed 5), grid
  `x = 3.0, 4.2, 4.8, 5.5`, default step (`eps` 2.5e-4):

      lane order 2 se: 0 0 0 0.875321
      base order 2 se: 0.284889 0.664495 0.703796 0.818123
      lane, eps = 6e-3: 0.284937 0.691979 0.830141 0.958575

  The extra part alone, from the kernel's closed-form derivatives, is
  `sqrt(v2)` = 0.00543, 0.193, 0.440, 0.500, so the right answer is
  about the `eps = 6e-3` row. The lane returns 0 (a negative variance
  clamped by `pmax()`), below even the coefficient part that base
  reported. `simultaneous = TRUE` on a 21-point grid gives a critical
  value of 19.34 with one `.se` of 0 (base 2.51).
* The stencil itself against the closed form, `L E L' / v`:

      order 1 step 6e-6: -0.85 1.004 0.9998 0.9999
      order 1 step 6e-5:  0.997 1.00004 1 0.999999
      order 2 step 6e-4: -160.7 0.930 1.711 0.844
      order 2 step 6e-3:  1.009 1.00008 1.00003 0.99997

  At order 1 the error is about 1e-4 relative where the extra part
  matters (the new-level slope `.se` is 0.99993 of
  `sqrt(Var(b_t) + Sg[2, 2])`), and harmless beside the coefficient
  part where it does not (the -0.85 is at a position inside the data,
  where `v1` is 1.7e-6 of a total variance of 0.013). So order 1 and
  the peak standard error of `frm_curve_feature()` (an order-1 stencil
  on `E - Ew`) are usable; order 2 is not.

No test reaches it: `test-deriv.R` (38 pass) and `test-new-levels.R`
(57 pass) test the new paths at order 1 only.

Fix direction: difference the sources, not their covariance. For a new
level that is the stencil applied to the rows of `M` before `M S M'`
(as `D V D'` is formed); for the kriging residual it is the stencil
applied to the cross kernel and the weights, or the kernel's analytic
derivatives. A separate, larger step for `E` alone would be a weaker
fix. Then a test at order 2 on both constructions above, seen to fail
on the lane build.

## Minor

**m1. `test-gp-by-draws.R` depends on a pinned seed of a chain the lane
knows sticks.** Both fixtures run `frm_sample(fit, ..., seed = 4)` from
the FIT route, which keeps flat priors. The findings record that seed 2
of the second fixture's own design sticks at `sdgp = 0`; seed 4
samples. I reproduced the failure mode on a third design:
`frm_sample(fit, chains = 1, iter = 600, seed = 4)` on `y ~ gp(x)`
(60 points, seed 5) gave every draw at one field, so the across-draw
sd at an unseen row was exactly 0 on the lane and base stopped with
"system is exactly singular" (`dev/gpby-rev-sample.R`, first run).
That is the pinned-seed class of test that broke on the macOS and
Ubuntu runners before. Use the formula route, whose defaults are now
brms's (my rerun on it sampled: sd of `sigma` 0.025).

**m2. The sampler's kriging draw includes the nugget as white noise.**
`krige_draw` draws from `gp_krig_cov()` with the `1e-6 sd^2` nugget
in it, while the lane's own spline code removes that nugget from every
derivative because it is white noise. Two rows 1e-9 apart differ by up
to 7.85e-3 across 300 draws of `posterior_epred()` (row sd 0.45;
`dev/gpby-rev-sample-lane.txt`). brms jitters by 1e-8 absolute, about
1e-4 in sd. A draws-based curve over a fine grid is jagged at about
1e-3 of the field's sd. Consider drawing from `extra_cov -
extra_white` plus a jitter of brms's size.

**m3. "One value" at a repeated position holds to 1.5e-8, not
exactly.** Two rows at one unseen position differ by up to 1.498e-8
in a draw (the eigen root of a rank-deficient block,
`dev/gpby-rev-sample-lane.txt`). The code comment says the eigen root
keeps them one value. Either draw once per distinct position and copy,
or reword.

**m4. Cost of the kriging draw.** `posterior_epred()` at 300 unseen
rows over 300 draws: 7.85 s on the lane against 0.47 s on base
(`dev/gpby-rev-sample.R`, one run each, same process type). The cost
is one `eigen()` of the unseen block per draw, O(n^3) in the unseen
rows; a pivoted Cholesky, or one draw per distinct position, is
cheaper. Not a correctness defect.

**m5. `extra_cov = TRUE` always allocates two dense `n x n`
matrices.** 2000-row grid on `y ~ gp(x)`: 0.66 s, peak Vcells 386 MB,
62 MB returned (`dev/gpby-rev-core.txt`). `frm_curve()` on that grid
peaks at 398 MB against base 225 MB; on `y ~ s(x)`, where every entry
of `E` is zero, 332 MB against 255 MB (`dev/gpby-rev-core2-*.txt`;
wall 13.5 s against 11.8 s and 11.4 s against 12.2 s, single runs,
load noise not separated). Return `NULL` or a zero sentinel when
nothing contributes, and form `extra_white` only where an exact `gp()`
is unseen.

### B1: fixed

`frm_extra_cov_deriv()` against references written independently in
`dev/gpby-rev2-deriv.R` (explicit SE-kernel derivatives, not Hermite
polynomials). Log `dev/gpby-rev2-deriv.txt`; each figure is the
largest entry difference of the full `n x n` matrix over its largest
entry:

    gp(x, z, iso = FALSE), var x, order 1 / 2   4.9e-11 / 3.5e-12
    gp(x, z, iso = FALSE), var z, order 1 / 2   2.6e-11 / 3.3e-11
    gp(x, z, iso = TRUE),  var x, order 1 / 2   3.2e-12 / 8.9e-12
    gp(x, z, iso = TRUE),  var z, order 1 / 2   3.1e-11 / 1.5e-11
    gp(x, by = f), order 1 / 2                  1.0e-12 / 3.5e-12
      (each sub-GP with its own theta and positions; cross-level
      block exactly 0)
    gp(x, by = w), order 1 / 2                  1.0e-12 / 1.4e-12
    gp(x) + (1 + x | g), unseen level, o 1 / 2  9.2e-11 / 3.9e-11
      (gp part plus Sg[2, 2] at order 1, plus 0 at order 2)

The exact form keeps its positions in data units, so it needs no
chain rule through a center or a scale: its `dmax` is only a
reporting divisor. The Hilbert-space form has no extra part.

Difference curves of derivatives (`dev/gpby-rev2-deriv-b.R`): `.se^2`
against `D V D'` (with `D` differenced from `frm_lp_basis()` rows
here) plus the hand-written extra part:

* two unseen levels, order 1: 1.00000000 on 3 of 3 rows;
* a seen level against an unseen one, order 1: 1.00000000;
* both cases at order 2: `.se` exactly 0. That is the right answer:
  the gp residual cancels within one field, and a linear random slope
  has no curvature.

frmtmb.spline refuses a derivative difference across two different
`x` grids by design, so the gp-at-two-positions case does not arise
there. The lane's own figures (order-2 `.se` 0.284941 to 0.958588,
crit 2.4994, `test-deriv.R` seen to fail on the pre-fix build,
pass=39 fail=5 error=2) reproduce in its logs.

**m2: the code holds; the nugget measurement was too small for a
decision.** The draw is now the fit's own law: the smooth conditional
covariance plus the nugget as white noise per distinct position. The
lane's measurement fit one data set per design in five designs. I
replicated it with 15 seeds of three designs at four nuggets, 180

| nugget | conv 0 | pdHess | logLik less 1e-6's | se past the data / 1e-6's |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1e-6 | 45 (15/15/15) | 45 | 0 | 1 |
| 1e-8 | 39 (13/14/12) | 45 | within 1.3e-4 | [0.994, 1.041] |
| 1e-10 | 6 (3/0/3) | 43 | within 1.5e-4 | [0.318, 8.40] |
| 1e-12 | 0 | 9 (1/1/7) | +1.4e-4 to +6.5e-4 | [0.020, 5.15] |

* At 1e-8, 6 of 45 fits report non-convergence. Their largest
  gradients are 1.3e-4 to 2.0e-3: near-converged, but each is a
  user-visible warning. The lane's "1e-8 converges on all five" is a
  five-fit artifact.
* 1e-12 converges on none of the 45.
* The lane's brms comparison is one brms fit and 6 rows. That is
  enough for the direction, because the widening is structural: the
  nugget conditions on noisy latent values. Across draws, all three
  nuggets are within 2.2 percent of brms's spread, and that spread is
  what an interval is built from.

**My recommendation to the user: keep 1e-6.** It is the only value
that converges on every fit. 1e-8 gives a per-draw conditional sd
closer to brms's at the cost of a convergence warning on about one fit
in eight. Drawing with a smaller nugget than the fit uses would draw
from a law the fit does not have. If per-draw parity with brms is
wanted later, the place for it is a sampled fit with a 1e-12 jitter,
where no optimizer is involved, not a maximum-likelihood fit.
