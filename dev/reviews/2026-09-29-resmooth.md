**Qualifications on the claim's wording.**

1. "now within 2 percent" is true of the POPULATION-smooth fit only.
   `dev/resmooth-mcse.R` rerun here reproduces the worker's log
   bit-for-bit, and its own table says 0.0209 for `s(x, k = 8)` and

   effect, not a missing term: it is present in both arms and is the
   pre-existing accuracy of this route on a fit with random intercepts.
   The claim should read "within 2 percent on a population smooth and 5
   percent beside a group effect".
2. The review brief's phrase "`Est.Error` changes on every fit with a
   smooth" is too broad. It changes on the finite-difference route only:
   an ordinal, categorical or multinomial `fitted()`, and an
   `autocor(cov = FALSE)` standard error. `NEWS.md` states that narrower
   scope and is correct; the measurements above are the evidence.

**A cost the findings do not report.** `dev/resmooth-rev-cost.R`. The
instrument was checked first: `proc.time()` ticks at 10 ms here, so each
arm grows a block of repeats past 1.2 s and reports the minimum of three
blocks divided by the repeat count, and a CONTROL with no smooth is
carried.

    quantity                                        base      lane   ratio
    ---------------------------------------------   -------  -------  -----
    fitted(newdata, NA), cumulative gp(x), n_b=160  0.0339s  0.9800s  28.9

The control's 1.18 bounds the machine noise, so the 28.9 is real. The
cause is in `re_b_batches()`: a row of a smooth or `gp` block loads many
of that block's columns, so `max(rowSums(Sb)) > 1` fires and the
function returns `NULL`, which abandons batching for the WHOLE fit. The
dense fallback then costs two model evaluations per differenced
coefficient. For an exact `gp()` the coefficient count is the number of
observations, so this grows quadratically in `n`. Not a correctness
finding and not a blocker; it is a number the organizer should have,
because the same change makes the ordinary group-level levels of a
smooth-plus-`(1|g)` fit fall out of batching too.

## 5. The partial-`re_formula` refusal: facts, no decision

The message, from `R/re-formula.R:250`:

> `predict()`: re_formula = ~(1 | f) keeps some group-level terms, and
> this fit also has group-level content a formula cannot name: s(x,g). A
> partial formula cannot say whether that content stays, so it is
> refused rather than guessed: use re_formula = NULL to keep every term,
> or NA to drop the group-level terms and keep the smooths, as brms does

**Is it true of the code?** For a `car()` or `spde()` field, yes. For a
factor smooth, **no**, and the message contradicts itself within one
sentence: its second clause states that `NULL` keeps the smooth and `NA`
keeps it too, which is precisely the information a partial formula does
not need. It is less wrong than the old message, which claimed `NA`
drops the content, but "cannot say whether that content stays" is false
of a smooth. It also still calls a smooth "group-level content", which
the same lane's `?frm_linpred` rewrite says it is not.

**The whole accept-and-refuse surface, measured, identical in both
arms.** `dev/resmooth-rev-probe2.R` section 10, on
`gaussian(bf(y ~ s(x, g, bs = "fs", k = 5) + (1|f) + (1|h)))`:

    re_formula             result          smooth kept?
    --------------------   -------------   ------------------------
    NULL                   OK              yes
    NA                     OK              yes (max|-NULL| 0.529)
    ~0                     OK, = NA        yes
    ~1                     OK, = NA        yes
    ~(1|f) + (1|h)         OK, = NULL to 0 yes
    ~(1|f)                 REFUSED         unanswerable by fiat
    ~(1|nosuch)            REFUSED (name)  n/a

and on a fit with ONE bar term, `~(1|f)` is accepted and equals `NULL`
exactly. So the smooth is kept at **every** `re_formula` frmtmb accepts,
and the refusal fires on exactly the one shape where the formula drops
some bar columns. The reason the refusal was written for no longer
exists for a smooth; what remains is the 0.63.0 user decision. Two
tests pin the fragment "cannot name" and both pass.

Facts given. No decision taken here, as instructed.
