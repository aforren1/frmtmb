## What I verified, and what held

Every substantive claim the lane made reproduced. The parts I attacked
hardest are the parts that held up best.

- **The identity, through the shipped code.** `frm(bf(pgram ~ lf),`
  `family = whittle())` equals a hand-written `sum(log S + I/S)`
  optimizer to 1.6e-7 in the coefficients, and `logLik()` is exactly
  that quantity with no dropped constant. It also equals
  `glm(family = Gamma(link = "log"))` to 2.5e-6, which is the reason I
  could use `glm` as a fast stand-in for the Monte Carlo below.
- **`frm_periodogram()` is exact.** Against `stats::spec.pgram(taper =`
  `0, detrend = FALSE, demean = FALSE, fast = FALSE)` the ordinate
  ratio is 1 and the frequency difference is 0 at n = 16, 17, 1023 and
  1024, at fs = 1 and fs = 256. Odd and even both correct: R returns
  512 rows at n = 1024 and 511 at n = 1023, this returns 511 in both,
  and the dropped indices are provably 0 and m/2 (checked against a
  hand-built `Mod(fft())^2` at n = 64). Taper normalization holds
  (`mean(pgram)/(var(y)/fs)` = 1.0000 none, 1.0038 Hann, 1.0009 split
  cosine, mcse 0.0001 to 0.0031). Segments give `floor((m/k - 1)/2)`
  rows and CV 0.975 / 0.493 / 0.340 against 1 / 0.5 / 0.354. The
  multi-series, group, unequal-length, factor-column and NA-group paths
  all behave as documented.
- **The shape really is pinned**, not estimated: `fixef()$shape` is
  `log(4)` to 1e-12, `map$betad` is `NA`, the fit has one fewer free
  parameter than the free-shape Gamma, `predict(type = "disp")` returns
  4, `summary()` prints `Fixed dpar: shape = 4`, and `logLik()` equals a
  hand-written shape-4 Gamma Whittle to 1e-8. `bf(shape = 9)` still
  overrides it and `bf(shape ~ 1)` still estimates it.
- **The parse seam is inert for everything else.** `fixed_dpars` is set
  in exactly one place (`R/spectral.R:375`) and read in exactly one
  (`R/parse.R:1622`); no other family in core or in any extension sets
  it. `test-families.R` and `test-parse.R` are byte-identical to main
  and give identical results against a main build and a lane build
  (32 and 43 passes, 0 failures, both ways).
- **`frm_series_draw()` is right distributionally, not just in the
  mean.** Over 400 draws, `I/S` pooled across 204,400 ordinates has
  mean 0.9975 and variance 0.9921, and a KS test against Exp(1) gives
  D = 0.0094, p = 0.76. The round trip lands on the fit's own grid,
  the length is odd, the mean is zero to 1e-12, two draws are
  uncorrelated and `seed` reproduces.
- **All four named refusals fire**, each naming both replacements
  (`rexp`/`rgamma` and `frm_series_draw`), while `fitted()`,
  `predict()`, `residuals()` and `conditional_effects(method = "epred")`
  keep working. `whittle(tapers = 2.5)` and `whittle(tapers = 0)` are
  refused at construction, and `cens()` is refused although
  `exponential()` would accept it.
- **Diff hygiene is exactly as claimed.** `NAMESPACE`, `R/parse.R` and
  `_pkgdown.yml` modified; `DESCRIPTION` untouched, so no extension can
  reach `Suggests`; the new files are the six the lane listed and
  nothing else; no `dev/*.log`, no scratch scripts. `roxygenise()` on a
  copy regenerates `NAMESPACE` and all 85 `man/*.Rd` byte-identical, so
  roxygen is idempotent. The `_pkgdown.yml` block indexes all three
  exports and the vignette (8 reference sections, 10 articles); the two
  pre-existing index quirks (`pp_check`, `set_rescor`) are on main too.
- Every source line number the lane reported is exact: `R/spectral.R`
  :16, :52, :157, :258, :360, :460 and `R/parse.R:1622`.

### The refusal does not false-fire on a correct model

This was the failure mode I was told to hunt, and I could not produce
it on any legitimate spectrum. 100 replicates per cell, raw
periodograms, response passed straight to `whittle()$valid_y`:

| legitimate raw periodogram | ordinates | refused |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AR(1) phi = 0, 0.5, 0.9, 0.99 | 255 and 2047 | 0% (all 8 cells) |
| AR(1) phi = 0.999 | 255 | 10% |
| AR(1) phi = 0.999 | 2047 | 0% |
| AR(2) resonance r = 0.9, 0.98, 0.995 | 1023 | 0% |
| 1/f background + strong alpha peak, amp 1, 3, 10 | 1023 | 0% |
| 10 stacked series of n = 64 | 310 | 0% |
| 10 stacked AR(1) 0.9 series of n = 64 | 310 | 0% |
| band subset 0.2 to 0.3 of AR(1) 0.9 | subset | 0% |

## Findings, ranked

### 1. The dispersion refusal is structurally inert below 200 ordinates, and that is exactly where the headline failure is worst (MEDIUM)

`R/spectral.R:273` returns early when the response has fewer than 200
rows. The lane documents *why* (`R/spectral.R:254`, and the calibration
table in the findings), and the reasoning is sound. What is nowhere
stated is the consequence: at the shortest epoch in the lane's own
bias table the refusal never fires at all.

Measured, 100 replicates, untapered power law of exponent 3:

| series n | ordinates | refused |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 256 | 127 | **0%** |
| 512 | 255 | 93% |
| 1024 | 511 | 97% |
| 2048 | 1023 | 96% |

n = 256 is a one-second epoch at 256 Hz, it is a row of the lane's own
tapering table, and it is the row with the worst raw bias in that table
(-1.04). There, nothing warns the user.

The same hole is worse for the error the check was actually built for,
an averaged periodogram declared raw, because averaging *costs* the
resolution the check needs:

| series n | segments k | ordinates | refused |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 512 | 2, 4, 8 | 127, 63, 31 | 0%, 0%, 0% |
| 1024 | 2 | 255 | 100% |
| 1024 | 4 | 127 | **0%** |
| 1024 | 8 | 63 | **0%** |
| 2048 | 8 | 127 | **0%** |
| 4096 | 2, 4, 8 | 1023, 511, 255 | 100%, 100%, 100% |

To be checkable at all, k = 4 needs n >= 1604 and k = 8 needs
n >= 3208. Above the floor the check is perfect (100% in every cell I
ran); below it, it is absent. The lane's power table is honest that it
was measured "at 2047 ordinates", but nothing tells a reader that the
number is 0% at the lengths they are most likely to use.

The vignette's firing rates (97%, 81%, 14%) have the same problem: they
are properties of the check *at 1023 ordinates*, quoted as though they
were properties of the check.

Reproduction: `frm_periodogram(rnorm(1024), segments = 4)` then fit
with `whittle()` (no `tapers`); it is accepted silently.

Aggravating: the 200-row floor is documented **nowhere user-facing**.
`man/whittle.Rd:27` says only that the family has "the
periodogram-specific refusals attached" and never says what they test
or when they are silent; the vignette never mentions the threshold.
It exists only in a `@noRd` comment and in `dev/freq-findings.md`.

### 2. The vignette's own worked example sits below that floor and undercuts its own lesson (MEDIUM)

`vignettes/spectral.Rmd:245-248` builds the peak example with
`segments = 8`, then subsets to `pk[pk$freq < 45, ]`. Measured: 510
rows before the subset, **179 after**. So the response handed to
`whittle(tapers = 8)` is below the 200-row floor.

Two lines later (`:250`) the vignette says `segments = 8` and
`tapers = 8` "are one decision written twice, and they have to agree."
A reader who follows that block and forgets `tapers = 8` gets a
silently wrong fit with no refusal. I confirmed it: the same `pk` with
`family = whittle()` is accepted.

This is the sharpest instance of finding 1, and it is in shipped
teaching material at the exact point where the seam is being taught.

Reproduction: run the vignette's peak block, then
`frm(bf(pgram ~ logf), family = whittle(), data = pk)` and watch it
succeed.

### 3. `pp_check()` is a fifth refusing entry point and is documented nowhere (LOW-MEDIUM)

`R/spectral.R:319`, `man/whittle.Rd:47` and
`vignettes/spectral.Rmd:388` all say `simulate()` is refused "and so
are the three entry points built on it", naming `frm_simulate()`,
`frm_bootstrap()` and `conditional_effects(method = "predict")`.

`pp_check()` also refuses, with the same message. It is exported and
indexed, and it is the verb a user reaches for first when checking a
fit. The enumeration is simply incomplete in all three places. The
refusal itself is fine and hands over the right alternatives; only the
list is wrong.

Reproduction: `pp_check(fit)` on any whittle fit gives
`simulate(): family 'whittle' has no simulator yet. This one is`
`deliberate...`.

### 4. The justification for the check being one-sided is overstated (LOW)

`R/spectral.R:245` claims the two extra terms, "a spectrum that moves,
rows that are not in frequency order", "can only ADD". The second half
is false. A random reordering does add (measured: a row-shuffled raw
periodogram is refused 0% of the time, 100 replicates). A **monotone**
reordering subtracts, heavily: sorting a legitimately raw periodogram
by its own response is refused **100 out of 100 times**.

Practical exposure is small, since sorting a periodogram by power is an
odd thing to do. The problem is that a provably false statement is
carrying the weight of the one-sidedness argument. The true statement
is narrower: any reordering that is not monotone in the response can
only add.

Reproduction:
`whittle()$valid_y(sort(frm_periodogram(rnorm(4095))$pgram), NULL)`.

### 5. The refusal message quotes a threshold it does not use (LOW)

The test at `R/spectral.R:275` is `v < trigamma(tapers)`, that is, half
the model value. The message at `:279` reports `2 * trigamma(tapers)`
and says ordinates of that shape "have at least" it. So a user reading
"var(diff(log(y))) is 0.0517, and ordinates of shape 1 have at least
3.29" cannot tell that the trigger was 1.645, and "have at least" reads
as a bound on a sample when it is a statement about an expectation.

### 6. `frm_series_draw()` returns a `ts` whose frequency is not the recorded sampling rate (LOW)

For a fit built from an even-length series the drawn series has an odd
length, so the sampling rate implied by the grid is `fs * m / (m + 1)`:
from a 1024-point series at 128 Hz the draw comes back at **127.875
Hz**, not 128. The round-trip claim is still true, because
`frm_periodogram()` reads `fs` from the `ts`. But a user who passes the
rate they actually recorded at (`fs = 128`) gets a grid wrong by 0.1%
at the top of the band. The help says the rate "follows from the grid
spacing", which is accurate but easy to skim past.

### 7. "40 of 42 cells" is at the edge of Monte Carlo resolution (LOW)

The lane self-corrected from 42 to 40 and named the two exceptions,
which was the right direction. One of them does not survive a reseed:
at n = 512, phi = 0.95 I measure Whittle -0.0055 against exact ML
+0.0085, so Whittle wins there, where the lane has -0.0072 against
+0.0043. With an exact-ML mcse of 0.0018 the two runs differ by about
2 standard errors. The count is seed-dependent and should be stated as
such. This does not touch the conclusion, which reproduced exactly.

### 8. Informational, no action

`frm_series_draw(fit)` fails by default on any model written in a
transformed frequency, which is the vignette's own main workflow
(`pgram ~ logf`); it needs `freq = pg$freq`. This is documented in the
`@param`, stated by a good error message, and covered by a test. Worth
knowing only because the vignette shows the call solely on the one fit
(`s(freq, k = 20)`) where the default happens to work.

## The leakage result, measured independently

My own synthesis: a power law `f^-chi` built at 16x the target length
and then **cut**, so the segment is not periodic. Exponent estimated
through `frm_periodogram()` and an exponential-Whittle log-linear fit.
100 replicates per cell; 1 non-convergence in 1800 fits.

| chi | n | raw estimate | raw bias | mcse | Hann estimate | Hann bias |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 256 | 1.0074 | +0.0074 | 0.0098 | 0.9745 | -0.0255 |
| 1 | 1024 | 1.0101 | +0.0101 | 0.0048 | 0.9919 | -0.0081 |
| 1 | 4096 | 0.9989 | -0.0011 | 0.0021 | 0.9948 | -0.0052 |
| 2 | 256 | 1.8886 | -0.1114 | 0.0100 | 1.9957 | -0.0043 |
| 2 | 1024 | 1.9121 | -0.0879 | 0.0070 | 2.0038 | +0.0038 |
| 2 | 4096 | 1.9272 | -0.0728 | 0.0056 | 1.9991 | -0.0009 |
| 3 | 256 | 1.9641 | **-1.0359** | 0.0273 | 3.1003 | +0.1003 |
| 3 | 1024 | 1.8846 | **-1.1154** | 0.0156 | 3.0331 | +0.0331 |
| 3 | 4096 | 1.8513 | **-1.1487** | 0.0123 | 3.0123 | +0.0123 |

The lane's claims all hold, and one is stronger than reported.

- **Saturation confirmed.** True exponents of 2 and 3 come back between
  1.85 and 1.96. The estimate carries essentially no information about
  which of the two is true. The lane said 1.91 to 1.97; the conclusion
  is identical.
- **A longer recording does not help, and here it is monotone.** At
  chi = 3 the bias goes -1.036, -1.115, -1.149 as n goes 256, 1024,
  4096. The lane reported -1.033 and -1.094 at the first two lengths;
  I reproduce those to about one mcse, and the n = 4096 point I added
  extends the trend rather than breaking it. This is the counter-
  intuitive claim and it survives.
- **At chi = 1, flatter than the f^-2 leakage floor, the raw estimate
  is unbiased.** Confirmed at all three lengths.
- **Hann is the remedy.** At chi = 3 it brings the bias to +0.100,
  +0.033, +0.012. The lane reported 0.087 and 0.029.
- The refusal fires live on the untapered chi = 3 case, exactly as the
  vignette shows.

### The refusal as a backstop

Firing rates at 1023 ordinates, 100 replicates, my synthesis, against
the lane's:

| true exponent | mine | lane |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1.0 | 0% | 0% |
| 2.0 | 15% | 14% |
| 2.5 | 73% | 81% |
| 3.0 | 96% | 97% |

Reproduced. And an averaged periodogram declared raw is caught 100% of
the time at k = 2, 4 and 8 whenever it is above the row floor.

**Does the vignette say clearly enough that this is not a test to lean
on?** Yes, for the skimmer. `vignettes/spectral.Rmd:222` is a bold
sentence, "Taper whenever the spectrum falls faster than about `f^-2`
across the band being fitted. Do not rely on the refusal to remind
you", followed immediately by the 97/81/14 numbers and "The refusal is
a backstop for the extreme case, not a test you can lean on." That is
as plain as it can reasonably be put. The gap is not candour, it is
finding 1: the quoted rates are silently conditional on having 1023
ordinates.

## The bias comparison, spot-checked

Whittle profile over phi with the innovation variance concentrated out,
on `frm_periodogram()` output, no taper, against
`arima(order = c(1,0,0), method = "ML")`. 300 replicates to n = 512,
200 at n = 1024, 100 at n = 4096.

| n | phi | Whittle bias | mcse | Whittle median | exact ML bias | mcse | exact median | smaller abs bias |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 64 | 0.90 | -0.0377 | 0.0052 | 0.8728 | -0.0589 | 0.0045 | 0.8557 | Whittle |
| 256 | 0.50 | -0.0071 | 0.0030 | 0.4954 | -0.0115 | 0.0030 | 0.4894 | Whittle |
| 256 | 0.95 | -0.0128 | 0.0016 | 0.9403 | -0.0062 | 0.0022 | 0.9365 | **exact ML** |
| 512 | 0.95 | -0.0055 | 0.0009 | 0.9462 | +0.0085 | 0.0018 | 0.9442 | Whittle |
| 1024 | 0.95 | -0.0028 | 0.0007 | 0.9488 | +0.0170 | 0.0021 | 0.9764 | Whittle |
| 4096 | 0.95 | -0.0005 | 0.0005 | 0.9499 | +0.0301 | 0.0023 | 0.9921 | Whittle |

- The n = 64, phi = 0.9 cell reproduces (-0.038 / -0.059 against the
  lane's -0.041 / -0.061).
- **The claimed exception at n = 256, phi = 0.95 is real and
  reproduces almost exactly**: -0.0128 against -0.0062, where the lane
  has -0.0129 against -0.0071. Exact ML genuinely wins that cell.
- The claimed exception at n = 512, phi = 0.95 does **not** reproduce
  (finding 7).
- Whittle mcse at n = 64 is 0.0052, inside the lane's stated 0.0042 to
  0.0064.
- **The phi = 0.95 characterization is correct and the lane does not
  claim a win it did not earn.** `arima()` drifts to the unit root:
  median 0.9764 at n = 1024 and 0.9921 at n = 4096, against Whittle's

  0.949 / 0.950. Crediting that column to the reference optimizer
  rather than to Whittle is the honest reading, and it is the reading
  the lane and the vignette both take.

# Re-check, 2026-09-08 (second round)

The lane FIXED all five punch items rather than documenting around
them. I re-ran the attack against the new threshold, because a check
that now fires where it used to be silent is where a false alarm would
hide.

**Verdict: MERGE**, with one follow-up recorded below that must not be
lost. The change is a strict improvement on every axis I can measure,
and the one defect I found is *not* a regression it introduced: it is a
pre-existing hole that I missed in round 1 and that the new rule
actually narrows at its worst point.

## The new rule, and whether it is honest

`whittle_smooth_frac()` (`R/spectral.R:280`) interpolates a fraction on
`log(nf)` from 0.123 at 24 ordinates to the old 0.500 at 384 and above,
applied at `:311` as `frac(nf) * 2 * trigamma(tapers)`, with a hard
floor of 24 ordinates at `:309`.

## The one finding: a Hann-tapered periodogram is refused

This is the "genuinely tapered periodogram whose smoothness differs
from the calibration" case, and it does fail.

A Hann window correlates adjacent ordinates by construction. Measured
on white noise at 1024 points:

| taper | cor(log I_j, log I_j+1) | E var(diff(log I)) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| none | -0.001 | 3.291 |
| **hann** | **+0.306** | **2.277** |
| split_cosine (p = 0.1) | +0.009 | 3.267 |

The check's null is independence, so the statistic for a *legitimately
raw* Hann-tapered periodogram sits at 2.28 rather than 3.29, against a
threshold of 1.645. The margin is 1.4x instead of 2x, and the sampling
spread crosses it:

| legitimate Hann periodogram | ordinates | new rule | old rule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| white noise, n = 66 | 32 | 0.05% | 0% (inert) |
| white noise, n = 130 | 64 | 0.18% | 0% (inert) |
| white noise, n = 256 | 127 | **1.03%** | 0% (inert) |
| white noise, n = 512 | 255 | **1.90%** | 2.43% |
| white noise, n = 1024 | 511 | 0.25% | 0.25% |
| white noise, n = 2048 | 1023 | 0.00% | 0.00% |
| exponent 3 + hann, n = 256 | 127 | 0.27% | 0% (inert) |
| exponent 3 + hann, n = 512 | 255 | **1.73%** | 2.00% |

Why it matters more than the rate suggests:

1. **It is the remedy the refusal itself prescribes.** The message ends
   by telling the user to pass a Hann taper to `frm_periodogram()`. A
   user with a steep spectrum at a two-second epoch does exactly that
   and has roughly a 1-in-60 chance of being refused again, by a
   message telling them to do the thing they just did.
2. **The lane's own test rides on it.** `test-spectral.R:283` fits a
   Hann-tapered periodogram through `whittle()`. It sits 28.9% above
   the trigger, and over 3000 fresh seeds of the same construction it
   would be refused 0.30% of the time, with a minimum V of 1.502
   against a trigger of 1.645. It passes because the seed is pinned.
3. **Both halves are documented, and their product is not.**
   `?whittle` "What it cannot see" calls the sorted-periodogram case
   "the one way to make it refuse something legitimate" (the test
   comment at `test-spectral.R:325` says the same), while "What it is
   not", two sections below, says "Tapering reduces that bias and
   correlates the rows". That is the mechanism, stated, next to a list
   that claims to be complete.

**This is not a regression.** Under the old rule the same cells gave
2.43% at 255 ordinates and 0.25% at 511; the new rule gives 1.90% and
0.25%, so it *reduces* the worst cell and adds about a point below 200
ordinates, where it previously refused to look at all. I missed this in
round 1 because I probed untapered spectra only. Blocking a strict
improvement over a defect it did not introduce would be the wrong call,
so it is a follow-up rather than a punch.

**Follow-up (required before this is advertised to the tapering
audience, since that audience is the exposed one):** add the taper
interaction as a third entry in `?whittle` "What it cannot see" and
drop "the one way" from that list and from the test comment; and make
the message not recommend a Hann taper when the response already looks
Hann-tapered. Note that `split_cosine` is unaffected, so suggesting it
instead is safe. A principled fix, if one is wanted later, is to read
the statistic off a lag-2 difference, which the Hann correlation does
not touch.
