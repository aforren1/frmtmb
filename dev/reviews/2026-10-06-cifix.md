## Item 1: one kriging row per distinct position

Verified:

- The patch is bit-for-bit neutral on grids without a repeated
  position. `dev/cifixrev-bitwise.R` (reference BLAS) compares 123
  outputs of 11 fixtures between rellib-r6 and the patch: four ordinal
  and mixture fits without random effects, sleepstudy `(Days |
  Subject)`, cbpp binomial GLMM with emmeans, `s(x) + s(z)`, `gp(x)`
  on a 41-row unseen grid with `frm_curve()`, `gp(x, by = f)` with a
  difference curve across f, `gp(x, x2)`, and `gp(x, by = w)` with a
  numeric w. 110 of 123 are `identical()`. The 13 others are
  `frm_joint_cov()` results that gained the `lost_pos` element (values
  identical, 0 of N entries differ) and two `frm_curve()` objects whose
  data columns are identical and whose attached fit environment is not
  comparable. The `gp(x, by = w)` fixture moved no entry, so the ulp
  movement the findings allow did not occur there.
- `gp(x, by = f)`: the dedup runs inside one sub-GP, so the same x in
  two levels is never merged; the cross-level kriging covariance is 0
  and two rows at one position in one level covary by exactly the
  variance (`dev/cifixrev-dedup.R`).
- Two-dimensional positions that share one coordinate are not merged.
- A by level the fit never saw is refused before kriging.
- With OpenBLAS the property holds where rellib-r6 broke it:
  test-gp-multidim.R on rellib-r6 + OpenBLAS pass=58 fail=4; patch
  62/0 on both BLAS (`stf-*.sum`).

Not as claimed:

- "Distinct is decided exactly" is false in core (m2). `pos_rowkey()`
  is `paste()` of the coordinates, which formats at 15 significant
  digits, so 1/3 and 1/3 * (1 + 2^-52) share a key. The patch merges
  them (A rows identical); rellib-r6 gave them A rows 9.1e-11 apart,
  which is the per-row rounding, not the position (two points 1e-14
  apart differ by 4.3e-11 on both builds). gp_krig_cov() and the
  observed-position match already used this key before the patch, so
  this is not new and is numerically harmless here, but the spline's
  `sp_row_key()` deliberately compares doubles exactly, so the two
  layers disagree on what "one row" is.
- Cost. By construction: one string key per row in pred_design() and
  in gp_krig_cov(), and for a grid with repeats one extra n x n
  (`S[idx, idx]`). The `by = <numeric>` branch now forms `outer(w, w)`
  and `S * outer(w, w)` whole, two n x n, which undoes part of the
  blockwise discipline of the gpby review's m5 (m9). Peak Vcells
  measured with `gc(reset = TRUE)` on a 2000-row grid were not
  distinguishable from load noise (patch 125.8 and 137.8 Mb, base 120
  and 155.7 Mb; `dev/cifixrev-krigmem.R`), so no regression is shown.

## Item 2: joint_cov_repair()

The block identity was checked against independent computations.

| Fit | lost | null columns | repaired vs `solve(Q[-lost, -lost])` |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1: test fixture, seed 21, n = 100 | theta_1, theta_2 flat | coordinate | max abs diff / max 1.50e-09; diagonal ratio - 1 in [-8.9e-16, 2.3e-09] |
| R3: `ls ~ mo(income) * age + (1 \| g)`, seed 71 | theta_1 flat | coordinate | 1.13e-15; diagonal ratio 1 to 1 |
| R1: gr(g, by = f) fixture, seed 11 | theta_2, theta_3 flat | one eigen-direction, loads on both | 7.8e-03; diagonal ratio 0.966 to 410 |

- F1's residual 1.5e-9 is the difference between the Hessian sdreport()
  put into Q and the one the rescue inverted: the Schur complement of
  Q's random block equals `fit_outer_hessian()$H` to 1.77e-11 relative,
  and that is amplified by the inverse.
- The repaired V is positive semi-definite to rounding (smallest over
  largest eigenvalue -4.1e-24 on F1, 3.7e-16 on R1); `solve(Q)` had
  eigenvalues from -2.87e9 to 1.84e8 on F1 and a ratio of -3.5 on R1.
- On a coordinate loss the repaired V IS the covariance with the lost
  parameters held. On an eigen-direction loss it is not (m3): the
  combination of theta_2 and theta_3 orthogonal to the removed
  direction keeps its variance and propagates into the slope effects
  (b "[f = a].3.x": 7.06e-04 repaired against 1.72e-06 held,
  `dev/cifixrev-r1.R`). The prediction SEs differ by at most 0.4%
  (0.28948 against 0.29045). This is the same pseudo-inverse convention
  the no-random-effect branch uses, so the code is consistent; the
  words "conditional on the lost ones" in NEWS, the frm_joint_cov() Rd
  and the joint_cov_repair() comment are exact only for coordinate
  losses.
- mgcv. On `y ~ s(x) + s(z)` with a linear truth in x (seed 3, n =
  200), mgcv ML puts sp at 5.0e5 and frmtmb runs the sd to exp(-11.87).
  That fit loses no SE (tier 1 accepts it, see item 4), so it takes the
  unchanged path; frmtmb's prediction SE over mgcv's Vp is 0.9936 to
  0.9940 on 7 rows, identical on both builds. mgcv does not give an
  independent check of the repair itself; the Q-deletion inverse above
  does.
- "Bitwise unchanged when nothing lost": confirmed on the 11 fixtures
  of item 1, every fixture with an empty `se_lost`.
- Predictions along a lost direction on a fit with random effects. The
  OLRE fixture of test-se-check.R (`y ~ x + (1 | id)`, seed 5, sigma
  and the id sd lost, `dev/cifixrev-olre.R`): `frm_linpred(dpar =
  "sigma", se.fit = TRUE)` and `fitted(dpar = "sigma")` give `0 0 0`
  with no warning on rellib-r6, under ML and under REML, and `NaN NaN
  NaN` with one "move along a direction the fit does not determine"
  warning with the patch. mu predictions are unchanged. This is lane
  nanse's B2 now holding with random effects; no test pins it and NEWS
  does not say it (m4).
- Under REML on that fixture `vcov()` and the repaired beta block
  agree on the diagonal (0.0131581, 0.0145232), because the loss is
  decoupled from beta there.
- Consumers on F1: `conditional_effects(fit, "x")` se__ range 0.0000
  to 0.0958 on rellib-r6 (a band of width 0), 0.0641 to 0.152 with the
  patch, no warnings. `frm_linpred()` 0.000758 to 0.0966 against 0.0638
  to 0.143.
- `ranef(condVar = TRUE)` still reads `diag.cov.random` from sdreport's
  own inverse (m6). On R1 condsd / sqrt(diag V_bb) runs from 0 to 99
  with the patch; on rellib-r6 V_bb itself had negative diagonal
  entries. So on such a fit ranef() and frm_linpred() now describe the
  same b with different covariances. Pre-existing, not a regression.
- `VarCorr()` and `emmeans()` on R3 (seed 71) are unchanged between
  builds; on R1 VarCorr warns once on both builds.
- A converged fit with random effects AND a lost fixed-effect
  direction was not found: the separation design with `(1 | g)` stops
  at nlminb code 7 on five seeds (`dev/cifixrev-sepre.R`), where the
  convergence warning explains the fit and no SE is rescued; the
  nonlinear ridge with `(1 | g)` loses no SE because tier 1 accepts the
  FD Hessian (item 4). The OLRE case above (sigma is a fixed-effect
  coefficient of the sigma linear predictor) is the closest, and it
  behaves correctly.

## Item 3: the core CI fixes

- Separation test. Reproduced (`dev/cifixrev-sep.R`): at the default
  budget OpenBLAS stops at code 9 after 1997 evaluations with
  "Optimizer did not report convergence: function evaluation limit
  reached", and the word separation never reaches the user; the
  reference BLAS stops at code 0 after 1939 and names separation. At
  4000 both reach code 0 (2090 and 1939). `glm()` says "fitted
  probabilities numerically 0 or 1 occurred" after 25 iterations. So
  the test change is honest about the test but moves it off the
  default, and the default diagnosis of a separated logistic is
  platform-dependent. That is an optimizer-facing defect the findings
  do not name (m5): the convergence check should name separation when
  a binomial-type mean coefficient has run past |10| with fitted
  probabilities at 0 or 1, whatever code nlminb returns. Not a CI
  blocker; 4000 also leaves a platform that needs more than 4000 to
  fail by name, which is what the new `expect_identical(..., 0L)` does.
- The two `allow_warnings()` (Subject:a and Subject:b sd at exp(-29),
  Hessian row +-7.1e-12). Measured independently: these are 2 of the 5
  fits whose verdict differs between the BLAS builds (item 4). The
  wrap is the right minimal fix; `require` is not set, which is
  correct because the warning is platform-dependent.

## Item 6: "a flat-direction fit depends on what ran earlier"

Reproduced, and the mechanism is not history (m1). `dev/cifix-determ.R`
alone, identical inputs, fresh processes: 12 runs gave theta_1 =
-11.424124791143393 eight times and -11.356207797215273 four times; 6
more with OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 gave 2 and 4 (`det2-*.txt`). Six fits in
one process always agree (`dev/cifixrev-determ4.R`, 8 processes, with
and without `gc()`: 48 of 48 identical). The joint gradient
`obj$env$f(order = 1)` at a FIXED point from a fresh object differs
between processes and never within one (`dev/cifixrev-determ3.R`, 16
processes: two digests, 78 and 18 evaluations; the objective value is
identical in all 96).

Pinned to one logical CPU each (`dev/cifixrev-affinity.ps1`), the
digest follows the CPU: CPUs 0, 1, 10, 11, 12, 13, 22, 23 give one
value and 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 14, 16, 18, 20, 21 the other, on two
separate runs. The machine is an Intel Core Ultra 9 285K, whose
P-cores Windows numbers 0-1, 10-13 and 22-23. So the rounding of the
AD gradient depends on whether the process ran on a P-core or an
E-core when some compiled code chose its kernel, which then holds for
the life of the process. That matches a one-time CPUID query, such as
Eigen's cache-size query that sets its product blocking; the library
was not identified. It is not global state in frmtmb and not a defect
in it. The worker's "after the earlier fits" arm happened to land on
the other core type.

Recommendation: correct the findings on the page, add an entry to
`dev/rtmb-pitfalls.md` (pin affinity, or repeat on several processes,
before calling a difference on this machine history-dependent), and
keep the lane's rule of asserting properties rather than values on
flat-direction fits. The CI runners are not hybrid.
