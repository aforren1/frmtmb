  arms pinned to core 10): 18 fits without a latent residual, lane against
  rellib-r18, digit for digit.

## Verdict: FIX

The algebra is right. On 12 new designs the sparse route agrees with an
independent dense inverse to 3.3e-14 and with the lane's dense route to
4.2e-14, the selected inverse is in the original order (exact to 4.6e-15 at
every stored entry), and 18 fits without a latent residual are bitwise
unchanged against rellib-r18. The memory is linear to 64000 rows. One claim
is false: on a zero-inflated or hurdle family with a group effect on `zi` or
`hu`, every fitted row falls out of the factor's pattern and is solved
directly, so `fitted()` is quadratic in the rows again (M1). The edited tests
in `test-latres.R` and `test-latres-newrow.R` compare the sparse route with
itself (m1).

## Findings

### MAJOR

**M1. On zero-inflated and hurdle families, `fitted()` is quadratic again.**
The code (`R/joint-sparse.R`, header), the findings and `?frmtmb-autocor` say
the pairs a row loads "are coupled in `Q_rr` by the row's likelihood", so a
fitted row reads the selected inverse, and the cost "grows with the rows,
not with their square". A hurdle likelihood separates `eta_mu` from `eta_hu`
on every row, and a zero-inflated one on every row with `y > 0`. The mean
`(1 - hu) * mu` loads both, so the pair (latent of row i, level of `hu`) is
not in `Q_rr`, nor in the fill. `dev/review-latperf-pattern.R`, one poisson
series, `ar(cov = TRUE)` on `mu`, `(1 | id)` with 50 levels on `zi` or `hu`,
seed 1:

| n | design | rows sent to the direct solve | `fitted()` s | fit s |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1500 | `zi ~ (1 \| id)` | 713 of 1500 | 0.28 | 5.00 |
| 1500 | `hu ~ (1 \| id)` | 1500 of 1500 | 0.41 | 3.84 |
| 1500 | `(1 \| id)` on `mu`, control | 0 | 0.21 | 5.20 |
| 8000 | `zi ~ (1 \| id)` | 3935 of 8000 | 5.26 | 7.82 |
| 8000 | `hu ~ (1 \| id)` | 8000 of 8000 | 5.06 | 4.89 |
| 32000 | `hu ~ (1 \| id)` | 32000 of 32000 | 57.48 | 15.39 |

Design "two" at the same sizes (`dev/review-latperf-scale.R`): 0.69 s at
8000, 3.75 s at 32000. The hurdle cost grows 11.4 times for 4 times the
rows; on that trend 100000 rows would take about 9 minutes, for a fit of
under a minute. The values are right (against the dense route at 1500 rows: 6.7e-16
and 4.4e-16 relative), and `frm_linpred()` on `mu` sends no row (0.13 s at
32000). The lane's pattern count (`dev/latperf-pattern.R`, "0 rows on every
design") had no family whose likelihood separates its dpars.

Fix: for the pairs outside the pattern, solve one column of `W` per distinct
position on the shorter side (here the 50 levels of `hu`), as `jcs_W()`
already does for a block, instead of one solve per row; the rows then read
`W[latent, level]` from those columns. Test: a hurdle fit above 200 rows,
the count of rows sent to `jcs_rowvar_solve()` (0 after the fix), and the
values against the dense route. Correct the sentence in the code header,
the findings and `?frmtmb-autocor`.

**m6. The sparse route inverts the Schur complement with base `solve()`.**
`dev/review-latperf-why.R`: `unstr(t, g)` with `(1 | id)`, code 1, 95 lost

`solve_joint_precision()` uses `Matrix::solve()` for this reason (its
comment). Not a wrong number from the lane: the Schur complement has a
negative eigenvalue (-9.9e-9), and the dense route still gives 420 finite

findings.

**m7. TMB internal, no floor.** `get0("solveSubset", envir =

fix makes this fallback cheap too if it solves by distinct position. CRAN
may object to the unexported call on manual review: a question for the user.
