# Part 1. The ndt finding

## The crux, settled: the third arm fits a valid model

**This was the question the review existed to answer, and the answer is
the benign one.**

`wiener(max_ndt = 0.45, allow_unreachable = TRUE)` does NOT install a
per-subject bound. It replaces one global bound with a laxer global
bound and lifts the refusal that guards it. With `allow_unreachable`
set and no variability parameter, `frmtmb.eam/R/wiener-family.R:343`
switches the density to

    ddm_lpdf_both(ddm_floor(y - dpars[["ndt"]] - delta, delta), ...)

with `delta = 1e-9 * min(y)`, so a row the model cannot reach is not
`-Inf` but the density evaluated at a decision time of `delta`. If any
row were floored, the arm's "log likelihood" would not be a log density
and 121.4 log units would mean nothing.

I checked directly, on the arm's own fit, at its own optimum
(`scratchpad/rvsl-eam-arm.R`, arm `unbounded`):

| quantity | measured |
|---|---|
| `min(y - ndt_row)` over all 12,000 rows | 0.02193851936 |
| rows with `y <= ndt` | 0 of 12,000 |
| rows at or below the floor `delta` = 2.26e-10 | 0 |
| fitted subject `ndt` range | 0.204296 to 0.284124 |
| subjects whose fitted `ndt` exceeds their OWN fastest response | 0 of 30 |
| `min(own fastest - fitted ndt)` | 0.0219385 |

The floor never activates. Every row is inside the support with a
margin of 22 ms, so the arm's log likelihood is a genuine Wiener
first-passage log density on all 12,000 rows. The two arms have the
same 7 parameters and the plain arm's parameter space, `ndt` in
`(0, 0.2262)`, is a strict subset of the third arm's, `(0, 0.45)`.
A higher log likelihood in the larger of two nested spaces at the same
parameter count is exactly what a binding constraint looks like.

There is also an internal check that makes this near-certain without
the measurement, and it is worth stating because it is cheap: a single
floored row would contribute about `-w^2 / (2 delta)`, of order -1e9,
to the sum. The arm reports -7027.37. One floored row could not hide.

**The finding stands.** The one thing the lane should say and does not:
the third arm demonstrates the CAUSE, it does not demonstrate the FIX.
It relies on `allow_unreachable = TRUE`, whose own help page
(`wiener-family.R:65-69`) says the flag exists for mixtures, where the
other component covers the unreachable rows. Here there is no other
component; the arm is safe only because the optimum happened to land
inside the per-subject-valid region, which is a property of this fit
and not a guarantee. Item 1.0a's per-subject bound would make it a
guarantee. Say so in `dev/scale-findings.md` and in
`extensions/frmtmb.eam/dev/scale-row.md`, because "the bound lifted"
reads as "the constraint removed", and a reader who copies
`max_ndt = 0.45, allow_unreachable = TRUE` into their own script has
copied a model that can silently evaluate rows outside its support.

The plan's own check for item 1.0a, "the log-likelihood is at least the
-7027.4 the bound-lifted arm reaches", is sound: that optimum satisfies
the per-subject constraint with 22 ms to spare, so a per-subject bound
can reach it.

## F1. MAJOR. The reason given for `unbounded_dpar` staying silent is wrong

The lane says, in `dev/scale-findings.md` and again in the plan's item
2.1 and again in the new Core seams row:

> It inspects a dpar's FIXED-EFFECT columns, and in all three the fixed
> intercept is fine and it is the VARIANCE COMPONENT that is at the
> edge.

The first half is right and the second half is not. I evaluated the
check's own condition on the check's own inputs
(`scratchpad/rvsl-eam-diagcond.R`, which calls `frmtmb:::par_est_se()`
the way `diagnose()` does):

| arm | dpar | link | est (link) | se (link) | `abs(est) > 10` | `se > abs(est)` | fires |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `sv` | ndt | scaled_logit | **11.200986** | **2.3724411** | TRUE | FALSE | no |
| plain | ndt | scaled_logit | **7.1660269** | NaN | FALSE | (NA) | no |

In the `sv` arm the fixed `ndt` intercept is NOT fine. It stands at
11.2 on a scaled logit whose bound is `min(y)`, that is
`plogis(11.2) = 0.99999` of the bound. `diagnose_unbounded_dpar()`
(`R/confint.R:812`) looks straight at it, its first condition passes,
and it is the SECOND condition that fails: the standard error is 2.37,
which is not larger than 11.2.

That is the real seam, and it is a different fix from the one filed.
`R/confint.R:820-822` and this project's own NEWS entry state the
premise explicitly:

> The pair is the evidence, not either half: a dpar legitimately far
> out on its link keeps a small standard error.

That premise is a statement about an UNBOUNDED link. It was calibrated
on `student()`'s `nu` over 84 fits, where running off means `|eta|`
grows without bound and the standard error explodes to 4878-1.2e4. On
a BOUNDED link the same failure does the opposite: the scaled logit
saturates at a modest linear predictor and its derivative vanishes, so
an ILLEGITIMATELY saturated dpar also keeps a small standard error.
Both of the check's conditions point the wrong way on a bounded link,
and this package now ships four bounded `ndt` links (`wiener()`,
`lba()`, `rdm()`, `gddm()`).

In the plain arm the check misses for a third reason again unrelated to
variance components: 7.17 is below the `abs(est) > 10` threshold, even
though `plogis(7.17)` is 0.99923 of the bound. The threshold is in
linear-predictor units and a bounded link saturates long before 10.

The lane's own text contains the counter-example and does not notice
it. Two paragraphs after asserting that the check reads fixed-effect
columns and the fixed intercept is fine, it writes: "It did fire once,
on an earlier variant of the rlddm row ... and it named
`ndt: (Intercept)` there." A check that names `ndt: (Intercept)` is a
check that reads the fixed intercept and can fire on it.

One more correction while the seam is being written. The lane says
"nothing in the 0.55.0 diagnostic set looks at that". `diagnose()`
already carries `extreme_theta = sd_i[abs(th[sd_i]) > 8]`
(`R/confint.R:1196`), which DOES look at random-effect log standard
deviations. Measured on these fits: the `ndt` component's log sd is
2.054628 on the `sv` arm and 1.13629 on the plain arm, that is 7.80 and
3.12 on the link, both far below a threshold of 8. The threshold is
link-agnostic; on a scaled logit a standard deviation of 7.8 already
pins most subjects at one end or the other.

**What to change.** Restate the finding in `dev/scale-findings.md`, in
the plan's item 2.1 note, and in the Core seams row as: *`diagnose()`'s
`unbounded_dpar` evidence pair is calibrated for an unbounded link and
inverts on a bounded one; a dpar pinned at the edge of a scaled logit
keeps a SMALL standard error, and the intercept saturates below the
`abs(est) > 10` threshold. `extreme_theta` looks at variance components
but with a link-agnostic threshold.* With the numbers above. As
written today the seam would send an implementer to add a
variance-component check and leave the `sv` arm's saturated fixed
intercept undetected.

## The disclosure

Both of these are worth writing whether or not Phase 1 lands soon,
because a user fitting a hierarchical DDM today gets a confident wrong
answer with no warning.

**NEWS bullet, `frmtmb.eam` (lift as is):**

> `wiener()` bounds the non-decision time by `min(rt)`, the GLOBAL
> fastest response in the data, through a scaled logit. With a random
> effect on `ndt` this is the wrong constraint: the information about a
> subject's non-decision time is that subject's own fastest response,
> and a subject whose true `ndt` is above the global minimum cannot be
> represented at any value of the random effect. At 30 subjects x 400
> trials with a between-subject `ndt` spread of 26 ms on a mean of
> 250 ms, 20 of the 30 subjects are in that position while NONE is
> inconsistent with its own data. Without `variability` the fit does
> not converge and says so: maximum absolute gradient 1.3e11, Hessian
> not positive definite, all seven standard errors NaN. With
> `variability = "sv"` it CONVERGES: code 0, maximum gradient 7.5e-05,
> positive definite Hessian, no bad standard errors, and `diagnose()`
> reports nothing. The population non-decision time then comes back
> pinned at the bound, 0.2236 against a truth of 0.25, with a
> delta-method standard error of 7.2e-06 on it, because the scaled
> logit's derivative vanishes where the estimate has been pushed. Do
> not put a random effect on `ndt` until the per-subject bound lands;
> the same defect reaches `rlddm()` in frmtmb.learn, which takes its
> diffusion parameterization from this package. Fitting the same data
> with the bound raised above every subject's truth recovers everything
> and finds a log likelihood 121.4 units higher at the same parameter
> count.

**Backlog entry, core (lift as is):**

> `diagnose()`'s `unbounded_dpar` check cannot see a distributional
> parameter pinned at the edge of a BOUNDED link. Its evidence pair,
> `abs(estimate) > 10` and a standard error larger than the estimate,
> is calibrated for `student()`'s `nu` on `logm1`, where running off
> makes the standard error explode. On a scaled logit the derivative
> vanishes at the edge, so the standard error COLLAPSES and the linear
> predictor saturates below 10. Measured on the Phase 0 eam rows:
> `ndt: (Intercept)` at 11.20 with a link-scale standard error of 2.37
> (the check needs it above 11.20), and 7.17 in the non-convergent arm,
> below the magnitude threshold entirely. Both fits sit at 0.9992 and
> 0.99999 of the bound. `extreme_theta` does look at random-effect log
> standard deviations, but its threshold of 8 is link-agnostic and the
> `ndt` component stands at 2.05. Four bounded `ndt` links ship today
> (`wiener()`, `lba()`, `rdm()`, `gddm()`), so the check needs to know
> whether a link is bounded and, if it is, to test proximity to the
> bound rather than magnitude and a large standard error.

## Assertions: no timing tolerance anywhere, but two other brittle shapes

  the interval is wide.

## R2. NEW, MINOR. A warm number reported as a cold one, again

The lane split `frm_curve(simultaneous = TRUE)` into a first call and a
later call, correctly. It did not split the arm next to it, and the
reason is structural rather than an oversight of that one call.

`scale_interleave()` runs its arms IN ORDER within each round. On the
spline row the arms are `curve_sim`, `curve_pw`, `feature`. So
`curve_sim`'s round-1 call is genuinely the first curve call on that
fit and its `first` is a true cold number. By the time `feature` runs,
`frm_curve()` has already memoized the joint-precision solve on the
fit, so `feature`'s round-1 call is WARM and `feature_first_s` is not a
cold number at all.

Measured directly, three fresh fits of the same design in one process,
each asked a different first question
(`scratchpad/rvsl-spline-cold.R`):

| what | seconds |
|---|---|
| A: `frm_curve_feature()` as the FIRST curve call on a fresh fit | **0.615914** |
| A: the same call again on that fit | 0.012563 |
| A: a simultaneous band after it | 0.113659 |
| B: `frm_curve(simultaneous = TRUE)` first on a fresh fit | 0.545017 |
| B: pointwise after it | 0.00425482 |
| B: `frm_curve_feature()` after both, which is what the tier times | 0.0128119 |
| C: five consecutive simultaneous calls on one fresh fit | 0.56295, 0.08761, 0.10980, 0.08227, 0.09461 |

C reproduces the lane's five-call demonstration in shape (its 0.454,
0.074, 0.066, 0.063, 0.079) and its "a fresh fit pays it again": the
cold-to-warm ratio is 6.84 here against the lane's 7.9 to 8.7, and a
fresh fit pays 0.55 s again. That part of the finding is solid.

The problem is that `dev/scale-findings.md` reports
`frm_curve_feature()` as **0.0096 s** with no split, in the same table
where the band beside it has one, and `dev/extension-gaps-plan.md:76`
carries it as "the feature search is NOT the cost: 0.010 s". A user who
calls `frm_curve_feature()` once on a fit pays **0.616 s**, sixty-four
times that, because it needs the same memoized solve.

The tier cannot see this, and that is the generalizable part.
`feature_spread` in the row I just ran is **1.0516**: the spread
statistic is the lane's own memoization detector, and it reports 1 for
every arm after the first one that touches a given cache, because an
earlier arm warmed it. So `scale_interleave()` detects memoization only
on the first arm that reaches a cache, and the spline row happens to
have two arms that reach the same one.

For comparison, the arms that are NOT memoized behave as they should,
which is how I know the instrument is otherwise fine: `hmm_probs()`
first 0.164 against a minimum of 0.115 (spread 1.43), `hmm_viterbi()`
0.0656 against 0.0627, `lca_probs()` 0.0140 against 0.0117,
`posterior_predict()` and `ranef()` controls of 1.03 in round 1. Only
the spline row has a shared cache.

**The conclusion is unaffected**: 0.62 s cold against a 6.0 s fit is
still not the cost, items 1.3 and 4.1 still stay where they are, and
the plan's worry was that the search "calls `predict()` per Newton
step", which is answered by the 12.6 ms warm number. Both numbers are
needed to say that. **What to change:** report the feature search the
way the band is reported, "0.62 s on the first curve call on a fit,
0.013 s after", in `dev/scale-findings.md` and in the plan's Phase 0
row; and add one line to `dev/scale-lane-notes.md` saying that
`scale_interleave()`'s spread is blind to a cache an earlier arm has
already warmed, so an arm that shares a cache with an earlier one needs
its own fresh fit rather than a place in the list.

## R3. CONFIRMED, and it is the better claim

Every number in the restated coupling finding reproduces. I ran the new
`coh-id` rung through the tier and separately as a standalone script,

| rung | contrast | interval | width |
|---|---|---|---|

`1 - 0.068695 / 0.145668 = 0.5284`, so "53 percent too narrow" is
right, and adding `(1 | id)` moves the width by 0.4 percent, which is
nothing.

The mechanism the lane gives is also right and worth keeping in those
words. The design is fully crossed and balanced, 40 subjects x 2
conditions x 60 frequencies, so a subject main effect is orthogonal to
a within-subject contrast and contributes nothing to its standard
error; `(1 | id:cond)` is the only term that does not cancel. That is
standard theory and the numbers land on it. The one caveat to keep in
mind when Phase 2 generalizes it: the exact cancellation is a property
of a BALANCED design, and an unbalanced one would leak a little of the
subject variance into the contrast. The finding is "look properly
hierarchical and still be 53 percent too narrow", and it is stronger
than the claim item 2.6 was written for. Item 2.6 now says it, with
both models and both intervals.
