## The claim being tested

> A correlated random-effect block across every parameter of a
> reinforcement-learning family recovers its fixed effects, its variance
> components and its correlations at 100 learners by 200 trials, and the
> importance correction succeeds at that scale.

**The verdict, in one paragraph.** TRUE for every parameter that has a
population truth on the scale it is fitted on, which is all five of
`bandit2arm_delta()`'s and nine of `rlddm()`'s eleven components, all
covering between 0.90 and 0.98 over 60 replicates per arm. FALSE, and
interestingly so, for the two `rlddm` components that have no population
truth: `sd(ndt)` and `cor(bs, ndt)` under `ndt_group()`. Those two are
not an estimator failure but an exact property of the parameterization,
`qlogis(ndt_i / floor_i) = log(ndt_i) - log(m_i)`, which makes the
link-scale component about 94 percent a property of the data rather than
of the learners. The importance correction settled on every replicate
run, with the row's own threshold worry discharged by a factor of 249.

**The sentence this row is for.** At the same scale, in the same model,
the Stan identity on the correlated block agrees to 4.9e-15 relative
while the link-scale variance component is 94 percent nuisance. The
estimator is exact to machine precision at computing a thing that is
not what a reader assumes it is computing.

## The designs

Both designs draw a block with real off-diagonal structure. A design
that draws independent deviations cannot answer whether an estimator
recovers a correlation; it only says whether the estimator invents one.
Correlations were chosen so no two pairs share a value and the signs
differ, so that shrinkage toward zero and a sign error are separately
visible in the table rather than only in a norm.

## Two truths per component, and why

For **bandit2arm_delta** the block the model fits IS the block the
design drew. `alpha` is estimated on the logit and `tau` on the log,
which are the scales the deviations were drawn on, so the drawn
correlation is a truth in the ordinary sense and the coverage column
beside it is a coverage.

For **rlddm** under `ndt_group(id)` it is not, and item 1.0b's second
warning is the reason. The model estimates `qlogis(ndt_i / floor_i)`,
where `floor_i` is that learner's own fastest response, which is an
observed minimum of the learner's own drawn data. A learner with a wider
boundary is slower, so its floor is later, so it needs a SMALLER
fraction to express the same non-decision time. Every table below
therefore carries two targets:

* **population**, the value the design was written with. Available for
  every bandit component and for the six rlddm components whose fitted
  link is the drawn link.
* **realized**, the same component computed from THIS replicate's own
  drawn deviations and its own observed floors, with no fit at all.

Coverage against a realized target is not a coverage and is not read as
one here: the target carries the sampling noise of 100 draws, and in the
limit where the deviations were observed rather than estimated the
estimate would equal it exactly and the rate would go to one. It is
reported because a rate far BELOW nominal there still says the fit is
not reading the block it fitted.

## Resolution: what 60 replicates can and cannot settle

At a nominal 95 percent, 60 replicates give a coverage standard error of
`sqrt(0.95 * 0.05 / 60)` = **2.8 points**. Nothing between about 89 and
100 percent is distinguishable from nominal in these tables, and no
conclusion below rests on a difference smaller than that. A bias is
called real only when it exceeds twice its own Monte Carlo standard
error, which is printed beside it.

### What `ndt ~ (1 | p | id)` estimates under ndt_group(id)

A learner's floor is its own non-decision time plus the fastest DECISION
it happened to make, `floor_i = ndt_i + m_i`, where `m_i` is that
learner's smallest first-passage time over its trials. So

```
qlogis(ndt_i / floor_i) = log(ndt_i / (floor_i - ndt_i))
                        = log(ndt_i) - log(m_i)
```

exactly. `dev/learnhier-target.R` checks it numerically at a worst
relative error of **1.9e-16** over 24 replicates.

The fitted deviation is therefore NOT the non-decision time on a link.
It is the log non-decision time MINUS the log of an observed minimum.
The first term is the random effect the design drew. The second is a
nuisance: it depends on how fast that learner happened to decide, which
is a function of their drift and their boundary and of 200 draws of
luck, and has nothing to do with their non-decision time.

`Var(eta) = Var(log ndt) + Var(log m) - 2 Cov(log ndt, log m)`, and over
24 replicates at 100 by 200:

| term | value |
|---|---|
| `sd(log ndt)`, the random effect the design drew | 0.1494, against a truth of 0.15 |
| `sd(log m)`, the observed-minimum term it did not | **0.4404** |
| their correlation across learners | 0.2621 |
| `sd(eta)`, the realized target | 0.4264 |
| **variance share of the target from the observed minimum** | **106.7 percent** |

Genuine between-learner non-decision-time variation is 12.3 percent of
that target's variance, the observed minimum is 106.7, and the
covariance takes back 19.0.

### The same identity explains the correlation, and it is not an estimator fault

`m_i` is the fastest decision, so it is driven by the boundary
separation. Pooled over 24 replicates, the correlation of `log(m_i)`
with each learner's own drawn deviation is **`bs` +0.872**, `drift`
-0.385, `alpha` -0.046. Since `eta_i = log(ndt_i) - log(m_i)`, `eta` is
mechanically anti-correlated with `bs`.

Strip the nuisance term out and the design's own correlation is
recovered exactly:

| quantity | value | drawn truth |
|---|---|---|
| realized `cor(bs, ndt)` in the fitted parameterization | -0.794 | |
| fitted `cor(bs, ndt)` | -0.931 | |
| **`cor(bs, log ndt)`, the nuisance removed** | **+0.2989** | **0.3** |

Item 2.2's warning 2 said a diagonal truth is the wrong truth and gave
about -0.78 for the induced correlation. That is confirmed, and this is
the reason for it.

### One cause, two symptoms

The identity does not only explain the variance component. It also
explains why a fit-health check fires on this design's best fits.

`eta_i = log(ndt_i) - log(m_i)`, and `log(m)` correlates **+0.872** with
each learner's own `bs` deviation, so the parameterization MECHANICALLY
presses `cor(bs, ndt)` toward -1: the realized value is -0.79, the
fitted one -0.93, and stripping the nuisance returns **+0.2989 against a
drawn 0.3**. A correlation parameter pressed against its boundary takes
the covariance matrix toward singularity, which makes the Hessian
near-singular in the unconstrained parameterization, which is precisely
what the positive-definite check objects to.

So one cause, two symptoms:

* the link-scale variance component is 94 percent nuisance, so
  `sd(ndt)` does not mean what a reader takes it to mean;
* the same nuisance term pins a correlation near its boundary, so the
  positive-definite Hessian check fires on good fits.

**It also explains the direction that otherwise looks paradoxical.** The
flagged replicates have the HIGHEST log-likelihood surplus over their
own oracles, mean 23.741 against 17.193 for the clean ones, and the
worst flagged one beats the clean mean. They are the replicates where
the nuisance coupling is strongest, so they are simultaneously the
best-fitting and the nearest to singular. The flag is reading the
geometry of the parameterization, not the quality of the fit.

**The boundary is not sufficient on its own**, and that is stated rather
than smoothed: the clean group contains a replicate at `cor(bs, ndt)` of
-0.9844, more extreme than three of the five flagged ones. Mean -0.9646
flagged against -0.9360 clean, and 2 of the 3 replicates below -0.98 are
flagged.

**This is an explanation of THIS lane's data and not a general claim
about the check.** The cross-lane pattern is filed separately below,
stated as the three lanes found it.

| the bias is structural, a property of the parameterization rather than of how well it is estimated | open, Test 2 |

**The retraction, stated plainly.** The information candidate was called
refuted on the grounds that `sd(ndt)` and `sd(alpha)` carry nearly the
same reported standard error on the log scale, 0.102 against 0.107,
while differing six-fold in bias. That argument is wrong: the standard
error the fit reports on a VARIANCE COMPONENT and the information the
data carry about an INDIVIDUAL LEARNER are different quantities, and the
first does not settle the second. Test 1's own regression measures the
second directly and says they differ a great deal by component, so the
candidate is back on the list.

**And that reliability figure does not, on its own, imply a biased
standard deviation.** Attenuated conditional modes are EXPECTED under
shrinkage in a correctly specified model, and maximum likelihood
estimates a variance component from the marginal likelihood rather than
from the spread of the fitted deviations. So the attenuation is reported
below as its own measurement and not as an explanation.

**A mechanism that needs no edge, named because it is the textbook
one.** Laplace and penalized quasi-likelihood are documented to
underestimate variance components in nonlinear mixed models, most
severely where there is little information per group. That is a
candidate in its own right, it subsumes the information candidate, and
`frm(importance =)` is the instrument that prices it directly. This lane
measured that instrument at 32x the Laplace fit, which on a 23-minute
fit is about 12 hours per replicate, which is why Test 2 uses the trial
count as a cheap proxy instead.

### Test 1's prediction, written down before the measurement

Recorded here before `dev/learnhier-test1.R` was run, so that it is a
test and not a description of whatever came out.

The surviving explanation for `sd(ndt)` coming back 22 percent low is
the Laplace approximation at the hard edge of the Wiener density: the
density is zero at and below `ndt`, so a learner's conditional
log-likelihood in its own `ndt` deviation is bounded at that learner's
fastest response, and a Laplace approximation is exact only where the
conditional is quadratic.

**If that is the cause, the damage must be worse for learners whose
conditional is more sharply bounded**, that is those whose observed
floor sits CLOSEST to their own true non-decision time. Write
`margin_i = floor_i - ndt_i`. The prediction is:

* the fitted deviation is attenuated toward the mean, and the
  attenuation is STRONGEST at the SMALLEST margin;
* so in a regression of the fitted deviation on the induced true one,
  the slope RISES with `margin`, and the interaction is positive;
* the effect is within a replicate rather than between, so every
  quantity is standardized inside its own replicate before pooling.

**The competing explanations predict no relationship with `margin` at
all.** The ordinary downward bias of a maximum likelihood variance
component, and boundary attraction on a strongly correlated pair, are
both properties of the block rather than of an individual learner's own
conditional.

So a flat slope refutes the edge candidate, and that would have to be
reported as such rather than keeping the candidate because nothing else
is left.

#### Test 1's result: the control failed, and that was worth more

2,400 learners over 24 replicates, `dev/learnhier-test1.R`.

| component | bounded conditional | attenuation | interaction with margin | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `alpha` | no | 0.650 | -0.0107 | (-0.0299, 0.0085) |
| `drift` | no | 0.950 | **+0.0098** | (0.0011, 0.0185) |
| `bs` | no | 0.969 | -0.0005 | (-0.0055, 0.0045) |
| `ndt` | yes | 0.609 | +0.0187 | (0.0073, 0.0302) |

**`drift`, a control, did not come back flat**, and the fault is in the
statistic rather than the model. `margin_i = floor_i - ndt_i` IS that
learner's smallest first-passage time, so it is a function of their own
drift and boundary, and any shrinkage that varies with a learner's speed
appears as a margin interaction on every component. The repair is the
paired excess over the three unbounded components, taken per replicate
so both come from the same learners: **+0.0154, 95% CI (0.0031,
0.0276), p = 0.016, positive on 16 of 23 replicates.** That adjustment
was made because the CONTROL failed, an instrument-driven reason, and
not because of anything the `ndt` number said.

So the edge leaves a real signature and it is an order of magnitude too
small to be the explanation: the gap it would have to account for is
0.343, the `ndt`-to-`drift` attenuation difference.

**And the table contains a dissociation that was not being looked for.**
`alpha` is attenuated almost as much as `ndt`, 0.650 against 0.609, and
its variance component is biased by 3.5 percent where `ndt`'s is biased
by 22. A six-fold gap at nearly equal attenuation is not something a
per-learner information story survives, which is what finally moved this
investigation onto the target rather than the estimator.

A sharper operationalization of `margin` exists, how many trials land
within a window of the floor rather than the minimum alone, and it is
named here as an UNTESTED refinement rather than adopted after a null,
which would be fishing.

### Test 2's prediction, also written down before the measurement

Test 2 is NOT a test of the edge. That was a misreading of it, corrected
here. Raising the trials per learner does three things at once: it
concentrates each learner's conditional, so the Laplace approximation
improves; it raises the per-learner information, so shrinkage falls; and
it moves the observed floor.

**Any bias driven by APPROXIMATION or by INFORMATION shrinks with the
trial count. Only a bias that is STRUCTURAL, a property of the
parameterization rather than of how well it is being estimated,
survives it.** So this one experiment separates the whole remaining
candidate set from the alternative, and it does so whatever happens to
the edge.

The committed direction: **the relative bias in `sd(ndt)` falls as the
trial count rises**, and the reliability of the `ndt` deviations rises
toward the reliability of `drift`. If neither moves, the cause is
structural and both remaining candidates are in trouble.

The arms share their drawn deviations EXACTLY. `lh_rlddm_data()` draws
the block from `set.seed(seed + 2)` before it simulates anything, so the
same seed gives the same hundred learners at every trial count and the
comparison is within one population rather than across three. Only the
data those learners produce, and therefore their floors, differ.

Warning three of item 2.2 applies with full force here and is why each
arm is scored against its OWN realized target: a floor is an observed
minimum, so it moves with the trial count, and a figure from one count
is not comparable to a figure from another unless the target moved with
it.

### Which way I expect Test 2 to come out, written down first

Test 2 has three possible readings and they are not equivalent:

1. the ESTIMATE converges toward a stable target as trials rise, which
   would say the estimator was approximating badly;
2. the TARGET moves toward the estimate, which would say the target was
   the noisy quantity;
3. both move together and the gap holds, which would say the effect is
   structural.

**I expect 2, and for a specific reason that has not been raised yet.**
`ndt` is the ONE component whose target is ESTIMATED rather than known.
`alpha`, `drift` and `bs` each have a population truth on the scale the
model fits them on; the scale tier's own record says `ndt=no link truth`
for the fourth. Its only target is the realized one,
`sd(qlogis(ndt_i / floor_i))`, and `floor_i` is an OBSERVED MINIMUM.

An observed minimum is noisy, so that target carries variance the
learners do not have: two learners with the SAME true non-decision time
get different floors and therefore different induced deviations. A
maximum likelihood fit estimates the component that actually generates
the data, and floor noise is not a random effect, so the estimate
SHOULD come back below the naive realized target. On this reading the
22 percent is partly an artifact of the target rather than a fault in
the estimator, and it is the coincidence worth testing before anything
is concluded: the one component whose target is estimated is the one
component whose bias is six times the others.

That is testable WITHOUT Test 2, by decomposing the realized target, and
`dev/learnhier-target.R` does it. Test 2 then confirms or refutes the
trial-count half.

### Test 2's prediction REVERSED, with both versions kept

The original prediction above is superseded, not edited. Both stay on
the page with the reason each was made, because a pre-registration that
is rewritten once the mechanism is understood is worth nothing, while
one that is superseded in public is a record of how the understanding
moved.

**Version 1, made before the identity was found.** The bias SHRINKS as
the trial count rises. Reason: more data per learner concentrates the
conditional, so the Laplace approximation improves, and raises the
per-learner information, so shrinkage falls. That prediction belonged to
the approximation and information candidates.

**Version 2, made after the identity.** Both of those candidates are
gone, because the identity removed the thing they were invented to
explain. What is left predicts the OPPOSITE. The fitted deviation is
`log(ndt_i) - log(m_i)` and `m_i` is a MINIMUM over that learner's
trials, so raising the trial count can only push `m_i` down. The design's
own `sd(log ndt)` is fixed at 0.15 by construction and does not move. So
**more trials do not repair the parameterization**, and the committed
direction is that the nuisance share does NOT fall with the trial count.

**What is certain and what is not.** Certain: the LEVEL of `m` falls
with the trial count, because a minimum over more draws cannot rise. Not
certain: whether `sd(log m)` across learners grows, holds or shrinks.
For a Wiener first-passage time the density near zero has an essential
singularity, `exp(-a^2 / 2t)`, rather than a power-law tail, so the
usual extreme-value argument that fixes the spread of a log-minimum does
not apply cleanly and the direction is not obvious on paper. The claim
being tested is therefore the substantive one, that more trials do not
fix it, and not a sign on `sd(log m)`.

**If the nuisance share does fall with the trial count**, the identity
is still exact but the reasoning about `m` above is wrong, and this
document says so rather than keeping the story.

The target half of this needs NO FITS at all: `sd(log m)` is a property
of simulated data. `dev/learnhier-mvsn.R` measures it at 100, 200 and
400 trials without fitting anything, and Test 2's fits then say whether
the ESTIMATE tracks the target that the simulation shows.

#### Test 2's result: more trials do not repair it

12 seeds, paired on learners, no fits, `dev/learnhier-mvsn.R`.

| trials | mean log m | sd(log m) | sd(log ndt) | sd(eta) | nuisance share | cor(eta, bs) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 100 | -2.8476 | 0.4526 | 0.1517 | 0.4362 | 107.9% | -0.7680 |
| 200 | -2.9810 | 0.4361 | 0.1517 | 0.4226 | 106.7% | -0.7863 |
| 400 | -3.1018 | 0.4288 | 0.1517 | 0.4130 | 108.2% | -0.8062 |

Paired 400-minus-100 differences: `mean log m` **-0.2542**
(p = 4.6e-13), so the level of `m` falls as it must; `sd(log m)` -0.0238
(-0.0421, -0.0054); and the share itself **+0.0023, 95% CI (-0.0350,
+0.0395), p = 0.895**. Quadrupling the data moves the nuisance share by
two tenths of a percentage point against a resolution of about four
points. The mechanical `bs` coupling does not improve either; it
slightly worsens, -0.768 to -0.806, p = 0.004.

**Version 2's substantive claim is confirmed: more trials do not repair
the parameterization.** One detail did not go the way the reasoning
behind it suggested: `sd(log m)` SHRINKS about 5 percent over a fourfold
increase rather than growing. It does not matter to the conclusion,
because `sd(log ndt)` is fixed by the design and `sd(eta)` shrinks in
step, so the share is unmoved. Recorded because a prediction made on
reasoning that turned out to be loose, whose substantive half survived
anyway, is worth more on the page than a claim of full confirmation.

#### Test 2's fit arm at 100 trials, and a finding about the design

12 replicates at 100 learners by **100** trials, same seeds, same
learners. The estimate-side half of Test 2 is REPORTED WITH A HEALTH
WARNING because the design barely fits at that count: **convergence code
0 on 1 of 12 and a positive definite Hessian on 0 of 12**, with a median
maximum gradient of 8.7e-03 and a worst of 1.59, against 58 of 60 and 54
of 60 at 200 trials.

That is itself worth recording. A full four-parameter correlated block
on `rlddm()` is not reliably fittable at 100 trials per learner, which
is consistent with the variance-component collapse this package
documents at 20 to 60 trials and puts the usable floor between 100 and
200 rather than below 100.

What the arm does show, for what it is worth at that fit quality, is
that the bias does not improve: `sd(ndt)` comes back **25.0 percent**
below its realized target at 100 trials against 19.2 percent at 200, and
`cor(bs, ndt)` is -0.965 against a realized -0.768. So the estimate side
agrees with the target side: fewer trials do not help and, from the
no-fit sweep, neither do more.

#### The five-parameter block, which is the other reading of "every parameter"

2 replicates, the same data with `bias ~ (1 | p | id)` added, whose
truth is a variance of exactly zero. **Convergence code 0 on 1 of 2 and
a positive definite Hessian on 1 of 2.** A probe at this size settles
nothing about a rate, and it is reported as the reason the main arm
holds `bias` at 0.5 rather than as a measurement: adding a fifth
component whose true variance is zero puts the block on a boundary, and
one of two fits did not come back clean. The scale row's choice to hold

**Test 2's 400-trial FIT arm was dropped and priced at 12 replicates,

does not move, so there is nothing to converge on. The 100-trial fit arm
was kept for the estimate-side comparison at a quarter of the cost.

## The importance correction

`dev/learnhier-imp.R` per replicate, `dev/learnhier-imp-summarize.R`
over the directory. `bandit2arm_delta` with the correlated block, 100
learners by 200 trials, 100 draws per group, the same seeds the recovery
arm used so a replicate's correction and its Laplace row are the same
dataset.

Counted with `frmtmb:::imp_stalled()`, which is the statistic item 2.2
names, and not by eye. Three outcomes: `settled`, the iteration reached
its own fixed point inside the round cap; `stalled`, the cap bit and the
rounds are taking the same step every time, which is what a collapsed
variance component does to the fixed-point map; `slow`, the cap bit and
the steps are still shrinking. Only `settled` is a success.

**The observation: 24 of 24 settled**, in 3, 4 or 5 rounds, never
capped. **The bound: a failure rate below 11.7 percent at 95 percent
confidence**, which is the rule of three at this count and is compatible
with better than one failure in nine. A reader should not take "the
correction succeeds at this scale" from this; the observation is 24 of
24 and the bound is 11.7 percent. Extending to 40 replicates, which
would move the bound to about 7.5 percent, was priced at **65 minutes
and declined**; a later session can buy it.

Rounds used: 12 runs took 3, 11 took 4 and 1 took 5, against a cap of 5.
The one that took 5 still settled rather than hitting the cap.

**The row's own threshold warning is discharged, and decisively.** Item
2.2 notes that the threshold separating a stalled correction from a slow
one has margins of only 1.54 and 2.10, measured over 184 fits in
`dev/reviews/2026-09-08-debts.md`, so a rate near either edge would be a
finding about the threshold as much as about the model. This design is
nowhere near either edge. The round-to-round spreads run **2.49 to
3.68**, which is **249x** `imp_stall_tol` and **119x** the narrowest
still-moving spread that review measured. Minimum effective sample size
per draw runs 0.48 to 0.856 against a 0.25 floor, so the proposal is
covering its integrand as well.

**What the correction is worth here**, Laplace against corrected, means
over the 24 replicates:

| component | truth | Laplace | corrected | shift |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `alpha_(Intercept)` | -0.6190 | -0.6085 | -0.6093 | -0.0007 |
| `tau_(Intercept)` | 1.0986 | 1.1114 | 1.1120 | +0.0006 |
| `sd(alpha)` | 0.5 | 0.4622 | **0.4837** | **+0.0215** |
| `sd(tau)` | 0.3 | 0.2999 | 0.3027 | +0.0027 |

The fixed effects barely move, which is what the package's own Laplace

**It was not run on rlddm, and that is priced rather than guessed.** At

## What I decided not to do, and why

## Defects found and not fixed

**`imp_stalled()` cannot be reached by the code that needs it.** Item
2.2's own wording is "counted with 0.55.0's `imp_stalled()` rather than
by eye", and an extension cannot do that: the function is `@noRd` in
core and the plan's scope rule forbids an extension reaching core with
`:::`. Three ways out, none of them this lane's to choose: export it;
export the VERDICT instead, as a field on `fit$importance` computed
where the rule already lives; or leave it and accept that every
consumer re-implements `(max - min) / mean` against a constant it has
to copy. The third is what a reader of `fit$importance$moves` does
today, and it copies a threshold whose measured margins are 1.54 and
2.10.

**Three of a kind, and they are one failure with three surfaces.** This
round produced all three, in this lane and in others, and they belong
together because the fix for each is the same shape: make the thing
being reported be the thing being measured.

| surface | what happened | how it was caught | how it is closed |
|---|---|---|---|
| a count taken from LAUNCHES rather than results | a launcher tallied a replicate that ran 44 minutes and wrote nothing | the tally was files, so it read 0 | `dev/learnhier-summarize.R` prints distinct seeds and gaps against the seed grid |
| a guard whose condition had never been observed TRUE | the stall check grepped `ps -W` for a string that output never contains, so it fired at once | the arm was demonstrably alive | the guard now tests progress plus a process count, both values of which have been observed |
| a count taken from the PLAN rather than the files | "24 of 24 settled" written into the findings with 9 files on disk | re-reading the prose against the directory | `dev/learnhier-counts.R` generates every count into the document mechanically |

The third is the one worth dwelling on: the lane's verifier could not
catch it, because the verifier checks FILES and that was a SENTENCE. A
number typed by hand is a number with no provenance, and being more
careful next time does not close it.

**Filed for the next round: three lanes, three fit-health instruments,
three different misreadings.** `diagnose()`'s `unbounded_dpar` inverts
on a bounded link, already under Core seams. The eamhier lane found `sv`
collapsing to a log link's floor with `diagnose()` printing "No
convergence problems detected" verbatim. And this lane found the
positive-definite Hessian check firing on the five fits with the highest
log-likelihood surplus in their cohort, three of which the optimizer
itself declares converged at a maximum gradient below 2e-3. The common
shape is that **each instrument tests a proxy for health that a boundary
or a bounded link breaks, and none of them tests the likelihood value
against anything.** This lane's own oracle comparison, the objective at
the replicate's realized truths, cost a few seconds per fit and
separated the five cleanly; that is a cheap check core does not have.

**A lane-process defect worth carrying into `dev/lane-rules.md`, not a
package one.** `Rscript` reads a script INCREMENTALLY rather than
parsing it whole, so editing a script while replicates are executing it
splices the file under the running processes. This lane lost a
44-minute rlddm fit that died at the very end with
`unexpected symbol in "rec$total_s <- as.numeric(difftim comparisons"`,
which is the start of one line joined to the tail of another. The fit
had completed; only the write-out was lost. Freeze a runner before
launching it.

work uses `confint_varcorr()`, `VarCorr()`, `ranef()`, `fixef()`,
`diagnose()` and `single_response()`, all exported and all present at

**One caveat for whoever picks the number.** The documentation change is
not cosmetic: `?rlddm` now tells a user that a variance component they
may already have reported is about 94 percent a property of their data.
A reader of the old page could have published `sd(ndt)` as a
between-learner spread. That is a correction to what the package SAYS
rather than to what it does, and whether it deserves more than a patch
is a judgment about how loudly it should land, which is not this lane's
to make.
