======== H. hmm_starts_scale()'s 'unit' fallback, by construction ========
The branch is reached when confint() errors, returns the wrong
number of rows, disagrees with fit$opt$par, or reports no usable
standard error at all. Each is tried below.

-- H1: can the alignment test EVER fail? --
confint.frmtmb_fit() sets its `est` column to object$opt$par
(R/confint.R:383). The test compares that column with
fit[['opt']][['par']], so it is a vector against itself unless
confint()'s own length(est) != length(se) fallback fires, which
the core comments call defensive only.
identical(ci[,'est'], opt$par): TRUE 
nrow(ci) == length(opt$par): TRUE 

-- H2: a fit with NO usable standard error --
Over-stating K on data from a single state leaves the extra
state's parameters unidentified. If every standard error comes
back non-finite, `!any(ok)` fires and scale_how is 'unit'.
  K=2 on one-state data        npar  6  se finite&pos 6 of 6
  K=2 on one-state data        scale_how = se
  K=3 on one-state data        npar 12  se finite&pos 12 of 12
  K=3 on one-state data        scale_how = se
  K=3 on 8 rows                npar 12  se finite&pos 11 of 12
  K=3 on 8 rows                scale_how = se-partial
  collinear covariate          npar  8  se finite&pos 8 of 8
  collinear covariate          scale_how = se
  y constant within state      npar  6  se finite&pos 5 of 6
  y constant within state      scale_how = se-partial

-- H3: the fallback's own arithmetic, called directly --
A fit whose confint() cannot be produced at all reaches the
branch; the branch itself is exercised here on a stub, to show
what it returns rather than to claim a fit reaches it.
  confint() on a stub errors -> how = unit  s = 1 1 1
