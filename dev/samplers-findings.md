## Answers

### Q1. brms + stanli against frm() and against rstan

Designs: 9 survey pairs (p003 `|ID|` with a distributional sigma block,
p041 rescor + mm(), p060 von_mises `|ID|`, p062 s(), p075 cumulative,
p082 hsgp, p096 negbinomial, p121 `||` slopes, p152 weibull cens()) at
n = 200, and 3 large GLMMs: big_pois (poisson (1 | g), 2000 groups,
n = 10000), big_crossed (bernoulli (1 | a) + (1 | b), 300 x 40,
n = 6000), big_slope (bernoulli (1 + x | g), 500 groups, n = 4000).
Tables Q1a to Q1c.

- Time. frm() took 0.29 to 1.56 s on every design. stanli's setup
  (`stanli_model()`, which replaces the C++ compile) took 0.05 to 0.82 s;
  rstan's compile took 45 to 73 s. Sampling 4 x 1000/1000: stanli
  0.33 to 4.5 s on the survey pairs and 17.8 / 18.3 / 106 s on
  big_slope / big_crossed / big_pois; rstan 1.8 to 33 s and 115 / 97 /
  941 s. stanli sampled 2.3 to 14.2 times faster than rstan (median about
  7) at the same ESS (Q1b), so the gain is per gradient, not in mixing.
  Including the compile, stanli is 8 to 127 times faster end to end.
  stanli's gradient on big_slope's program (1005 parameters, 4000 rows)
  took 5e-05 s (`dev/samplers-slope-probe.log`).
- stanli against rstan. Every design: same divergence picture (p082 hsgp
  19 against 37, p062 1 against 2, all others 0 or 1), max R-hat 1.012 or
  less on both, min bulk ESS within a factor 1.35. Posterior means agree
  within Monte Carlo error: max |z| is below the 5% Bonferroni bound on
  every design (largest: big_pois 4.14 against 4.37 over 4006 variables;
  lp__ itself |z| 1.77 or less). No brms formula failed under stanli:
  the 12 designs here and the 4 of Q2 (importance weights, equidistant
  thresholds, binomial trials, gr(cov = A)) all compiled and sampled.
- frm() against the posterior (brms default priors). Population
  coefficients sit within 0.16 posterior sd of the posterior median on
  every design. Standard deviations of random effects sit 0.25 to 0.66 sd
  below the median: the ML estimate of a scale is biased low and its
  posterior is right-skewed, so this is the expected, legitimate
  difference. Random-effect levels: median |z| 0.03 to 0.12. Two
  hyperparameters differ by more, both for a known reason
  (`dev/samplers-q1-mlez.log`):
  - p060: the ML correlation of the `|ID|` block is at the boundary,
    +1.00, with sd(kappa) at 0.044; brms's lkj(1) prior and the sd prior
    give a posterior median of 0.18 (sd 0.55), z = 1.49.
  - p082 hsgp: the ML fit runs along the ridge lscale -> 0 (1.98e-05)
    with sdgp = 51.1; brms's inv_gamma prior on lscale holds the
    posterior at lscale 0.037 and sdgp 1.54, z = 69. The likelihood is
    flat along that ridge (the survey's flat-prior L1 for this pair is
    "equivalent"), so the priors decide the posterior. Not a frmtmb
    defect: frm() says so itself, with a warning that the length scale
    ran below the basis's resolution and that only sdgp^2 times the
    length scale is determined, and NaN standard errors for both
    (`dev/samplers-p082-warn.log`).
- brms mock backend. `brm(backend = "mock", mock_fit = ...)` returned in
  0.9 to 8.4 s on the survey pairs. On big_slope it did not return in 15
  minutes for stanli's stanfit, nor in 5.5 minutes with
  `as_stanfit(plain = TRUE)`, nor for rstan's own stanfit of 400 draws
  (`dev/samplers-slope-mock*.log`); on big_crossed dev/samplers-q1.R
  passed an hour inside the step after rstan's sampling (which takes
  97 s) and was stopped. So the stall is in brms 2.23.0's mock path (or
  its renaming) on large random-effect fits, for either sampler; it is
  not a stanli defect. The large designs were measured without it
  (`dev/samplers-q1raw.R`, Stan names mapped to brms names by hand).

### Q2. Can stanli stand in for rstan in the survey and the brms tiers?

How the tooling calls Stan: `brms_stan_model()` (helper-brms.R) compiles
with `rstan::stan_model()` into the content-addressed `dev/stan-cache`
(587 programs), then
1. L1 and the log-density tiers: `rstan::sampling(chains = 0)` for a
   stanfit, then `unconstrain_pars()`, `log_prob()` and
   `grad_log_prob()` with `adjust_transform = FALSE` (31 call sites in
   tests and the survey), and the Jacobian of the prior blocks as
   `log_prob(TRUE) - log_prob(FALSE)`;
2. L4 "posterior": `rstan::sampling()` 2 x 2000 (11 survey pairs), and
   `test-default-priors-fit.R` in frmtmb.sample;
3. L4 "optimizing": `rstan::optimizing()` without the Jacobian, the ML
   point of brms's program (5 survey pairs, `test-brms-agreement.R`);
4. the method tier: a Fixed_param brmsfit at frm()'s estimate
   (helper-brms-methods.R), `pars = exclude, include = FALSE`.

Measured on 4 survey pairs (p027, p075, p113, p124: thresholds,
importance weights, a gr(cov = A) slope block with a Cholesky
correlation; tables Q2a to Q2c):

- (1) stanli refuses `adjust_transform = FALSE` by design ("stanli's
  compiled graph includes the parameter-transform Jacobian"). The
  workaround works exactly: a second stanli program, brms's code with its
  model block emptied, has the Jacobian alone as its density, and
  full - Jacobian-only reproduces rstan's no-Jacobian log density to
  5.5e-16 relative and its gradient to 1.1e-14, at frm()'s estimate and
  at 5 points around it, on all 4 pairs (ordered, lower-bounded and
  Cholesky-correlation transforms included). stanli's `unconstrain()`
  returns rstan's unconstrained vector exactly. Setup is 0.07 to 0.11 s
  per program, against 0.66 to 1.11 s to read a cached rstan program
  plus 0.07 to 0.11 s for the chains = 0 stanfit.
- (2) L4 sampling, 2 x 2000: stanli 0.37 to 1.97 s, rstan 2.5 to 17 s
  (4.1 to 8.6 times faster); means agree (max |z| below the bound on
  all 4). `as_stanmodel(code)` with `rstan::sampling()` runs on stanli
  unchanged.
- (3) No equivalent: `rstan::optimizing()` on an `as_stanmodel()` is an
  error, and `optimize_model()` always includes the Jacobian. A swap
  would maximize full - Jacobian-only in R (`optim()` on the shim's
  value and gradient). Not measured.
- (4) Fixed_param works, with one stanli bug: `pars = exclude, include =
  FALSE` first errors on names the program lacks (rstan ignores them),
  and with the list cut to the program's names stanli 0.25.0 keeps ONLY
  lp__ (`dev/samplers-q2fp-debug.log`). Passing the complement with
  `include = TRUE` gives a brmsfit with the same variables and
  posterior_epred() and fixef() identical to rstan's (relative
  difference 0) on all 4 pairs. That is an upstream bug to report
  (`dev/upstream-bugs.md`, not filed by this lane).

What a swap would take: one adapter in `brms_stan_model()` (or beside
it) that returns a small object with `unconstrain`, `log_prob(adjust)`,
`grad_log_prob(adjust)`, `constrain` and `sampling` built on two stanli
programs; replacing the about 31 direct `rstan::log_prob/grad_log_prob(
adjust_transform = FALSE)` calls and the `rstan::sampling(chains = 0)`
calls by calls into it; an R-side optimizer for the 5 optimizing pairs
and `test-brms-agreement.R`; the include = TRUE workaround in
helper-brms-methods.R; and a guard that the brms code has the
`model {` ... `generated quantities {` layout the shim cuts. Two to
three days of work plus a full survey rerun to show no verdict moves.

Time saved: with the cache warm (today's state), the survey's 172 pairs
took 927 s in all (`dev/equiv-data/results.tsv`); the Stan steps of a
pair are 0.7 to 1.2 s of that (cache read 0.66 to 1.11 s, chains = 0
stanfit 0.07 to 0.11 s), and stanli's two programs take about 0.2 s, so
roughly 100 to 170 s per full run, plus 2 to 15 s on each of the 11 L4
posterior pairs (2.1 to 15 s saved on the 4 measured). The real saving is a
cold program: 45 to 73 s of compile per new brms program becomes
0.1 s. Every new survey pair, test case or brms upgrade (the cache key
holds the rstan version, so an rstan upgrade recompiles all 587) pays
that compile today.

### Q3. SparseNUTS against frm_sample() (tmbstan route)

How frm_sample() builds its objective (extensions/frmtmb.sample/R/
sample.R): `prior_augmented_obj()` or, with non-centered blocks,
`ncp_objective()` retapes `build_objective()` (the bare likelihood) plus
`neg_log_prior_fn()` of the resolved brms default priors, as a new
`RTMB::MakeADFun()` with `b` (and `miss`) random; that object goes to
`tmbstan::tmbstan()`. The lane captured that exact object with a tracer
on `tmbstan::tmbstan()` (frm_sample(reparameterize = FALSE), the
centered blocks SNUTS decorrelates through the joint precision) and
gave it to `sample_snuts()`. So yes: SparseNUTS runs on the same
prior-augmented objective, with no change to it.

Arms: frm_sample() as a user calls it (defaults: brms default priors,
non-centered `us` blocks, 4 x 1000/1000, mode initialization) against
`sample_snuts()` defaults (metric "auto", 150 warmup with Stan's metric
adaptation off, 1000 draws). ESS per second = min bulk ESS over the
variables both arms report (lp__ excluded) / wall time of the whole call
(SNUTS's includes its optimization and Q). Tables Q3a, Q3b.

| Design | frm_sample ESS/s | SNUTS ESS/s | SNUTS / tmbstan | SNUTS metric |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| small_pois, poisson (1 \| g), 30 groups | 120.5 | 1076 | 8.9 | dense |
| big_pois, poisson (1 \| g), 2000 groups | 1.78 | 12.4 | 7.0 | sparse |
| big_crossed, bernoulli crossed 300 x 40 | 1.01 | 7.45 | 7.4 | sparse |
| slope, gaussian (1 + x \| g), 40 groups | 75.5 | 177 | 2.3 | dense |
| small_pois, laplace = TRUE on both | 56.9 | 80.5 | 1.4 | diag |
| gp, exact gp(x), 60 positions | 0.29 | 0.14 | 0.5 | dense |
| weak, bernoulli (1 \| g), 2 rows per group | 14.3 | 0.33 | 0.02 | sparse |
| ald, asym_laplace (1 \| g) | 86.4 | fails | - | - |

- Where the Laplace approximation is good (well-informed random effects),
  SNUTS gives 2.3 to 8.9 times the effective draws per second, and on
  the large models 7 times: big_pois 225 s against 852 s with 2788
  against 1512 min ESS. Posterior means agree (max |z| below the
  Bonferroni bound on every design both ran; posterior sd ratio
  0.92 to 1.09 on the large designs).
- Where it is weak, SNUTS is worse than the non-centered tmbstan route:
  - weak (2 rows per group): 53 divergences, R-hat 1.13, min ESS 23,
    against 1 divergence, R-hat 1.011, ESS 648. The centered funnel is
    not a Gaussian SNUTS can whiten, and frm_sample()'s non-centering is
    the right tool there.
  - gp (exact field, 60 positions): neither arm is usable (tmbstan 293
    divergences and treedepth 10 in 368 s; SNUTS 1158 divergences and
    R-hat 1.55).
  - ald: `sample_snuts()` stops at once ("leading principal minor of
    order 20 is not positive"): the Laplace marginal has no maximum, the
    exact reason frm() refuses this model, so there is no Q to build a
    metric from. frm_sample() samples it (0 divergences, R-hat 1.01).
    SNUTS's metric = "stan" would run (plain NUTS, no Q); not measured.
- Names: SNUTS returns draws in `obj$env$last.par.best` order (made
  unique as `b[1]`...), the same coordinates tmbstan samples, so
  frm_sample()'s own map `frmtmb.sample:::sample_finish(a, fit,
  list(laplace = ...))` turns them into brms-named draws (b_, sd_, cor_,
  r_g[...]) with no change. The comparison above used exactly that. A
  non-centered, mo() softmax, GEV or autocor chart objective would need
  the same recipe frm_sample() passes; SNUTS on a non-centered objective
  was not measured.
- laplace = TRUE: `sample_snuts(laplace = TRUE)` samples the outer
  parameters on the Laplace marginal, the same density as
  `frm_sample(laplace = TRUE)` (tmbstan's laplace route). On small_pois
  the two agree (max |z| 0.79 over 4 variables, sd ratio 0.99 to 1.03),
  SNUTS 1.4 times faster per effective draw.
- Costs seen: SNUTS rebuilds the RTMB object without random effects from
  `obj$env$data` and `parList()`. That worked for frmtmb's closures but
  drops `gp_tape_scope()`'s tape settings (the atomic matmul and the
  compressed sparse Hessian), so a large exact gp() field would retape
  at the memory cost that function exists to avoid; not measured.
  Every R process in which StanEstimators sampled ended with "terminate
  called without an active exception" and exit status 127 after its
  work was written (the 7 Q3 runs that sampled and
  `dev/samplers-smoke.R`); the two ald runs, where `sample_snuts()`
  stopped before sampling, and the processes that ran only stanli and
  rstan exited 0. Upstream entries: `dev/upstream-bugs.md` brms-53,
  stanli-1, StanEstimators-1, SparseNUTS-1 (all "not reported").

### Q4. Recommendations

**stanli: optional backend for our tooling now; candidate default for
the brms tiers once its rstan-shaped surface settles.**
- Evidence: 2.3 to 14 times faster sampling than rstan at the same ESS,
  0.1 s instead of about 60 s per new program, posteriors that agree
  within MC error on 12 designs including 3 large GLMMs, no brms formula
  it failed on (16 tried), and an exact no-Jacobian density through the
  Jacobian-only twin program.
- Cost: the adapter and call-site changes of Q2 (2 to 3 days), an
  R-side optimizer for 5 survey pairs, the include = TRUE workaround, a
  survey rerun.
- Risks: not on CRAN (R-universe binaries only); 3 months old with 27
  releases (0.25.0 to 0.27.0 in three weeks), so the API and the
  runtime layout move; the runtime is a separate download pinned to the
  package version; no `adjust_transform = FALSE`, no Jacobian-free
  optimize; the include = FALSE bug above. Windows works (binary
  package, runtime DLL, the compiler as a subprocess). The CI would need
  the R-universe repository and a runtime download step. Keep rstan as
  the reference until a full survey run under stanli shows no verdict
  change.
- For users (brm(backend = "mock") with as_stanfit()): works on small
  and medium fits, but brms 2.23.0's mock path stalled on two large
  random-effect fits for either sampler, so it is not a route to
  recommend in frmtmb's docs today. Nothing to integrate into frmtmb
  itself: frmtmb does not call Stan for fitting.

**SparseNUTS: not now as a backend; worth an opt-in experiment in
frmtmb.sample later.**
- Evidence: 2.3 to 8.9 times the ESS per second of frm_sample() where the
  Laplace approximation is good, 7 times on the 2000-group and crossed
  GLMMs; it runs on frm_sample()'s prior-augmented objective unchanged and
  its draws map through `sample_finish()`. But it is 40 times worse on a
  weak-Laplace bernoulli, fails outright on asym_laplace (the model class
  where frm_sample() is the only route frmtmb offers), and no better on
  an exact gp().
- Cost of an opt-in `frm_sample(sampler = "snuts")`: pass the centered
  prior-augmented objective, map draws with the existing recipe, add the
  laplace route; refuse or fall back to tmbstan when Q is not positive
  definite. About 2 days, plus tests that cannot run on CRAN.
- Risks: SparseNUTS (1.0.2, last commit 2026-04-16) and StanEstimators
  (0.4.0) are not on CRAN, so frmtmb.sample could only Suggest them with
  an Additional_repositories line; the process abort at exit
  (status 127) would break R CMD check and testthat runs that load it;
  the RTMB rebuild loses frmtmb's gp tape settings; and its value
  depends on the Laplace approximation being good, which is exactly
  when users least need MCMC over frm(). Revisit if SparseNUTS reaches
  CRAN or Stan gains SNUTS (its own vignette expects tmbstan may then be
  the better long-term route).

## Defects found (none in frmtmb code changed by this lane)
- stanli 0.25.0: `rstan::sampling(as_stanmodel(code), pars = x,
  include = FALSE)` keeps only lp__; and it errors where rstan ignores
  exclusion names the program lacks. Upstream.
- brms 2.23.0: `brm(backend = "mock")` does not return in 5 to 60 minutes
  on large random-effect fits (500-group correlated slopes; 300 x 40
  crossed), for rstan's and stanli's stanfits alike. Upstream.
- StanEstimators 0.4.0 / SparseNUTS 1.0.2: the R process aborts at exit
  ("terminate called without an active exception", status 127). Upstream.
- SparseNUTS: no fallback when Q is not positive definite (ald); the
  error is a bare Matrix message. Upstream.
- None in frmtmb: every frm()/posterior difference above has a stated
  reason, and the one degenerate fit (p082) carries frm()'s own warning.
