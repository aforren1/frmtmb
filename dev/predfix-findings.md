## 2. A finite-but-absurd `predict()` draw: not detected, on measurement

Reproduced on the recorded construction (`dev/shapes-p2-measure.R`
part B, the row at `x_bad = 1577.94`, `eta = 709.5`): Estimate
1.270552678e+306, Est.Error `Inf`, and 0.62.0's non-finite warning
already fires there (199 of 400 cells). The Est.Error 64956 on a
no-new-level row that the brief quotes is in no record under `dev/`
or `dev/reviews/`, and I did not find a construction that gives it;
nothing below depends on it.

The candidate detector, calibrated rather than guessed
(`dev/predfix-absurd.R`, `-absurd2.R`, `-absurd3.R`, 20 seeds per
row, 1000 draws): `one1`, the share of a row's total absolute
deviation from the median carried by its single largest draw. When it
passes 1/2, the Estimate describes one draw.

| row | kind | one1 min to max | over 1/2 | Estimate ratio between two predict() seeds |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| all-zero poisson cell | flat | 0.553 to 1.000 | 20/20 | 1.0e-3 to 9.0e17 |
| x2 = x + N(0, 1e-3), off-direction row | flat | 1.000 | 20/20 | 1.6e11 to 1.3e19 |
| x2 = x + N(0, 1e-2), off-direction row | flat | 0.787 to 0.907 | 20/20 | 0.37 to 1.35 |
| poisson at 100 x the data range | extrapolation | 0.869 to 0.9998 | 20/20 | 0.04 to 0.20 |
| lognormal sigma 4.5 | genuine | 0.434 to 0.493 | 0/20 | 0.0098 to 0.013 |
| lognormal sigma 5.5 | genuine | 0.517 to 0.560 | **20/20** | |
| lognormal sigma 6.5 | genuine | 0.568 to 0.600 | **20/20** | |
| Gamma 0.2, negbinomial 0.05, student t(1.05) | genuine | 0.018 to 0.148 | 0/20 | |

Three findings, and together they are the decision:

- **The ranges overlap.** A well-identified lognormal with sigma 5.5
  scores 0.517 to 0.560, the flat all-zero cell 0.553 upward. Any cut
  either misses flat rows or fires on genuine ones.
- **The genuine rows are just as unstable.** A lognormal with sigma
  4.5 gives an Estimate that moves by a factor of 100 between two
  `predict()` seeds (ratio 0.0098). The mean of a draw law that heavy
  is unstable whether the law is honest or not, so instability cannot
  be the signal either.
- **The worst flat cases already warn.** The all-zero cell raised
  0.62.0's non-finite warning in 20 of 20; the near-collinear 1e-3
  row in 0 of 20, but its Est.Error is `Inf`-scale and its interval
  says so.

So nothing is added. What would help is a different summary, not a
detector: `robust = TRUE` already reports the median, which is stable
on every row above. Whether to say so in `?predict.frmtmb_fit` is a
documentation choice left open.

## 5. `vcov()` on a REML or profile fit with no random effects

Reproduced (`repro-base.txt`): poisson `cnt ~ x` under `REML = TRUE`
and under `profile = TRUE`: "length of 'dimnames' [1] not equal to
array extent"; gaussian answers. `summary()` ANSWERS on this
construction (it catches the error), so only `vcov()` dies here; the
brief's "and summary()" did not reproduce, though `summary()`'s
standard errors are now read, not caught.

Cause: with no outer parameter at all (no dispersion, no random
effect, every `beta` inner) TMB's `sdreport()` returns the inner
Hessian as the joint precision without dimnames, and every reader here
finds its blocks by row name. Gaussian has `sigma` outer and so gets
names. Fixed where the matrix is made, `autoscale_sdreport()`, through
`sdr_name_joint()`, which names it only when it is unnamed AND there is
no outer parameter, so every other fit's matrix is untouched (item 6's
battery: 0 other fits moved).

Families this reached, from the battery: poisson, bernoulli, binomial,
geometric, exponential and categorical, under both REML and profile, 36
fits. On all 36 `logLik`, `opt$par` and the code are bitwise unchanged
and only `vcov()` went from an error to numbers.

**CORRECTED in punch round 1.** This said the standard errors are
`glm()`'s. That holds for poisson (1.9e-11 relative) and bernoulli
(3.8e-8), and NOT for geometric or exponential, which differ from
`glm()`'s by 2.3e-2 under both REML and profile
(`dev/predfix-p1-glmse.R`, `p1-glmse.txt`, glm at epsilon 1e-12,
dispersion 1). frmtmb inverts the observed information and `glm()` the
expected information, and on a non-canonical log link the two differ.
The reviewer measured categorical against `nnet::multinom` at 5.4e-8.

Tests: `test-predfix.R`, both modes and `summary()`/`fitted()`, 5
assertions failing on base, and the gaussian case kept equal to `lm()`.

### M1. Random slopes

Reproduced on my construction (`dev/predfix-p1-slope.R`,
`p1-slope-before.txt`), gaussian `y ~ x + (1 + x | g)`, 20 groups x
15, 3 seeds: at sd(x) about 1e-3 the plain fit is 1.88 to 3.14 units
short; at 1e-6 autoscale = TRUE is STILL 5.46 to 6.35 short, code 0.

Cause, measured on theta: the slope's log sd stays at its start
(1.2e-8 at 1e-6, against 3.5 at scale 1), and the correlation
parameter runs to 134 to 1543. The log sd starts at 0, which is a
slope variance of `sd(x)^2` in the response's units, effectively zero
on a small column, where the gradient of a log sd vanishes. Autoscale
rescaled the X column and not the Z column, so its pre-fit started
from the same flat point. The reviewer's guess was right.

Fix, within autoscale: `autoscale_plan_z()` finds each random slope on
a planned column and the pre-fit divides that Z column by the same
`s`, which is an exact reparameterization (`b * s`, `log sd + log s`,
correlations unchanged under a diagonal change). Only where that holds
by construction: `us`, `diag`, `us_t`, `diag_t`, whose first `dim`
theta are per-component log sds, single-membership components, an
identity coefficient map, and a Z column checked equal to the X column
before it is trusted.

much larger spread than a fixed effect. Calibrated with

### B1. The default hid separation and errored where FALSE returned

Reproduced on the round-1 lane build from the reviewer's probes
(`dev/predfix-review2/r2-b1-sep2.txt`, `r2-b1-stage.txt`) and by my
tests (`test-predfix-p2-before.txt`): on a separated bernoulli fit the
default reported code 0, no warning and Est.Error 2.8e132. On
`yb ~ z + x + (1 + x | g)` at spread 0.03, the pre-fit raised "NA/NaN
gradient evaluation" and the default died with it.

What a pre-fit that should not seed looks like, measured on the
standardized model (`dev/predfix-p2-prefit.R`, `p2-prefit.txt`):

| pre-fit | code | coefficient-block Hessian |
|---|---|---|
| separated, design A, 10 seeds | 1 | eigenvalues about 1e-250, min at or below 0 |
| separated slope, design B, 9 of 10 seeds (1 errors) | 1 | condition 3.4e-7 or worse, or not positive definite |
| healthy, 15 fits | 0 | condition 0.041 or better |
| random slope with no slope variance, 5 fits and the battery's | 1 on 5 of 6 | condition 0.056 or better |

A first rule, "nonzero code or a non-positive-definite Hessian falls
back", was too strict. It dropped the battery's zero-variance random
slope (a variance on its boundary, a legitimate optimum) and lost 25.1
units at spread 1e-6. It also dropped 13 skew-normal fits, losing up
to 14.46 units (`p2-skewcmp.txt` before the final rule). What shipped:

- `autoscale_prefit()` returns the pre-fit's outcome instead of
  raising. `autoscale_prefit_converged()` accepts it on code 0 with a
  positive definite Hessian (condition above 1e-4), or failing that,
  when the coefficient block (`beta`, `betad`) alone is positive
  definite at that condition.
- `autoscale_prefit_verdict()` decides what to do. Under `NULL`: if the
  pre-fit errored, fit as FALSE does. If it did not converge, `"choose"`.
  Under `TRUE`: an error stands, with a class and "autoscale = FALSE" in
  its remedies. A pre-fit that did not converge still seeds the fit,
  and a warning says so.
- `autoscale_choose()` fits both ways and reports the plain (FALSE)
  fit, unless the seeded fit has a lower objective AND either warns at
  least as often or is a verified optimum. Verified means code 0 and
  an outer Hessian, in natural units, that is positive definite with a
  condition above 1e-8 (`autoscale_fit_sound()`). The reported fit's
  warnings are replayed. The plain fit's messages are shown once.
  `verbose = TRUE` says which fit was kept.
