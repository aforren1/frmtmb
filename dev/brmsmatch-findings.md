## 1. `rhat()` and `neff_ratio()`

`bayesplot::rhat(stanfit)` is `rstan::summary(object)$summary[, "Rhat"]`,
the classic split-R-hat, and `bayesplot::neff_ratio(stanfit)` is rstan's
`n_eff` over the total. brms computes neither.

- `brms:::rhat.brmsfit` is
  `posterior::summarise_draws(draws, rhat = posterior::rhat)`.
- `brms:::neff_ratio.brmsfit` is
  `matrixStats::rowMins(cbind(ess_bulk, ess_tail)) / ndraws(draws)`.

Both bodies are printed by `dev/brmsmatch-formals.R`. `rowMins` over two
columns is `pmin`, so the implementation here uses `pmin`.

Size of the old disagreement, from the block above:

- `rhat()`: 0.00563108 relative, against a whole signal of
  `max|rhat - 1| = 0.01003176`. **1.15 times the entire excess over 1
  that the diagnostic reports.**
- `neff_ratio()`: 0.43164951 relative, **76.7 times the rhat gap**.

**A recorded figure that needed reconciling, not correcting.** The
review recorded `neff_ratio`'s gap as 0.412677. That is the figure
against `ess_bulk / ndraws`, which is what `dev/sgrev-rhat2.R` computed;
brms takes the MINIMUM of bulk and tail, and against brms's actual
definition the gap is 0.43164951. The block reports both, so neither
reader is left re-deriving the other. The review's 0.412677 is
reproduced exactly on the row marked `vs bulk-only`. The review's
statement "73x larger" becomes 76.7x for the same reason. Neither figure
is wrong; they answer slightly different questions and the review's text
named brms's `min` while its script measured bulk.

### What is NOT changed, and why

`nuts_params()` and `log_posterior()` still delegate to bayesplot on the
`stanfit`. That is not an oversight: `brms:::nuts_params.brmsfit` is
`bayesplot::nuts_params(object$fit, pars = pars, ...)` and
`brms:::log_posterior.brmsfit` is
`bayesplot::log_posterior(object$fit, ...)`, both printed by
`dev/brmsmatch-bodies.R`, so matching bayesplot there IS matching
brms. `mcmc_plot()` builds from `as.array()`, which already carries
frmtmb's names. The blast radius was exactly two names and it stayed
that way.

### WITHDRAWN: the trade round 1 documented

Round 1 of this file said, under *The trade, stated*:

> Following brms moves two published diagnostics away from agreeing
> with `ds$stanfit`. `summary(ds)` still reports rstan's `Rhat` and
> `n_eff` columns, because they come from `rstan::summary()` by
> construction.

**That paragraph is withdrawn, and this is the retraction on the page
rather than in conversation.** It was honest disclosure of a real
disagreement, and it was wrong about the cause. "Because they come from
`rstan::summary()` by construction" named the construction the package
then had, not a constraint. `brms:::summary.brmsfit` contains

    c(measures) <- list(Rhat = posterior::rhat,
                        Bulk_ESS = posterior::ess_bulk,
                        Tail_ESS = posterior::ess_tail)

so in brms `summary(fit)[, "Rhat"]` and `rhat(fit)` are one number by
construction, and brms's effective-size headers are `Bulk_ESS` and
`Tail_ESS`, never `n_eff`. **brms followed all the way leaves no
self-disagreement at all**, so there was no trade to make. Found by the
round-1 review, `dev/reviews/20260915-brmsmatch.md`.

### What replaced it: `summary()` moved too

`summary.frmtmb_draws()` now reports the same three posterior
quantities under the same three names. Section 4 of the block above is
the measurement; on this lane's own draws, BEFORE:

- `max |summary Rhat - brms's rhat|` was 0.00625183;
- `x` and `sigma_Intercept` were **on opposite sides of 1** in the two
  places, which is the exact threshold a reader checks;
- the effective size was overstated by up to **72.8 percent** against
  brms's `min(ess_bulk, ess_tail)`;
- and `rhat(ds)` could address **0 of the 4 rows** by name, because the
  table used frmtmb's names and `rhat()` used Stan's.

AFTER, the `Rhat` column is `identical()` to brms's and to this
package's own `rhat()`, `pmin(Bulk_ESS, Tail_ESS)` is exactly
`neff_ratio(ds) * ndraws(ds)`, and no variable crosses 1 between the
two. The `n_eff` column is gone.

The sampler's own numbers are not hidden, only unlabelled as brms's:
`rstan::summary(ds$stanfit)$summary` has them. This is said in
`NEWS.md`, in `?draws-diagnostics` under *Which R-hat this is*, and in
`vignettes/posterior-diagnostics.Rmd`.

## 3. The ten positional signatures

Every one of the ten now matches brms's method name for name over every
position a caller can reach, measured by the review's own criterion
(truncate each side at its own `...`, compare as far as both go).
0 of 10 diverge, from 10 of 10. That criterion has a blind spot, which
is recorded below rather than left for the next reader to find.

### The two that answered silently

`as.mcmc(ds, TRUE)` returned an `mcmc` of length 22000 and
`posterior_interval(ds, 0.9)` a matrix of length 8. In brms that second
position is `pars` on both methods.

**brms's own answer to both calls is a refusal**, and this was measured
rather than assumed. `dev/brmsmatch-pars.R` calls
`brms:::extract_pars(TRUE, all_pars = ..., fixed = FALSE)` and
`brms:::extract_pars(0.9, ...)` directly; both stop with

```
Argument 'pars' must be NA or a character vector.
```

and `brms:::as.mcmc.brmsfit` and `brms:::posterior_interval.brmsfit`
both reach `extract_pars` through `pars` before doing anything else.
(That path was read off the bodies plus a direct call to
`extract_pars`, not off a fitted `brmsfit`: fitting one would compile a
Stan program, and the bodies show the call site unconditionally.)

So AGREEING with brms here means refusing with brms's own message, which
is what the methods now do, character for character. That is a stronger
statement than "they now error": they carry brms's validation, so
`as.mcmc(ds, "^x$")` and `posterior_interval(ds, "^x$")` are answered,
and only the values brms refuses are refused.

### CORRECTED: `pars` is two different arguments in brms

Round 1 gave `rhat()` and `neff_ratio()` the same `pars` the other
three methods got, routed through brms's `extract_pars()` rule, and
`?draws-diagnostics` said of all of them "brms refuses a `pars` that is
neither `NA` nor character, and so does this". **That was a divergence
this lane INTRODUCED, and the lane's own criterion cannot see it,
because the argument NAME matches at position 2.** Found by the round-1
review.

`brms:::rhat.brmsfit(x, pars = NULL, ...)` never reaches
`extract_pars`. It passes `variable = pars` to `as_draws_array()`, so
that slot is an EXACT-NAME selector defaulting to `NULL`:

| call | brms | round 1 here | now |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `rhat(x)` | all 11 | all 11 | all 11 |
| `rhat(x, "x")` | 1 variable | 1 variable | 1 variable |
| `rhat(x, "^x$")` | error | 1 variable | error |
| `rhat(x, NULL)` | all 11 | error | all 11 |
| `rhat(x, NA)` | error | all 11 | error |

The worst was `rhat(ds, NULL)`: `NULL` is brms's own default for that
slot, so a ported script that spelled the default out was refused.

The two refusals come out of `posterior::subset_draws()`, which is the
function brms's own path reaches, so the failure mode is brms's as well
as the answer.

The two methods now take brms's rule for THEM: `(x, pars = NULL,
regex = FALSE, ...)`, exact names, `regex = TRUE` for a pattern, and a
missing name is an error out of `posterior::subset_draws()`. The other
three keep the `extract_pars` rule, which is brms's rule for THEM.
`?draws-diagnostics` documents both under *Two different `pars` rules,
both brms's*, and no longer claims one rule for methods that follow the
other. `variable` and `fixed` were REMOVED from these two, because
brms's methods do not have them and cannot: `rhat.brmsfit()` already
passes `variable = pars`, so a caller's own `variable` would be a
duplicate-argument error. `regex` stays, because brms's `...` forwards
it into `as_draws_array()`'s own `regex` slot.

### What I refused to change, and why

- **`log_lik(newdata =)` and `re_formula =`, and `psis(newdata =)` with
  them.** `draws_row_loglik()` runs the fitted objective's own
  composition over the assembled frame, which is what carries the
  response, the addition terms (`cens()`, `trunc()`, `weights()`,
  `trials()`) and the case weights for the rows the model was fitted on.
  `newdata` supplies none of them, so supporting it is a feature
  (re-assembling a frame with a response on new rows), not a signature
  fix, and it is not what item 2.5f asked for. The slot is in brms's
  position and refuses by name, so a positional call asks the same
  question and gets an answer instead of a different question's answer.
  `re_formula` on `log_lik()` is refused for the second reason in the
  same message: the density is conditional on each draw's own `b`, which
  is what `re_formula` would remove.
- **`pp_mixture(newdata =)` and `re_formula =`.** `mixture_probs(fit)`
  takes a fit and nothing else (`R/families.R:3957`): a component
  probability is a statement about a ROW's own response, and `newdata`
  carries no response to classify.
- **`posterior_interval()`'s default variable set. This is a
  DIVERGENCE, not a default.** Round 1 called it "a default, not a
  positional slot", which is the right distinction for item 2.5f's
  scope and the wrong one for "matches brms down to output": a default
  IS output. brms returns every variable including `lp__` and the
  group-level modes; this one returns 4 of 11 on the measured fit, the
  same columns `summary()` and `print()` leave out, so a ported
  `posterior_interval(fit)["r_g[1,Intercept]", ]` gets a subscript
  error here. Deferred, not defended: the reason for the narrow default
  is real (a large fit has thousands of group-level rows, and brms's
  own `summary()` drops them too) but it is a divergence and belongs in
  a later item with the other six.
- **The six methods above, and the eleven silent ones.** `pairs()` is
  the clearest of the six: brms's is
  `pairs.brmsfit(x, pars, variable, regex, fixed, ...)` and this
  package's is `(x, variable, ...)`, exactly the divergence
  `mcmc_plot()` had. Not one of the ten, because the review audited the
  28 owned generic names and `pairs` is base graphics'. Its `variable`
  argument IS now validated, so a name that is not there gets the
  message that lists `variables()` rather than base R's "subscript out
  of bounds" from the array index; the SIGNATURE is still brms's job
  for a later item.
- **`re_formula` and `re.form` given together.** This package refuses
  that pair by design, with a test that says so on purpose
  (`test-draws-spellings.R`, "agreeing values are refused too"), while
  brms silently prefers `re.form`. A positional brms call that fills
  BOTH slots, such as `posterior_predict(ds, nd, NULL, NULL, exp)`, is
  therefore refused here. Found by this lane's own harness. The idiom
  that matters, `posterior_predict(ds, nd, NA)`, fills slot 3 alone and
  works. Changing it would undo a deliberate, tested decision from
  another item, so it is reported and not touched.

### `rhat(ds, variable = "x")` returns all 11 SILENTLY

`...` absorbs the name and `draws_diag_array()` takes no `...`, so an
argument these two do not have is accepted and ignored. brms errors.
This is not a regression: 0.5.0 shipped `rhat(x, ...)` and `variable =`
was never released on either method. What made it worse was the SHARED
Rd, where `@param ...` said "Passed to the bayesplot function", which

`log_posterior()` on that page and FALSE of these two.

**The `...` documentation is now split per method**, and says plainly
that `rhat()` and `neff_ratio()` read nothing from `...`, so an
argument they do not have is accepted and IGNORED where brms errors.

**No refusal was built here, deliberately.** Plan item 2.5e is in
flight and adds `frm_check_dots()` across the monorepo, which refuses

local and one of them the monorepo's, is worse than waiting.
**Whoever lands 2.5e should revisit this `@param ...` wording**, which
is the accurate statement only until the refusal exists.

### The division direction in the `neff_ratio()` test

`neff_ratio(ds) == pmin(Bulk_ESS, Tail_ESS) / ndraws(ds)` is
`identical()` TRUE, because both sides are the same division of the
same two numbers. The multiplication phrasing, "`pmin` equals
`neff_ratio` times `ndraws`", is NOT the same relation: it is a round
trip through `x/N*N` and is off by 0.51 ulp on one row of this fit,
5.684e-14. `NEWS.md` and the test both use the division and are exact.
**A comment beside the assertion now says why the direction is
load-bearing**, so it does not get "simplified" into the form that
needs a tolerance.
